# Digital Trading Infrastructure for the Chamber of Gold Buyers
### A Proposal for a Controlled B2B Gold Exchange Platform

---

## 1. Recommendation at a Glance

For a platform connecting **Tier 1 Buyers** (large refiners, bullion banks, international traders) with **Tier 2 Buyers** (licensed local exporters, aggregators, jewelers, manufacturers), an open marketplace model like Alibaba is the wrong fit. Gold trading is high-value and heavily regulated, and the platform needs to reflect that.

The recommended approach is a **controlled exchange**, not a marketplace — one that combines RFQs (Request for Quote), negotiated trading, and optional auctions, wrapped in strict identity, licensing, and compliance controls. Trading is one module in a broader ecosystem; the platform's real value is that it helps the Chamber manage its members, digitize processes, and create new recurring revenue streams — value that persists even when trading volume fluctuates.

---

## 2. How the Platform Should Work

**Every participant is licensed and verified before they can trade.** From there, transactions move through a structured workflow rather than a simple buy-it-now listing:

1. Sellers list available gold lots on the platform.
2. Tier 1 and Tier 2 buyers submit bids or RFQ responses.
3. Sellers compare offers.
4. Buyer and seller negotiate.
5. The Chamber approves the transaction.
6. Payment is placed into escrow.
7. Gold is delivered.
8. Ownership formally transfers.

For high-demand lots, an **auction module** (timed, reverse, or sealed-bid) can run alongside the RFQ system, with the highest qualified bidder winning.

### Core platform modules
- Identity & licensing verification, KYC/AML compliance
- Buyer and seller management
- Gold lot management (weight, purity, serial numbers)
- RFQ and auction engines
- Negotiation and counter-offers, smart matching
- Digital contracts and escrow management
- Payment integration and delivery tracking
- Vault management and assay integration
- Export workflow, compliance, and audit trails
- Analytics dashboards

### Where AI adds value
- Matching buyers with suitable lots
- Predicting bid prices from market conditions
- Detecting suspicious trading patterns, collusion, or fraud
- Forecasting regional demand
- Recommending optimal sale timing

---

## 4. Positioning: Technology Partner, Not Marketplace Owner

If your company is building this **for** the Chamber rather than operating it as your own marketplace, the commercial framing matters. The Chamber owns the member relationships; you own and operate the technology.

Avoid: *"We own a gold marketplace."*
Say instead: *"We provide the digital trading infrastructure for the Ghana Chamber of Gold Buyers."*

The commercial model — whether membership fees, transaction fees, RFQ fees, featured listings, or export service fees — is the Chamber's decision. Your company supplies the software that makes those options possible.

## 6. Two Commercial Models

### Option A: Licensing Model

The Chamber pays for the platform directly; you retain full control over pricing.

| Item | Estimated Range |
|---|---:|
| Platform development (one-time) | US$150,000–300,000 |
| Annual platform license | US$30,000–60,000 |
| Support & maintenance | 15–20% of development cost/year |
| Enhancements | Time-and-materials or fixed-price |
| Cloud hosting | Pass-through cost + management fee |

This model gives you predictable, upfront revenue but asks the Chamber to commit capital before the platform proves itself.

### Option B: SaaS with Revenue Sharing (Recommended for Adoption)

This is typically the easier model to get an association to say yes to, because **the Chamber makes no upfront investment**. Your company assumes the technology risk; both parties share in the platform's success as usage grows.

**The Chamber provides:** brand, credibility, member access, marketing, governance and trading rules, member verification.

**You provide:** development, hosting, security, support, feature enhancements, operations.

**Revenue comes from platform users, not from the Chamber:**

| Revenue Source | Charged To |
|---|---|
| Buyer transaction fee | Buyer |
| Seller transaction fee | Seller |
| Platform service fee | Both parties |
| Premium membership | Businesses |
| Featured supplier listings | Sellers |
| RFQ submission fee (optional) | Buyers |
| Digital contract fee | Transaction parties |
| Escrow fee | Transaction parties |
| Logistics coordination fee | Opt-in users |
| Trade finance / insurance referral commission | Financial institutions / insurers |

**Worked example:** A buyer purchases 10 kg of gold valued at US$1,000,000. At a 0.10% fee on each side, the platform earns US$1,000 from the buyer and US$1,000 from the seller — US$2,000 total. Under a 70/30 revenue split, that's **US$1,400 to the IT company and US$600 to the Chamber** per transaction.

**Optional subscription tiers** add recurring revenue independent of trading volume:

| Plan | Monthly Fee | Features |
|---|---:|---|
| Free | US$0 | Basic access |
| Professional | US$100 | More RFQs, analytics, priority support |
| Enterprise | US$500+ | API access, integrations, advanced reporting |

**Suggested responsibility and revenue split:**

| Responsibility | Chamber | IT Company |
|---|:---:|:---:|
| Member acquisition | ✓ | |
| Industry governance | ✓ | |
| Technology | | ✓ |
| Hosting | | ✓ |
| Support | | ✓ |
| Marketing | ✓ | ✓ |
| Revenue share | 30% | 70% |

The exact split should reflect who bears the costs and operational load.

## 7. Getting the Fee Structure Right

Because trade values are large, fee design needs care. A high percentage commission on multi-hundred-thousand-dollar trades can discourage adoption outright. Better alternatives:

- A small **fixed fee per transaction**
- A small **fee per kilogram traded**
- A **percentage capped at a maximum amount**

A hybrid structure tends to work best for a Chamber-led platform: free access for verified members, low transaction fees, optional premium subscriptions, and value-added services layered on top — minimizing barriers to joining while still building multiple, growing income streams.

Once the core platform is established, natural extensions include:

---

## Bottom Line

Build a **regulated exchange, not an open marketplace** — membership-gated, RFQ- and negotiation-driven, with escrow, logistics, and certification layered on top. Position your company as the Chamber's long-term technology partner rather than a market intermediary. And structure the commercials as a **revenue-sharing SaaS model**: it removes the Chamber's upfront cost barrier, aligns incentives around platform growth, and gives your company a recurring revenue stream that scales with adoption.


Here are 50 detailed triangulation examples — each shows how an analyst fuses a direct source with one or more indirect sources to produce an inference that neither source could support alone. Framed around mining/resource intelligence, consistent with the thread.

## Production & Operational Status

1. **Satellite imagery (stockpile size growth) + vessel AIS tracking (fewer bulk carrier calls) →** Inference: the mine is producing faster than it can ship, signaling a logistics bottleneck or port capacity constraint.
2. **Job postings (surge in "night shift operator" listings) + local news (community noise complaint filings) →** Inference: the site has moved to 24-hour operations, likely to hit a production deadline.
3. **Satellite thermal imagery (active flaring/heat signatures) + customs export records (zero shipments filed) →** Inference: processing is occurring but output is being stockpiled or smuggled through undeclared channels.
4. **Drone/aerial imagery (new haul roads) + permit filings (no corresponding expansion permit) →** Inference: possible unauthorized expansion or illegal artisanal encroachment nearby.
5. **LiDAR elevation change over time + mining claims registry (adjacent unclaimed land) →** Inference: extraction activity may be creeping across a tenement boundary.
6. **Employee review site complaints ("equipment breakdowns weekly") + stock filing (unexplained capex line item) →** Inference: aging equipment is driving unplanned capital expenditure, a leading indicator of margin compression.
7. **Weather data (unusual rainfall in region) + commodity price movement (spot price spike) →** Inference: flooding likely disrupted a major supplier's output, market has priced in the shortfall before official confirmation.
8. **Vessel tracking (ship idling offshore for days) + port authority records (no berth allocation) →** Inference: port congestion or a contractual/payment dispute is delaying unloading.

## Corporate Structure & Ownership

9. **Corporate registry (new shell company formed) + beneficial ownership registry (director overlaps with sanctioned individual) →** Inference: possible ownership layering to obscure a sanctioned party's stake in a mining asset.
10. **Stock exchange filing (private placement announced) + LinkedIn (executive team suddenly hiring geologists) →** Inference: capital raise is earmarked for exploration expansion, not debt repayment as stated.
11. **Trademark registry (new subsidiary brand filed) + WHOIS records (new domain registered same week) →** Inference: company is preparing to launch a new downstream processing or retail arm.
12. **Bankruptcy filing (junior miner) + mining claims registry (claims still active, not transferred) →** Inference: assets are in limbo, creating acquisition opportunity or environmental liability risk for the region.
13. **M&A database (rumored acquisition target) + patent database (target recently filed extraction-efficiency patent) →** Inference: the real motive for acquisition is proprietary technology, not just reserves.
14. **Lobbying disclosure database (increased spend on mining law) + legislative records (bill introduced weakening royalty rates) →** Inference: the lobbying spend directly correlates with the bill's introduction, suggesting influence.
15. **Political donation records (large donations to a state governor) + government contract database (state grants exploration rights soon after) →** Inference: possible pay-to-play dynamic in permit allocation.

## Environmental & Safety Risk

16. **Environmental impact assessment (understated tailings volume) + satellite imagery (tailings dam visibly larger than EIA figure) →** Inference: the company may be misreporting waste volumes to regulators.
17. **MSHA-type safety database (rising near-miss reports) + employee reviews ("management ignores safety concerns") →** Inference: an accident is statistically more likely in the near term; insurers/investors should flag risk.
18. **NGO watchdog report (alleges river contamination) + satellite imagery (discoloration plume downstream of tailings pond) →** Inference: visual corroboration substantially increases confidence the NGO's allegation is accurate.
19. **Court records (new lawsuit filed by downstream farmers) + weather data (recent heavy rainfall preceding filing) →** Inference: a tailings breach or overflow likely occurred during the rain event, triggering the suit.
20. **Pollution/emissions registry (self-reported dust levels flat) + local news (multiple resident respiratory complaints) →** Inference: self-reported data likely understates real emissions; independent verification needed.

## Labor & Community Dynamics

21. **Social media (geo-tagged protest photos at mine gate) + local news silence →** Inference: protest is being suppressed or under-covered locally; international outlets may pick it up next.
22. **Job board postings (mass "security personnel" hiring) + social media (community organizing posts) →** Inference: company anticipates unrest and is reinforcing site security preemptively.
23. **Genealogy/land records (indigenous ancestral claim overlapping tenement) + court records (pending injunction) →** Inference: legal risk to project timeline is higher than disclosed in investor materials.
24. **LinkedIn (spike in departures from HR/community-relations team) + NGO report (alleging labor rights violations) →** Inference: internal dissent or crisis may be driving the resignations, corroborating the NGO claim.
25. **Union forum posts (strike vote discussion) + vessel tracking (ships rerouted away from usual port) →** Inference: the company is preemptively rerouting logistics in anticipation of a labor stoppage.

27. **Import/export records (spike in imports of processing chemicals) + patent filing (new extraction method patented) →** Inference: company is scaling up a newly patented processing technique.
28. **Credit rating agency report (downgrade) + procurement database (canceled equipment order) →** Inference: cash flow problems are already forcing operational cutbacks, not just a rating agency's forward-looking concern.

## Technical/Cyber & Infrastructure

41. **WHOIS/DNS records (new domain mimicking a mining company's name) + breach database (recent credential leak from that company) →** Inference: a phishing infrastructure is being built using leaked credentials, likely targeting the company's supply chain partners.
42. **Shodan/Censys scan (exposed SCADA systems at a mine site) + local news (recent cyber-incident report in the sector) →** Inference: the exposed systems represent a live vulnerability consistent with a known regional threat pattern.
43. **GitHub repository (mining company's engineer commits code referencing a new ERP system) + job postings (hiring for "SAP migration lead") →** Inference: the company is mid-migration to new enterprise software, a period of typical cybersecurity vulnerability.
44. **Certificate transparency logs (new subdomain "investor-portal-test") + upcoming stock filing calendar →** Inference: company is preparing a new investor relations platform ahead of an earnings or capital-raise announcement.

## Cross-Domain Composite Inferences

45. **Satellite imagery (new access road into forest) + land registry (no logging/mining permit on file) + social media (local activist post about "trucks in the reserve") →** Triangulated inference: high-confidence unauthorized incursion into protected land, corroborated by three independent source types.
46. **Weather data (drought conditions) + commodity index (lithium price steady despite drought in key brine-extraction region) + trade data (unexpected import surge from an alternate producer) →** Inference: buyers are proactively diversifying supply in anticipation of a drought-driven shortfall not yet reflected in price.
47. **Court record (indigenous land rights case filed) + genealogy/land record (historic ancestral boundary predates concession) + company ESG report (claims "no land disputes") →** Inference: the company's ESG disclosure is materially misleading; reputational/legal risk is understated to investors.
48. **Employee review site (complaints about tailings dam maintenance) + environmental agency inspection record (delayed/overdue inspection) + satellite imagery (visible cracking on dam wall) →** Triangulated inference: elevated near-term dam-failure risk that no single source would have flagged with confidence alone.
49. **LinkedIn (mass hiring of "decommissioning engineers") + stock filing (asset impairment charge) + local news (town hall meeting about "mine closure plan") →** Inference: the company is winding down operations at this site well ahead of any public closure announcement.
50. **Patent filing (new autonomous haul-truck patent) + job postings (reduction in "truck driver" listings) + union forum (discussion of "automation fears") →** Inference: the company is quietly automating its haulage fleet, with workforce reduction likely in the next 12–18 months, despite no public statement to that effect.

---

**The common pattern across all 50:** no single source is dispositive on its own — a satellite image shows *what*, a filing shows *what was disclosed*, a forum post shows *sentiment*, a registry shows *what's legally on record*. The inference only becomes "actionable intelligence" when the analyst notices the *gap or convergence* between what different source types independently reveal — corroboration raises confidence, contradiction flags deception or risk, and timing correlations reveal causality that no source states outright.

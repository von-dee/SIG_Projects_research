### Point 1: Data Assembly (The Legacy Data Problem)
**The Problem:** Tarkwa has over a century of exploration history. You have decades of soil geochem, various vintages of airborne magnetics, historical drill logs, and assay PDFs locked away in different systems. When you want to evaluate a new brownfields target or a deep extension, getting all that fragmented data onto a single grid requires weeks of senior GIS work. While your team is wrestling with data formats, the drill rig is burning cash.

**The Point (Automatic Assembly):** 
With TerraPulse, you simply upload your survey data—XYZ, GRD, CSVs, and even those old assay PDFs. You draw your licence boundary, and the platform automatically fetches the rest: Sentinel-2 alteration indices, Copernicus terrain, NOAA regional magnetics, and lithology from Macrostrat and OneGeology. It parses, kriges to a 50m grid, reduces to pole, and puts everything on one comparable map. 

**The Impact:** What previously took weeks of GIS assembly is now a boundary polygon and a short wait. Your senior geologists stop acting as data janitors and get back to interpreting the structural controls of the Banket Series on day one.

### Point 2: Target Ranking and Reasoning (Institutional Knowledge)
**The Problem:** Here is a hard truth at mature camps: a target is often just a judgment made by an experienced geologist looking at several screens, relying on years of local intuition. When that geologist moves on, or the board asks *why* you chose to spend $2M on a deep target, the reasoning is gone. Only the conclusion remains. And let’s be honest—a heat map is subjective. You can interpret a color, but you can’t debate it with corporate finance.

**The Point (Ranking That Explains Itself):**
TerraPulse uses an expert-weighted orogenic-gold deposit model over a unified evidence grid. We don’t just give you a heat map; we give you a ranked list of distinct drill targets. Every single target carries per-target evidence attribution. It tells you exactly which layers pushed the score up—whether it was a magnetic low, an iron-oxide anomaly, or a structural intersection—and by how much. It even attaches a depth-to-source estimate.

**The Impact:** You capture the "Tarkwa eye"—that local intuition—and institutionalize it. You can agree or disagree with a specific number rather than arguing with a color. The reasoning survives personnel changes and board reviews.

*Interactive Check-in:* "When key project geologists transfer out of Tarkwa, how much targeting knowledge walks out the door with them? What would it mean to your portfolio if that reasoning was permanently captured and quantified in the database?"

### Point 3: Defensibility and Compliance (The Gold Fields Standard)
**The Problem:** When new deep assays come in from the lab, the integration usually starts all over again from scratch. The last analysis was a one-off deliverable. Worse, when it’s time to report under NI 43-101 or JORC conventions—which Gold Fields takes very seriously—proving your "working" means digging through old emails and undocumented processing steps. That creates audit risk and delays capital allocation.

**The Point (Defensible Output & Full Lineage):**
TerraPulse is built so the work can be re-run instantly. New assays land? You press a button. Every layer records its source file, checksum, processing steps, and algorithm parameters. Every report cites what it drew on and is versioned. You can attach Qualified-Person (QP) notes and review statuses directly to targets, which follow them into exports (PDF, CSV, KML, Shapefile).

**The Impact:** The document and the screen can never disagree. You are permanently built for the moment someone asks you to show your working. You defend your deep drilling budgets with a verifiable paper trail, eliminating audit risk and accelerating reporting.

*Interactive Check-in:* "How much of your quarter is spent reconstructing 'why we drilled here' for resource updates and technical reports, rather than finding the next deep target?"

### Point 4: The Exploration Copilot (Querying the History)
**The Problem:** AI is entering every software space, but in geology, an AI that hallucinates a gold grade or invents a deposit name isn’t just unhelpful—it’s a massive liability for a major like Gold Fields. You need speed, but you cannot afford a hallucination.

**The Point (Grounded AI Copilot):**
Our Copilot can only read your project data through named tools, and it *must* cite what it used. It can answer questions like, "Why did Target 3 rank so high?" or render charts directly from live database values. Crucially, deposit names in any AI summary are checked against a known-deposits register (like USGS MRDS) before the text is shown. If it doesn’t match, it’s stripped out. 

**The Impact:** You get the speed of an AI assistant and the safety of a locked-down database. You can interrogate decades of Tarkwa data in plain English and get cited, verified answers instantly.

*Interactive Check-in:* "If you could query your entire historical data set in plain English and be 100% guaranteed it won't invent a grade, how would that change your daily workflow?"

### Point 5: Field Operations (The Ghanaian Reality)
**The Problem:** Exploration happens where connectivity doesn’t. Whether your teams are mapping in the bush or working out of a remote camp, if your targeting software requires a constant connection to the cloud, your field geologists are flying blind once they leave the main office. A dead signal shouldn't mean a dead project.

**The Point (Built for Offline):**
TerraPulse is built to degrade, not stop. The current map view and surrounding zoom levels are cached on project load. Targets and their evidence stay readable offline. If a QP writes a note in the field, it queues on the device and syncs the moment a connection is restored. Actions that need a network are visibly disabled and explained, rather than failing silently.

**The Impact:** Your field teams have the targets, the evidence, and the mapping tools in their hands, regardless of where they are in the Western Region. No connectivity, no problem.

*Interactive Check-in:* "When your field teams are out of cell range, what’s the friction cost of them not having the latest targeting data at their fingertips?"

### Point 6: Honest Limitations (The Challenger Approach)
**The Problem:** Software vendors often oversell capabilities, asking you to buy into features that don’t actually exist yet. That destroys trust. 

**The Point (What It Doesn’t Do Yet):**
We believe in engineering judgement over overselling. So, here is exactly what TerraPulse *does not do* right now:
1. **Gold Only:** The ranking model is tuned for orogenic gold. If you were exploring for copper or lithium, you would get all the data, maps, and layers, but *no ranked targets*. Returning numbers from the wrong deposit model would be worse than returning none. 
2. **Boundary Required:** Imagery, terrain, and analysis all require a license polygon. The system will refuse to guess an extent.
3. **Minor UI gaps:** Social sign-in and self-service branding are visible but not yet functional.

**The Impact:** You know exactly what you are evaluating. But here is the strategic upside: given that Tarkwa is an orogenic/paleoplacer gold system sitting in the Birimian, our current deposit model is perfectly suited for your immediate asset base.

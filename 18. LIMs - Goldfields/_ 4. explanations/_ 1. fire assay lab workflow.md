This looks like a **fire assay lab workflow** for gold/precious metal analysis, showing how many individual field samples get progressively bulked together at each stage until they converge into a single Certificate of Analysis. Here's what happens at each step:

**01 – Field (individual)**
Each sample is collected on-site as its own discrete unit — one bag/ticket per sample point (drill interval, trench, etc.).

**02 – Intake / Consignment**
Samples arrive at the lab and are logged in as a batch ("consignment") under a job number, checked against the submission sheet, and given internal lab IDs.

**03 – Drying (20–40 per oven)**
Wet samples are dried in an oven, typically 20–40 samples per oven load, to remove moisture before crushing (moisture would otherwise throw off weight-based splits).

**04 – Crush (20–50 per session)**
Dried samples are crushed to reduce particle size (often to ~2–10 mm or finer), run in batches of 20–50 through the crusher in one session.

**05 – Pulverise (20–50 per session)**
Crushed material is pulverised to a fine powder (often -75 or -106 micron) so the sample is homogeneous enough for a small charge to represent the whole. Also run 20–50 at a time.

**06 – Charge (50 per assay batch)**
A weighed sub-sample ("charge") — typically 30–50 g — is taken from the pulverised powder and mixed with flux. Batches of 50 are prepped together, usually including QC standards/blanks.

**07 – Fusion (24–50 per tray)**
Charges go into crucibles and are fused in a furnace (~1000–1100°C) with flux and lead oxide, producing a lead button that collects the precious metals. Trays typically hold 24–50 crucibles.

**08 – Cupellation (24–50 per muffle)**
The lead buttons are placed in cupels and heated in a muffle furnace; the lead oxidizes and is absorbed into the cupel, leaving a tiny precious-metal (dore) bead behind. Same batch size range per muffle load.

**09 – Finish (50 per sequence)**
The dore beads are dissolved/digested and measured — commonly by gravimetric weighing, AAS, or ICP — run as an instrument sequence of ~50 samples.

**10 – QA (50 / review)**
Results for that batch of 50 are reviewed against inserted standards, blanks, and duplicates to check for drift, contamination, or failures before release.

**11 – Report (50 / CoA)**
Once QA passes, results are compiled into a Certificate of Analysis (CoA) — the final signed-off report delivered to the client, covering that batch of 50 samples.

**Why the numbers shrink/merge:** the pipeline is a funnel — many individual field samples (01) get grouped into consignments (02), processed in physical batches that grow in size through drying/crushing/pulverising (20–50), then get formally batched for the analytical chain (charge → fusion → cupellation → finish, all at 50), and finally converge into one reviewed, reported output (QA → CoA) per batch of 50.

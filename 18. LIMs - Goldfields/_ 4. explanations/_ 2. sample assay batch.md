
This is the **quality-control makeup of a 50-sample assay batch** — showing that not every slot in the batch is a client sample. Out of the 50 positions run together, only 40 are actual unknowns; the other 10 are QC "spikes" inserted to prove the batch is trustworthy. Here's what each type does:

**Client sample — ×40 unknowns**
The actual samples submitted for analysis — the ones whose results the client is paying for. These make up 80% of the batch (40 of 50).

**Standard — ×4 CRM**
Certified Reference Materials: samples with a known, certified precious-metal content (traceable to an accredited body). Inserted at intervals through the batch, their measured result is compared to the certified value to confirm the whole analytical process (fusion, cupellation, finish) is accurate — not just precise. If a CRM comes back outside tolerance, the batch is suspect.

**Blank — ×2**
Material known to contain none of the analyte (e.g. pure silica or barren flux), run through the full process. It checks for **contamination** — carryover from crucibles, cupels, reagents, or cross-contamination between samples. A blank returning a detectable result signals contamination somewhere in the chain.

**Duplicate — ×2 repeat**
A second split taken from the *same* pulverised sample and run again independently. This checks **precision/repeatability** — whether the process gives consistent results on identical material. Used to calculate relative differences and flag sampling or process variability.

**Check — ×2 umpire**
A split sent to a **different (often independent/umpire) lab** or re-run via a different method as a cross-check. This tests for **systematic bias** between labs or techniques — important for resolving disputes or validating results for reporting/compliance (e.g. JORC/NI 43-101 standards in mining).

**Why this matters:**
Together these QC inserts (4+2+2+2 = 10 out of 50) let the lab statistically demonstrate three things every accredited result needs:
- **Accuracy** — via CRMs (are we getting the right answer?)
- **Contamination control** — via blanks (are we getting a false positive?)
- **Precision** — via duplicates (can we repeat ourselves?)
- **Inter-lab bias** — via check/umpire samples (do independent methods agree?)

This is standard practice for any accredited assay lab (ISO 17025-style QA/QC), especially in mineral exploration reporting where results have to withstand audit.

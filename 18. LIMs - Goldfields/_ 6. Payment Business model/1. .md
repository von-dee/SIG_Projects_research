
# Payment structures in mining software: comparison and analysis

Vendors rarely publish prices, and terms change often, so treat the specifics below as general industry patterns to verify in negotiation.

## 1. The six models

**A. Perpetual license + annual maintenance.** You pay a large upfront fee to own the right to use a specific version indefinitely. An annual fee (typically 15-22% of the license price) covers support and upgrades.

**B. Subscription (per user / per module).** You pay an annual or monthly fee per named user, concurrent seat, or module, and access stops if you stop paying. Deswik and Seequent Leapfrog work this way.

**C. Cloud/SaaS subscription.** This is like B, but the vendor hosts the software, so there is no on-premise installation. Updates are continuous, and the price often includes infrastructure. Examples are SAP RISE, Maximo SaaS, and cloud tiers of Leapfrog and Datamine.

**D. Metric-based (usage-driven) pricing.** The price scales with a measured quantity: data tags for historians like PI, asset counts for Maximo, tonnes, or number of machines. It is often layered on top of A, B, or C.

**E. Project-based hybrid (hardware + implementation + recurring).** You pay upfront for hardware, installation, customization, and training, then a recurring license and service fee. This is typical of fleet management (DISPATCH, Wenco) and integrated operational systems.

**F. Enterprise license agreement (ELA).** A multi-year, multi-site negotiated contract, usually a fixed or capped annual fee covering a defined product set. Large miners typically use it as a wrapper around the models above.

## 3. Benefits and risks of each

### A. Perpetual + maintenance

**Benefits**

- Lowest total cost over a long horizon, since maintenance is only a fraction of the license fee.
- You can keep running a frozen version if you stop paying, which is valuable for validated, audited resource models.
- Predictable costs, and works without internet.
- The asset can be capitalized, which suits some balance-sheet strategies.

**Risks**

- Large upfront outlay in a cyclical industry.
- You pay for licenses you may not use during care-and-maintenance periods.
- Lapsed maintenance can mean expensive reinstatement or upgrade fees.
- Technology can be left behind, and vendors are actively steering customers away from this model.

### B. On-premise subscription

**Benefits**

- Low entry cost, so a small team or a new project can start immediately.
- Scale seats up or down with project phases (exploration, feasibility, production).
- Always on the current version, and lower obsolescence risk.

**Risks**

- Cumulative cost becomes the highest over a long mine life.
- Access is cut off if payment stops, which is a risk for historical data and models.
- Renewal price increases are hard to resist once you're embedded.

### C. Cloud/SaaS

**Benefits**

- Minimal IT overhead: no servers, patching, or version management.
- Real-time collaboration between site, head office, and consultants.
- Fast deployment across multiple sites, and elastic compute for heavy modeling.

**Risks**

- Remote mine sites often have unreliable connectivity, which can make cloud tools impractical for operations.
- Data sovereignty and regulatory constraints in some jurisdictions.
- Deepest lock-in, since data export and exit can be difficult.
- Cybersecurity concerns where systems touch operational technology.
- Under IFRS, some SaaS implementation and customization costs must be expensed rather than capitalized, which can hurt reported earnings. Check with your accountants.

### D. Metric-based pricing

**Benefits**

- Cost follows actual use, so you don't pay for capacity you don't need.
- Fair for operations that ramp up or wind down.

**Risks**

- Budget surprises when operations expand or when sensor and data volumes grow.
- Metrics can be defined in the vendor's favor, so scrutinize the definitions (what counts as a "tag" or "asset").
- It discourages using the software more, which is the opposite of what you want.

### E. Project-based hybrid

**Benefits**

- Bundled hardware, software, and integration from one accountable vendor.
- Deep customization to the site's fleet, layout, and processes.
- The vendor is contractually on the hook for uptime and performance.

**Risks**

- High switching costs, because hardware and software are tied together.
- Implementation costs can equal or exceed the software fees, and overruns are common.
- Vendor-locked ecosystems (for example, a fleet management system that works best with the same OEM's machines).

### F. Enterprise agreement

**Benefits**

- Volume discounts and price caps on renewals.
- One negotiation instead of many, and simpler compliance and audit.
- Flexibility to swap between products in the suite, and dedicated vendor support.

**Risks**

- Overcommitment: you can end up paying for shelfware.
- Long terms (3-5 years) are risky if a mine closes or is sold, so negotiate assignment and downsizing clauses.
- Needs strong internal license management to capture the value.

## 4. Illustrative break-even example

These numbers are assumptions to show the logic, not real quotes.

- **Perpetual:** $100k license + 20% maintenance ($20k/yr)
- **Subscription:** $35k/yr

| Year | Perpetual (cumulative) | Subscription (cumulative) |
| ---- | ---------------------- | ------------------------- |
| 1    | $120k                  | $35k                      |
| 3    | $160k                  | $105k                     |
| 5    | $200k                  | $175k                     |
| 7    | $240k                  | $245k                     |
| 10   | $300k                  | $350k                     |

The break-even is about year 7. If the tool is needed for 3-4 years (a short project), subscription wins clearly. For a 15+ year mine, perpetual wins by a wide margin, provided the vendor still offers it and you stay on maintenance. The analysis changes further when you add the IT and hosting costs of on-premise (which favor subscription or SaaS) and discount future cash flows (which favors deferring cost).

## 5. Mining-specific factors that shift the decision

1. **Mine life and lifecycle stage.** Exploration and feasibility teams favor subscription flexibility, while long-life producing assets favor perpetual or enterprise agreements.
2. **Commodity cycles.** Low OpEx commitments are safer when prices crash, and subscriptions can be cut. Sunk CapEx cannot.
3. **Connectivity.** Remote sites push operational systems (fleet management, historians) toward on-premise or hybrid, and planning tools toward cloud or subscription at head office.
4. **Regulatory and audit needs.** Reserve and resource reporting (JORC, NI 43-101, SAMREC) requires reproducible models, and the ability to reopen old models on frozen software is a real advantage of perpetual licensing.
5. **Operational criticality.** For systems where downtime stops production (dispatch, control), the vendor's service commitments matter more than the license price.
6. **Ecosystem lock-in.** Pairing OEM equipment with the same OEM's software may be commercially attractive but reduces future negotiating leverage.
7. **Accounting.** CapEx versus OpEx treatment affects EBITDA, covenants, and tax, so involve finance early.

## 7. Practical recommendations

- **Match the model to the time horizon.** Under about 5 years, subscription usually wins. Over about 8 years, perpetual or a fixed-price enterprise agreement usually wins.
- **Use a hybrid portfolio.** Many large miners use perpetual or enterprise agreements for core geology and planning, subscription for specialist and collaborative tools, and hybrid contracts for operational systems.
- **Negotiate the exit, not just the entry.** Secure data export rights, access to historical files, price-increase caps (for example, CPI or 3-5% maximum), and rights to reduce seats or transfer licenses on mine sale or closure.
- **Model total cost of ownership, not license price.** Include implementation, integration, training, IT infrastructure, and internal admin time.
- **Ask for a multi-year quote with escalators.** Year-one pricing is often a teaser.
- **Test the cloud against your worst connectivity day** before committing operational workloads to it.

**Patterns across the industry**

- **Subscription** is the direction of travel, especially for planning and modeling tools, because vendors prefer recurring revenue and miners increasingly want cloud access.
- **Perpetual plus maintenance** is still offered by some vendors, with maintenance typically around 15 to 22% of the license fee per year.
- **Hybrid or project-based** pricing is typical for operational systems like fleet management. It includes hardware, installation, customization, training and multi-year service contracts.
- **Enterprise agreements** are common for large miners. They negotiate multi-year, multi-site deals with volume discounts rather than paying list price.
- Implementation, customization, integration and training are usually charged on top, and they can rival the software cost itself.

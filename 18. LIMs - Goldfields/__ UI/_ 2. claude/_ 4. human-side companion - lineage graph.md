This is the human-side companion to the lineage graph — same eleven stages, but told through who does the work and what they sign off on:

- **Field geologist** cuts the interval and starts chain of custody.
- **Receiving clerk** logs it in and assigns the lab ID.
- **Prep technician** owns three stages — drying, crush/split, pulverising — each ending in its own log or QC form.
- **Assay technician** owns fusion through finish, the part with the tightest instrument logging (tray position, furnace batch sheet, cupellation log).
- **Senior chemist / QA** is the gate before anything leaves the lab — checks standards, blanks, duplicates, and any mass-balance flags the earlier graph would have raised.
- **LIMS administrator** turns the approved result into the client-facing Certificate of Analysis.

Click any role chip and the timeline dims everything else, so you can trace just that person's touchpoints end to end — useful for spotting where a single role is a chain-of-custody bottleneck (prep technician touches three consecutive stages here) versus where handoffs between people are the real risk (receiving → prep, assay → QA).

Worth noting: every "form" on this page is exactly where the `operator`, `timestamp`, and `instrument` fields on the lineage graph's edges actually come from — this page and the graph page are describing the same events from two different angles.

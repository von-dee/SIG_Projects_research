Here is the end-to-end operational user flow, mapping the physical lab stages to the specific digital actions, forms, and graph operations performed by the users. 

This flow tracks a sample from the field to the final result, explicitly showing how the UI enforces the graph creation and mass balance at every step.

---

### Stage 1: Field Intake & Reception
**User:** Geologist / Sample Reception Clerk
**Physical Action:** Drill core is collected, bagged, and transported to the lab. 
**Digital Activity:** Creating the "Origin Node" of the graph.

*   **Form Filled:** "Sample Registration"
    *   `Hole ID` (e.g., DDH-01)
    *   `From` / `To` meters (e.g., 10.0m - 15.0m)
    *   `Gross Mass` (e.g., 3.000 kg - weighed on a digital scale)
    *   `Material Type` (Core / Chip)
    *   `Sampler Name`
*   **System Action (The Graph):**
    *   Creates a **`Field Sample` Node** with status `Active`.
    *   Creates a **`Context Node`** (Hole Interval) linked to the Sample Node.

---

### Stage 2: Drying
**User:** Preparation Lab Technician
**Physical Action:** Samples are placed in drying ovens to remove moisture.
**Digital Activity:** Logging the first transformation edge.

*   **Form Filled:** "Drying Log" (Accessed via scanning the sample barcode)
    *   `Parent Sample ID` (Auto-filled via barcode scan)
    *   `Dry Mass` (e.g., 2.850 kg - entered manually or via scale integration)
    *   `Drying Oven ID` / `Temperature`
*   **System Action (The Graph):**
    *   Creates an **Edge** (`Operation: Dried`).
    *   Creates a child **`Dried Sample` Node** (Mass: 2.850 kg).
    *   *UI Check:* System calculates `Delta = Gross Mass - Dry Mass` (150g loss). If > 10%, UI flashes red and demands a `Reason Code` before allowing the user to click "Commit".
    *   Parent Node status changes to `Consumed`.

---

### Stage 3: Crushing & Splitting
**User:** Preparation Lab Technician
**Physical Action:** Jaw crusher reduces core to gravel; sample is riffle-split. Coarse reject is bagged and stored.
**Digital Activity:** Logging the first major mass divergence (Split + Reject).

*   **Form Filled:** "Crush & Split Transformation"
    *   `Mass IN` (2.850 kg - inherited from parent node)
    *   `Split Mass OUT` (e.g., 150.0 g - the portion moving forward)
    *   `Reject Mass` (e.g., 2.700 kg - the portion stored in the yard)
    *   `Reject Storage Location` (e.g., Bin A-12)
    *   `P80 Check (µm)` (e.g., 75 µm - ensuring crushing quality)
*   **System Action (The Graph):**
    *   Creates an **Edge** (`Operation: Crushed & Split`).
    *   Creates a child **`Crushed Split` Node** (Mass: 150g, Status: Active).
    *   Creates a child **`Coarse Reject` Node** (Mass: 2.7kg, Status: Retained).
    *   *UI Check:* `Mass IN` must equal `Mass OUT + Reject Mass`. The "Commit" button is disabled until the equation balances.

---

### Stage 4: Pulverising
**User:** Preparation Lab Technician
**Physical Action:** Ring mill pulverizes the 150g split into fine dust. A portion is kept as pulp reject.
**Digital Activity:** Logging the transition from coarse material to analytical pulp.

*   **Form Filled:** "Pulverising Log"
    *   `Mass IN` (150g - inherited)
    *   `Pulp Mass OUT` (e.g., 100.0 g - sent to pulp library)
    *   `Pulp Reject Mass` (e.g., 50.0 g - stored on shelf)
    *   `Pulveriser ID` / `Grinding Time`
*   **System Action (The Graph):**
    *   Creates an **Edge** (`Operation: Pulverised`).
    *   Creates a child **`Pulp` Node** (Mass: 100g, Status: Active).
    *   Creates a child **`Pulp Reject` Node** (Mass: 50g, Status: Retained).

---

### Stage 5: Fire Assay Setup (Weighing & Tray Loading)
**User:** Fire Assay Technician
**Physical Action:** A 50g test portion is scooped from the pulp, mixed with flux, and placed into a furnace tray.
**Digital Activity:** Spatial mapping of the sample into the furnace.

*   **Form Filled:** "Assay Charge Weighing" (Often done via batch grid UI)
    *   `Parent Pulp ID` (Scanned)
    *   `Charge Mass` (e.g., 50.00 g)
    *   `Pulp Retained?` (Checkbox: True - crucial for graph logic)
    *   `Tray ID` (e.g., Tray 07)
    *   `Tray Position` (e.g., Pos 23)
    *   `Flux Lot Number` (e.g., Lot 9921)
*   **System Action (The Graph):**
    *   Creates an **Edge** (`Operation: Weighed Charge`).
    *   Creates a child **`Assay Charge` Node** (Mass: 50g). This node contains spatial and consumable metadata (`Tray 07, Pos 23, Flux 9921`).
    *   The parent `Pulp` Node remains `Active` (status is not consumed, as 50g remains in storage).

---

### Stage 6: Fusion
**User:** Fire Assay Technician
**Physical Action:** Tray is loaded into the furnace at 1050°C. Sample melts, lead oxide reduces to molten lead, collecting precious metals. Slag is discarded/retained.
**Digital Activity:** Batch transformation of charges to lead buttons.

*   **Form Filled:** "Fusion Batch Execution" (Usually applies to a whole tray at once)
    *   `Furnace ID` (e.g., Furnace A)
    *   `Fusion Time` / `Temperature`
    *   `Lead Button Mass` (e.g., 30.0 g - weighed after cooling)
    *   `Slag Disposition` (Retained/Discarded)
*   **System Action (The Graph):**
    *   Creates an **Edge** (`Operation: Fusion`) connecting the `Assay Charge` to the next node.
    *   Creates a child **`Lead Button` Node** (Mass: 30g).
    *   Creates a child **`Slag` Node** (Mass: 20g, Status: Retained). *Note: 50g charge = 30g button + 20g slag.*
    *   `Assay Charge` status changes to `Consumed`.

---

### Stage 7: Cupellation
**User:** Fire Assay Technician
**Physical Action:** Lead buttons are placed into cupels (magnesia cups) and fired at 1000°C. Lead oxidizes and absorbs into the cupel, leaving a tiny precious metal bead (prill).
**Digital Activity:** Logging the final physical transformation.

*   **Form Filled:** "Cupellation Log"
    *   `Parent Lead Button` (Inherited from tray position)
    *   `Cupel Lot Number` (e.g., Lot 883B)
    *   `Cupellation Time` / `Temperature`
*   **System Action (The Graph):**
    *   Creates an **Edge** (`Operation: Cupellation`).
    *   Creates a child **`Prill` Node** (Mass: 0.00006g / 60µg - usually left blank until the next step).
    *   `Lead Button` status changes to `Consumed`.

---

### Stage 8: Finish (Weighing & Grade Calculation)
**User:** Analytical Chemist / Microbalance Operator
**Physical Action:** The prill is dissolved in acid (AAS/ICP) or weighed directly on a microbalance to determine gold content.
**Digital Activity:** Generating the final analytical result node, closing the graph loop.

*   **Form Filled:** "Result Entry"
    *   `Prill Weight (µg)` (e.g., 60.0 µg - entered via direct RS232 microbalance integration)
    *   `Instrument ID` (e.g., AAS-02)
*   **System Action (The Graph):**
    *   Calculates `Grade`: (Prill Weight / Original Charge Mass) * 1000
        *   *Calculation:* (0.00006g / 50.00g) = 0.0000012 = 1.2 g/t Au.
    *   Creates an **Edge** (`Operation: Finish/Calc`).
    *   Creates a child **`Result` Node** (Value: 1.2 g/t Au).
    *   `Prill` Node status changes to `Consumed`.

---

### Stage 9: Audit & Sign-off
**User:** QA/QC Manager or Laboratory Auditor
**Physical Action:** Reviewing batch paperwork and verifying data integrity.
**Digital Activity:** Using the "Sample Graph Explorer" UI to validate the DAG.

*   **Activity: Visual Graph Inspection**
    *   Searches for `Result Node` (1.2 g/t Au).
    *   Clicks "Expand Full Lineage" to visually trace the graph backward from the Result to the Field Sample.
    *   Inspects edges for red Mass-Balance warnings.
    *   Checks the `P80` metadata on the Crushed Split node to verify prep quality.
    *   Checks `Flux Lot` and `Cupel Lot` nodes to ensure no bad consumables affected the batch.
*   **System Action:** 
    *   Graph validates that all nodes form an unbroken Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG).
    *   Manager clicks "Approve Batch". Results are exported to the Geology Database.

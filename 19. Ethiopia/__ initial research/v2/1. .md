
## Understanding the Business Before Designing the Solution

**How Accountable Africa Capital (AAC) actually makes money:** AAC has invested in a Carbon-in-Leach (CIL) processing plant. CIL plants don't mine ore themselves — they buy or toll-process **tailings** (previously mined, low-grade "waste" material) from 4–10 small-scale mining concessions, run it through cyanide leaching + carbon adsorption, and strip out the residual gold that earlier, less efficient mining/processing missed. Revenue is therefore a function of three levers: (1) **tonnage of tailings processed**, (2) **recoverable grade** (grams of gold per tonne) confirmed by geology, and (3) **gold price** realized on the doré/bullion sold. AAC's costs are dominated by reagents, power, **fuel and logistics for excavation/haulage**, labor, and payments (royalty, tolling fee, or outright purchase) to the concession holders who supply the tailings.

This is why your four monitoring requirements aren't just "nice to have" IoT — they sit directly on top of AAC's P&L:

- Fuel and excavator monitoring = **direct cost control** on the largest controllable opex line.
- Tailings volume/grade tracking = **revenue forecasting and feedstock planning** (how much ore is coming, from where, when).
- Dump truck geotagging = **chain-of-custody and shrinkage/theft control** between concession and plant — the single biggest leakage point in tolling operations.

## The End-to-End Operational Flow

```
Concession Holders (4–10 sites)
   ↓ AAC lends excavators (GPS + fuel-sensor equipped) → geotagged to permit boundary
   ↓ Excavator digs/loads tailings within permitted geofence
   ↓ Drone baseline + bi-weekly resurvey confirms in-situ volume vs. geologist model
   ↓
Dump Trucks (geotagged, weighed)
   ↓ Loaded at concession, GPS route from concession → CIL plant
   ↓ Weighbridge + moisture/grade check on arrival at plant gate
   ↓
CIL Plant (Accra-area or site)
   ↓ Crush/grind → Cyanide leach → Carbon-in-Leach adsorption → elution/electrowinning → smelting
   ↓ Doré bar produced
   ↓
Sale to refinery/bank (e.g., PMMC Ghana, Bank of Ghana gold purchase program, or export refinery)
   ↓
Revenue → less reagent/fuel/labor/royalty/tolling costs → EBITDA
```

Your tech platform sits as a **digital control layer wrapped around every arrow in that chain**.

## Solution 1: Excavator Utilization & Geotagged Permit Compliance

**Problem:** AAC lends excavators to concession holders. Without monitoring, machines can be used outside the permitted concession boundary (illegal mining exposure, permit violation, asset misuse) or sub-let/idle.

**Solution — IoT Telematics + Geofencing:**

- Install ruggedized **CAN-bus telematics gateways** (e.g., Technoton CANUp-class, or Cat/Komatsu OEM telematics where machines are branded) on every excavator, reading engine ECU data directly rather than relying on aftermarket add-ons.
- Each concession's **government-issued permit polygon** (lat/long boundary) is loaded as a **geofence** in a central GIS platform (e.g., built on QGIS/PostGIS or a commercial fleet platform like Geotab/Samsara with custom zones).
- Real-time alerts fire the moment a machine crosses the permit boundary, moves after-hours, or an unauthorized operator attempts ignition (RFID/PIN operator ID lock).
- Engine-hour and idle-vs-working-mode data is logged automatically (idle/optimal/overload states) for utilization reporting.

**Benefits & Business Impact:**

- **Legal/regulatory protection** — provable, timestamped evidence that AAC-owned equipment never operated outside licensed concession boundaries, protecting AAC from complicity in illegal ("galamsey") mining, which is a major reputational and legal exposure in Ghanaian mining.
- **Asset utilization visibility** — AAC can see which concessions are actually productive vs. under-using loaned equipment, informing renegotiation or reallocation of excavators to higher-yield concessions.
- **Reduced unauthorized use/theft** of high-capex assets (an excavator is typically $150K–$400K); geofence + ignition lock materially cuts unauthorized after-hours use.

## Solution 2: Fuel Usage Monitoring (Excavators & Fleet)

**Problem:** Fuel is one of the largest controllable costs in mining/earthmoving, and diesel theft from remote equipment is endemic industry-wide.

**Solution — Capacitive Fuel-Level Sensors + Flow Meters:**

- **Capacitive fuel level sensors** in every tank (accurate to ~1%), paired with the same telematics gateway, detect sudden-drop events (siphoning) vs. gradual consumption (normal burn), and flag return-line tampering/dilution.
- For plant-side generators or high-flow equipment, add **inline fuel flow meters** for consumption-per-hour precision.
- All data streams to a **cloud fuel-management dashboard** correlated against engine hours and GPS location — so AAC can compute **liters/hour/tonne-moved** as a real KPI per machine, per concession.
- Automated **fuel reconciliation**: fuel delivered (from supplier invoices) vs. fuel consumed (from sensors) vs. fuel remaining (from tank sensor) closes the loop and flags variance instantly instead of at month-end audit.

**Benefits & Business Impact:**

- Industry data consistently shows **fuel theft/leakage of 10–25%** on unmonitored remote heavy-equipment fleets; sensor-based monitoring typically recovers most of that — on a multi-excavator fleet this is a direct, fast-payback cost reduction (sensors + gateway usually pay for themselves in 3–6 months from theft prevention alone).
- **Predictive maintenance** — abnormal fuel/engine-load patterns flag failing injectors or engine issues before breakdown, cutting unplanned downtime.
- Turns fuel from an opaque, invoice-based expense into a **per-tonne cost metric**, letting AAC benchmark and negotiate concession-level or contractor-level efficiency.

## Solution 3: Tailings Volume Estimation — Drone + Geological Baseline, Bi-Weekly

**Problem:** AAC needs a defensible, repeatable estimate of tailings ore-waste volume/grade evolving at each concession, anchored to real geologist data rather than guesswork, to plan plant feed and forecast revenue.

**Solution — UAV Photogrammetry/LiDAR + GIS + Geological Model Fusion:**

- **Baseline survey per concession:** a geologist establishes an initial resource/grade model (drill/auger sampling, assay data) — this becomes the "ground truth" grade layer.
- **Drone volumetric surveys, bi-weekly:** RTK-enabled photogrammetry drones (e.g., DJI Matrice 4D-class for sub-100-acre concessions, or fixed-wing VTOL like WingtraOne for larger sites) fly each concession on a fixed schedule. Photogrammetry achieves roughly **2–5% volumetric accuracy** on stockpiles/dumps, and combining LiDAR ground returns with RGB photogrammetry improves this further in vegetated or shaded terrain — both are now standard, cost-effective techniques for mine tailings and dump-site volumetrics.
- **Change-detection processing** (Pix4D, DroneDeploy, or an open-source pipeline using OpenDroneMap + PostGIS) compares each survey's point cloud to the previous one, producing a **cut/fill volume delta** — i.e., exactly how much tailings was removed/added at each concession over the two-week window.
- This volumetric data is **fused with the geologist's grade model** in a GIS layer, so AAC converts "cubic meters moved" into an **estimated gold-ounce equivalent per concession per period** — the actual revenue-planning number.
- A central dashboard shows all concessions on one map with time-lapse volume trends, feeding a rolling feedstock forecast for the plant.

**Benefits & Business Impact:**

- Replaces manual, dangerous, and infrequent ground surveys with a **10x faster, safer, cm-to-decimeter accurate** process — turns quarterly/annual estimates into a **live, bi-weekly feedstock pipeline view**.
- **Direct revenue-planning value:** AAC can schedule plant throughput, reagent procurement, and cash flow against a real, evolving picture of available tailings rather than static geologist reports that go stale within weeks.
- **Contractual/dispute protection** — objective, timestamped volumetric evidence of what was extracted from each concession supports fair payment/royalty calculation to concession holders and defends AAC in any grade/volume disputes.
- Over time, the bi-weekly dataset becomes a **proprietary time-series asset** — useful for optimizing which concessions to prioritize and for due diligence on future concession acquisitions.

## Solution 4: Dump Truck Geotagging & Movement Monitoring

**Problem:** The concession → plant haul is the point of maximum shrinkage risk (diversion, under-reporting, mixing with unauthorized material).

**Solution — GPS Fleet Tracking + Route/Geofence Rules + Weighbridge Integration:**

- Every dump truck gets a GPS tracker with **defined route corridors** (concession exit gate → CIL plant gate); deviation beyond a tolerance band, unscheduled stops, or route reversal trigger real-time alerts to dispatch.
- **Load-point and drop-point geofencing**: a load event is only "valid" if the truck was inside the concession geofence at pickup and the CIL plant geofence at delivery — closing the chain-of-custody loop.
- Integrate **weighbridge data** (tare/gross weight at both ends, ideally with automatic ANPR/RFID vehicle ID capture) so tonnage claimed at the concession is reconciled against tonnage received at the plant — flags short-loading, over-declaration, or diversion.
- Optional: **axle-load sensors** to prevent overload fines/road damage, and dashcam/telematics video for high-value or high-risk routes.

**Benefits & Business Impact:**

- Closes the **single largest leakage point** in a tolling model — material that leaves a concession but never officially arrives at the plant is pure lost revenue; even a 3–5% shrinkage reduction on haul volumes is material at scale.
- **Real-time logistics visibility** improves plant feed scheduling (know exactly what's inbound and when) and reduces truck idle time/demurrage.
- Provides an **auditable custody trail** for AAC's investors, regulators, and any future ESG/responsible-sourcing certification (increasingly required for gold buyers/refiners).

## Unifying Architecture — The AAC Operations Platform

Rather than four disconnected point solutions, these should be delivered as **one integrated platform** so AAC gets a single source of truth:

| Layer         | Technology                                                                                                                                                               |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Edge/sensors  | CAN-bus telematics gateways, capacitive fuel sensors, GPS trackers, RTK drones, weighbridge/ANPR                                                                         |
| Connectivity  | Cellular (4G/LTE) primary;**satellite IoT backup** (e.g., low-cost sat modules) for remote concessions with poor coverage — common in Ghanaian rural mining areas |
| Data platform | Cloud time-series database + PostGIS for geospatial layers; ingestion via MQTT/REST                                                                                      |
| Intelligence  | GIS engine for geofencing/volumetrics; ML models for anomaly detection (fuel theft patterns, route deviation, grade-volume reconciliation)                               |
| Application   | Web dashboard (fleet manager, geologist, finance/exec views) + mobile app for field/concession staff                                                                     |
| Integration   | Exports to AAC's ERP/accounting for fuel cost allocation, royalty calculation, and revenue forecasting                                                                   |

This is the kind of build I'd scope as: **Phase 1** (fuel + excavator telematics + geofencing, 6–10 weeks), **Phase 2** (dump truck tracking + weighbridge integration, parallel), **Phase 3** (drone volumetrics + GIS fusion with geologist data, bi-weekly cadence operationalized, 8–12 weeks), **Phase 4** (unified dashboard + predictive analytics).

## Revenue & Financial Model

### How AAC (the client) makes money — illustrative unit economics

Using current spot gold (~**$4,300/oz** as of September 2026 — note gold has been volatile this year, so AAC's model should stress-test a range) as a reference:

| Driver                                                 | Illustrative assumption                                                                                                     |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Tailings processed                                     | e.g., 10,000–30,000 tonnes/month across 4–10 concessions                                                                  |
| Recoverable grade                                      | 0.3–1.2 g/tonne (typical for retreatment tailings; confirmed per-concession by geologist + drone-volume fusion)            |
| CIL recovery efficiency                                | 80–92%                                                                                                                     |
| Gold price                                             | ~$4,300/oz (~$138/gram) spot — model should also run a conservative case (e.g., $2,800–3,500/oz) for planning discipline |
| Cost to concession holders (royalty/tolling split)     | typically 10–30% of recovered value, or fixed $/tonne purchase                                                             |
| Plant opex (reagents, power, labor, fuel, maintenance) | major variable cost line — this is exactly what Solutions 1–2 compress                                                    |

A rough per-tonne economics example (illustrative only, not a substitute for AAC's own metallurgical/financial model):

- 1,000 tonnes at 0.6 g/t grade, 85% recovery = ~510 g recovered ≈ 16.4 oz
- At $4,300/oz ≈ **$70,500 gross revenue per 1,000 tonnes**
- Less concession payment (~20%), reagents/power/fuel/labor opex (~35–45% typically), leaves an EBITDA margin commonly in the **25–40%** range for well-run CIL toll-treatment operations — the exact figure depends heavily on grade and gold price realized.

The four monitoring solutions move this model from **estimate to measured fact** — grade × volume becomes auditable, fuel cost per tonne becomes a controllable KPI, and shrinkage between concession and plant (previously an unmeasured leak) becomes visible and closeable.

### How your technology company monetizes this consultancy

A layered model is typical for this kind of industrial IoT + GIS engagement:

1. **Implementation/integration fee** (one-time): hardware procurement/installation, platform build, drone program setup, geologist-data integration — typically the larger upfront number.
2. **Recurring SaaS/platform subscription**: per-asset or per-concession monthly fee (e.g., $ per excavator/truck tracked, $ per concession dashboard) covering hosting, alerts, dashboards, support.
3. **Drone survey-as-a-service**: either AAC's own drone team trained by you, or a recurring bi-weekly survey-and-processing fee if you operate it as a managed service.
4. **Data/analytics add-on**: predictive maintenance, grade-forecasting models, or ESG/responsible-sourcing reporting as premium tiers.

This gives you both a **project-based revenue** (implementation) and a **recurring revenue base** (SaaS + managed drone service) tied directly to the number of concessions and assets AAC operates — which scales naturally as AAC grows from 4 to 10+ concessions.


## What Accountable Africa Capital's (AAC) Business Actually Is

AAC is not a traditional mine operator — it's a **tailings re-processing / gold recovery business**. It has invested in a **CIL (Carbon-in-Leach) processing plant** that doesn't dig gold out of the ground itself; it buys or tolls the **waste material ("tailings")** left behind by 4–10 small-scale mining concessions, and extracts the residual gold that earlier, cruder mining/processing methods failed to recover (informal/manual small-scale mining in Ghana typically recovers well under 70% of available gold, leaving significant value in the waste). AAC's core asset isn't land or a mine — it's **process technology + logistics + capital**, applied to other people's low-grade waste.

---

## Who AAC's "Clients" Actually Are

It's useful to separate AAC's stakeholders into three distinct groups, because each interacts with the business differently:

**1. Feedstock suppliers (not customers — upstream partners):**
The 4–10 **small-scale/artisanal mining concession holders**. In Ghana, small-scale concessions are legally capped at up to 25 acres and licensed under the Minerals Commission framework. These concession holders are AAC's *suppliers of raw material* (tailings), not buyers of AAC's output. AAC either:

- **Buys** their tailings outright at an agreed $/tonne or $/gram-of-contained-gold price, or
- **Tolls** their material (processes it for a fee, returning a share of recovered gold), or
- **Royalty/revenue-shares** with them based on confirmed grade and volume.
  AAC lends them excavators as part of this relationship — an in-kind investment that secures feedstock supply and builds loyalty/exclusivity with each concession.

**2. Off-take buyers (the true "customers" for AAC's product — refined/doré gold):**

- **PMMC (Precious Minerals Marketing Company)**, Ghana's state-mandated buyer and assayer for small-scale-sector gold, which grades, assays, and purchases gold and has historically purchased hundreds of thousands of ounces from the small-scale sector for onward export.
- **Bank of Ghana**, via the **Gold-4-Oil / domestic gold purchase programme**, which PMMC has been mandated to help supply — an increasingly important buyer as Ghana channels domestically produced gold into its reserves and forex/oil-import strategy.
- **Licensed private gold buyers/exporters and overseas refineries**, where legally permitted, as an alternative or supplementary off-take channel.
  This is where AAC's actual revenue is realized — the doré bar sale is the invoice-generating event.

**3. Capital clients / stakeholders:**
AAC itself is an **investment vehicle** — so its own "client" in a sense is its investor base (the capital that funded the CIL plant), to whom it owes returns, transparency, and ESG-compliant, auditable operations. This is precisely why defensible data (geotagged permits, volumetric tailings tracking, chain-of-custody logistics) matters commercially, not just operationally — it's what makes the investment auditable and de-risked for AAC's capital providers.

## Full Business Flow (Value Chain)

```
1. CONCESSION SOURCING & AGREEMENT
   AAC signs supply/tolling agreements with 4–10 licensed concession holders
   → defines permit boundary, pricing/royalty terms, equipment loan terms
        ↓
2. GEOLOGICAL BASELINE
   Independent geologist surveys each concession → establishes grade (g/tonne) 
   and estimated tailings volume → this is the resource model AAC plans against
        ↓
3. EXCAVATION
   AAC-owned excavators (loaned to concession holders) extract/load tailings 
   within the permitted geofenced area
        ↓
4. HAULAGE
   Dump trucks move tailings from concession → CIL plant (this is the 
   custody-transfer point with the highest shrinkage/theft risk)
        ↓
5. PROCESSING AT THE CIL PLANT
   Crush → grind → cyanide leach → carbon-in-leach adsorption → 
   elution/electrowinning → smelting → doré bar
        ↓
6. SALE OF GOLD
   Doré sold to PMMC / Bank of Ghana (Gold-4-Oil) / licensed exporter/refinery
   → this is AAC's actual revenue-generating transaction
        ↓
7. SETTLEMENT & DISTRIBUTION
   Cash proceeds → pay concession holders (royalty/tolling share) → 
   cover plant opex → remainder is AAC's margin/return to investors
```

## Cost Structure

| Category                              | What it includes                                                 | Notes                                                                                                                      |
| ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Capex (sunk/depreciating)**   | CIL plant construction, excavators, dump trucks, plant machinery | The big upfront investment; excavators loaned out are a capital cost with no direct rental income unless structured as one |
| **Feedstock cost**              | Payments/royalties to concession holders for tailings supplied   | Structured as $/tonne purchase, tolling fee, or % revenue share                                                            |
| **Reagents & consumables**      | Cyanide, lime, activated carbon, flocculants, grinding media     | Scales directly with tonnage processed                                                                                     |
| **Fuel & logistics**            | Diesel for excavators, dump trucks, plant generators/power       | One of the largest controllable costs — and your highest-leverage monitoring target                                       |
| **Labor**                       | Plant operators, geologists, drivers, security, admin            | Fixed + some variable overtime cost                                                                                        |
| **Maintenance**                 | Excavator/truck servicing, plant equipment upkeep                | Predictive maintenance from telematics reduces this                                                                        |
| **Compliance & environmental**  | EPA permits, reclamation bonds, safety, ESG reporting            | Increasingly important for buyer/refiner due-diligence requirements                                                        |
| **Logistics/security overhead** | Route security, insurance for high-value transport (gold, cash)  | Rises with theft/diversion risk if unmonitored                                                                             |
| **SG&A**                        | AAC corporate overhead, finance, management                      | Fixed cost, not tonnage-driven                                                                                             |

**The critical insight:** feedstock cost, fuel, and shrinkage/logistics losses are the three largest *variable and controllable* cost lines — and they are precisely the four things your monitoring platform targets (excavator/fuel usage, tailings volume verification, truck custody tracking).

## Revenue Structure

Revenue is a single formula, repeated across every concession and every processing cycle:

**Revenue = Tonnes processed × Grade (g/tonne) × Recovery rate (%) × Gold price ($/gram)**

- **Tonnes processed** — driven by how much tailings AAC can source and haul from its concessions; this is where bi-weekly drone volumetrics matter, because it tells AAC (and its investors) how much feedstock is actually available and coming, not just what was originally estimated.
- **Grade** — set by the geologist's baseline model, refined over time as actual recovered gold from each concession is reconciled against the model (a feedback loop that improves forecasting accuracy).
- **Recovery rate** — a function of plant efficiency (CIL processes typically recover a high percentage of contained gold that's amenable to leaching, well above what informal manual processing achieves — this recovery uplift is the entire economic rationale for the business).
- **Gold price** — set by the market at the point of sale to PMMC/Bank of Ghana/exporter, largely outside AAC's control, so this is the variable AAC has least influence over and should treat as the risk variable in financial planning (gold has been highly volatile through 2026).

**Payment mechanics:** sale proceeds are typically settled by PMMC or the Bank of Ghana under Ghana's formal gold-marketing framework, with funds then flowing back through AAC's books to cover concession payments, plant costs, and investor returns — meaning **timing and reliability of this off-take relationship is itself a revenue-recognition and cash-flow risk** worth modeling explicitly (payment terms, assay disputes, and export/marketing-channel bottlenecks all affect when revenue actually lands).

## The Business in One Sentence

**AAC buys low-grade, previously-mined waste from small-scale mining concessions, applies superior chemical processing to recover the gold that informal mining left behind, and sells that recovered gold into Ghana's formal gold-marketing system — with margin coming from the gap between what it pays for tailings/inputs and the market value of the gold it recovers, minus fuel, logistics, and processing costs.**

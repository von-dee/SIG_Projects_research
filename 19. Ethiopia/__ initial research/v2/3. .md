## Who AAC's Clients Are (Ethiopia-Specific)

**1. Feedstock suppliers (upstream partners, not customers):**
Licensed artisanal mining **associations/cooperatives** and individual small-scale miners in the Assosa Zone — Ethiopia's formal ASM framework requires miners to be registered as associations or licensed individuals to legally sell gold. This is an important operating constraint: regional authorities have signaled they may **revoke licenses of associations that don't supply the central bank**, and there has been active crackdown on **unlicensed foreign (reportedly Chinese) involvement** in artisanal mining in Benishangul-Gumuz — meaning AAC's concession contracts need to be airtight on licensing status, and your geotagging/permit-compliance platform isn't just an efficiency tool here, it's **regulatory survival infrastructure** proving AAC only sources from licensed, legitimate concessions.

**2. Off-take buyer — currently the National Bank of Ethiopia (NBE):**
This is the single biggest structural difference from a Ghana-based model. The NBE has historically bought gold from artisanal producers at a **premium of 35–67% above the international gold price**, specifically to pull supply out of the parallel/contraband market — meaning AAC's realized price per gram can be *significantly above* international spot, not equal to it. This premium is a major, non-obvious revenue lever.

However: recent reporting (IMF, via Ethiopian press) indicates the **NBE plans to exit direct gold buying by end of 2026, with licensed private banks taking over** the purchase channel. This is a live structural transition AAC needs to track closely — it affects who the off-take "client" actually is going forward, and the deal terms (premium levels, payment speed, forex conversion) may change materially.

**3. Currency and forex exposure:**
Settlement is in **Ethiopian Birr**, and the Birr was floated in 2024, producing sharp local-currency price swings — NBE's gold buying price per gram roughly quintupled between January 2024 and October 2025 in Birr terms. This means AAC's revenue model must be run in **both USD-equivalent and Birr terms**, with explicit modeling of currency risk and repatriation rules, not just gold-price risk.

**4. Capital clients:** AAC's own investors, same as before — and given the regulatory volatility here (foreign-national crackdowns, licensing revocations, a buyer-channel transition), **auditable, geotagged, compliant sourcing data is arguably even more critical to de-risking this investment** than in a more stable jurisdiction.

Hosting an event on the scale of the Future Minerals Forum (FMF) requires a 12-to-18-month runway. The process must be treated like a major engineering project, with strict timelines, dependencies, and quality controls. 

Here are the **phases and stages** to successfully pull off an event of this magnitude, broken down by timeline, key activities, and critical notes.

---

### Phase 1: Foundation & Strategy (Months 12 to 9 Before)
This phase is about setting the anchor. If the anchor is set in the wrong place, the whole event drifts.

*   **Key Activities:**
    *   **Executive Steering:** Define the core theme (e.g., "Minerals for the Energy Transition") and target audience (Heads of State, Mining CEOs, Investors).
    *   **Financials:** Finalize the master budget, ticket pricing strategy, and sponsorship tiers.
    *   **Venue Sourcing:** Secure a venue capable of hosting plenary sessions, massive exhibition halls, bilateral meeting rooms, and high-security VIP areas.
    *   **Dates & Diplomacy:** Lock in dates that do not conflict with other major events (e.g., PDAC, Mining Indaba). Send "Save the Date" notices to key government and corporate stakeholders.
    *   **Core Team Hiring:** Recruit the Event Director, Content Director, and Commercial Director.
*   **Key Things to Note:** 
    *   *Government Alignment:* If this is an international forum, you must align with the host country’s Ministry of Mining/Industry early to leverage their diplomatic channels for VIP attendance.

### Phase 2: Development & Sales (Months 8 to 5 Before)
The skeleton is built; now you add the muscle. This is the most aggressive sales and marketing period.

*   **Key Activities:**
    *   **Commercial:** Launch the sponsorship prospectus. Begin outreach to major mining houses for Title/Platinum sponsorships. Open exhibition booth sales.
    *   **Content:** Open the "Call for Papers/Speakers." Invite high-profile Keynote speakers and secure top-tier moderators.
    *   **Marketing:** Launch the event website. Open general attendee registration. Publish the high-level agenda.
    *   **VIP & Protocol:** Send formal VIP invitations via the appropriate government channels. Begin securing hotel room blocks for delegations.
    *   **Visa Support:** Begin issuing Visa support letters for international attendees (crucial for large international events).
*   **Key Things to Note:**
    *   *Sponsor Dependency:* Major sponsors (like a national mining company) often demand speaking slots or pavilion space. Ensure the Commercial and Content teams communicate daily so promises made by sales align with the agenda's capacity.

### Phase 3: Execution & Marketing Push (Months 4 to 2 Before)
Momentum building. The event must feel inevitable and unmissable.

*   **Key Activities:**
    *   **Content:** Finalize the full agenda, breakout sessions, and workshop tracks. Collect initial speaker headshots, bios, and presentation synopses.
    *   **Marketing:** Shift from awareness to conversion. Launch targeted LinkedIn ad campaigns. Publish speaker announcements weekly.
    *   **Operations:** Conduct site visits with the venue. Finalize floor plans, F&B (Food & Beverage) menus, and AV (Audio/Visual) technical riders.
    *   **Tech:** Begin building the event mobile app. Set up the registration badge printing software.
    *   **Security:** If hosting heads of state, coordinate with national police, private security firms, and the venue’s security team to establish security perimeters and motorcade routes.
*   **Key Things to Note:**
    *   *Bilateral Meetings:* High-level attendees come for closed-door, B2B and G2G (Government-to-Government) meetings. Start mapping out who wants to meet whom and allocate the necessary private rooms.

### Phase 4: The Final Countdown (Weeks 4 to 1 Before)
The transition from planning to logistics. No major changes can happen now.

*   **Key Activities:**
    *   **Content:** Lock the Run of Show (ROS). Conduct pre-event Zoom rehearsals with moderators and speakers to QA presentations.
    *   **VIP:** Finalize VIP travel itineraries, airport greets, and dedicated chauffeur assignments. 
    *   **Operations:** Finalize the "Production Book" (the master document detailing every minute of the event, who is on stage, what lighting is used, etc.). 
    *   **B2B Matchmaking:** Open the event app so delegates can start booking meetings with each other. Release the finalized schedule.
    *   **Staffing:** Conduct briefings with temporary staff, volunteers, and security. Assign specific zone managers.
*   **Key Things to Note:**
    *   *The "No New Ideas" Rule:* At week 2, the door closes on new ideas. If someone suggests a new panel or a new VIP lounge, the answer must be "for this year, no; let's put it in the file for next year." Protect the execution.

### Phase 5: Days Before & During the Event (Showtime)
Execution mode. The goal is flawless delivery while putting out invisible fires.

*   **Days Before (Move-In):**
    *   Venue takeover. AV teams set up stages, lighting, and internet. 
    *   Registration desk built and tested.
    *   Final walk-through with the venue, security, and fire marshals.
*   **During the Event:**
    *   **Daily Morning Briefing:** The Event Director meets with all Department Heads at 7:00 AM to review the day's VIP movements, security alerts, and any schedule changes.
    *   **Content & Stage Management:** Stage managers ensure speakers are in the "green room" 30 minutes before their slot. Teleprompters are loaded.
    *   **VIP Hosting:** Protocol officers guide ministers and CEOs from session to session, ensuring they are never lost or waiting.
    *   **Crisis Management:** Have a dedicated "War Room" or operations center with key decision-makers to handle medical emergencies, VIP no-shows, or AV failures.
    *   **Live Marketing:** Push real-time photos, quotes, and video clips to social media and handle the global press corps in the media center.
*   **Key Things to Note:**
    *   *Radio Discipline:* All staff must be on two-way radios with designated channels (e.g., Ch 1: Ops, Ch 2: VIP, Ch 3: AV, Ch 4: Security). 
    *   *VIP Buffer:* VIPs are notoriously late or change plans on the fly. Have a dedicated "runner" to adjust schedules dynamically without disrupting the main stage.

### Phase 6: Post-Event (Days 1 to 30 After)
The event isn't over when the last attendee leaves. This is the measurement and retention phase.

*   **Key Activities:**
    *   **Teardown (Days 1-3):** Manage the safe teardown of AV, exhibition booths, and return of rented equipment. 
    *   **Data & Finance (Days 1-10):** Export registration data. Send out post-event attendee surveys. Finance begins reconciling final invoices and sponsor deliverables.
    *   **Content & PR (Days 1-14):** Publish post-event reports, white papers, and session recordings on the website. Send out press releases detailing the event's successes (e.g., "FMF facilitated $2B in new mining investments").
    *   **Follow-up (Days 7-30):** Send "Thank You" notes to sponsors, VIPs, and speakers. Share promotional codes for next year’s event with attendees while the memory is fresh.
    *   **Debrief (Day 30):** Hold a comprehensive "Lessons Learned" meeting with all Department Heads to document what went right, what failed, and what to change for the next edition.
*   **Key Things to Note:**
    *   *ESG Reporting:* For a minerals forum, sustainability is highly scrutinized. You must publish a post-event ESG report detailing the carbon offset, waste diverted from landfills, and diversity metrics of the speakers. Failing to do this damages credibility.

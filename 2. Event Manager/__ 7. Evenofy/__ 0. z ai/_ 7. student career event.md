Executing a university career fair or "Career Path Event" using a centralized Event Management Operating System (OS) transforms the typical chaotic campus planning process. The software acts as the central nervous system, replacing scattered Google Sheets, student organization WhatsApp groups, and manual resume checks with a single source of truth.

Here is the highly detailed, phase-by-phase execution playbook detailing **how to use the system** to pull off a successful student career event.

---

### Phase 1: Foundation & Strategy (Months 4 to 3 Before Event)
*The goal is configuring the system architecture, setting the budget, and mapping out the venue (e.g., student union, gymnasium) before opening registration.*

**System Activities:**
*   **Master Dashboard Setup:** The Event Director (or Career Services Lead) inputs the budget into the **Finance Module**. The system sets up KPI tracking for corporate sponsorships, student attendance goals, and target placement metrics.
*   **Venue CAD & Floorplan Builder:** The Ops team uploads the campus venue floorplans into the **Operations Module**. They digitally map out the employer booths, student networking lounges, and interview rooms. The system automatically calculates booth spacing and flags fire-exit blockages.
*   **Access Level Architecture:** The Tech team defines the access matrix in the **Registration Module** (e.g., Level 1 = Employers/Recruiters with early access; Level 2 = Student VIPs [e.g., honors students]; Level 3 = General Student Body; Level 4 = Faculty/Staff).
*   **Speaker & Panel Setup:** If the event includes keynote speakers or alumni panels, the Content team configures the **Speaker Portal** to collect bios, headshots, and presentation titles for the main stage.

**Key Things to Note:**
*   *Data Architecture is Everything:* If you configure the access levels or ticket categories incorrectly in the system during Month 3, it will cause bottlenecking at the doors. Take time to test the system's logic rules (e.g., ensuring only seniors get access to the exclusive interview suites) before opening registration.

---

### Phase 2: Development & Sales (Months 2 to 1.5 Before Event)
*The goal is securing employer sponsorships and getting students registered. The system shifts from configuration to active processing.*

**System Activities:**
*   **Commercial Launch (Employer Portal):** The Corporate Relations team launches the **Exhibitor Portal**. Employers log in, select their booth via the visual floorplan, pay sponsorship fees through the **Procurement System**, and input how many recruiters they are bringing.
*   **Student Registration Paths Go Live:** The Marketing team activates **Dynamic Registration Paths**. Students register using their university email (.edu), and the system prompts them to select their major, graduation year, and upload a digital resume.
*   **AI Matchmaking Pre-Setup:** The Tech team configures the **Matchmaking Algorithm** intent fields. When students and employers register, they select their goals (e.g., "Tech Internships," "Mechanical Engineering Full-time," "Diversity & Inclusion Initiatives"). The system begins scoring compatibility between student profiles and company hiring needs in the background.

**Key Things to Note:**
*   *Cross-Module Dependency:* The system modules must talk to each other. If the Corporate Relations team sells a "Gold Sponsorship," the system must automatically trigger the Content team to grant that employer a speaking slot on a panel, and the Registration team to issue premium parking passes for their recruiters.

---

### Phase 3: Execution & Marketing Push (Weeks 4 to 2 Before Event)
*The goal is campus-wide momentum and locking down complex logistics like interview scheduling and recruiter catering.*

**System Activities:**
*   **Interview Scheduler Opens:** The system unlocks the 1-on-1 interview room inventory. Employers can browse the student database (filtering by major/GPA), request meetings, and the system automatically cross-references the student's class schedule (synced via their app calendar) to book a 15-minute slot.
*   **F&B Dietary Matrix Aggregation:** As employer RSVPs flow in, the **F&B Module** aggregates dietary requirements for the recruiter luncheon. The system generates a report showing exactly how many vegetarian, vegan, and gluten-free meals are required, pushing this data directly to campus catering.
*   **Mobile App Beta & Soft Launch:** The Tech team launches the **Campus Event App**. Students can log in, build their personalized agenda for breakout sessions, and the system syncs with their Outlook/Gmail calendars. 
*   **Resume Book Generation:** The system automatically compiles all uploaded student resumes into a categorized, searchable "Resume Book" PDF, which is sent to employers who purchased that specific sponsorship tier.

**Key Things to Note:**
*   *The "Freeze" on Core Architecture:* At Week 2, the system configuration is frozen. No new room types, access levels, or ticket categories can be created. The system shifts from a "setup" tool to a "processing" tool to ensure stability for the influx of last-minute student sign-ups.

---

### Phase 4: The Final Countdown (Week 1 Before Event)
*The goal is finalizing data, rehearsing with speakers, and locking down the Run of Show (ROS). No new data is accepted.*

**System Activities:**
*   **Presentation QA Pipeline Lockdown:** The **Speaker Portal** locks. No more PPT uploads are allowed from alumni or faculty speakers. The system’s QA tool auto-converts all presentations to 16:9 PDF format, checks for embedded fonts, and pushes them to the main AV switcher. Version control is locked to prevent on-stage tech issues.
*   **ROS Engine Finalization:** The Content team finalizes the minute-by-minute **Run of Show (ROS) Engine** for the main stage. The system links panelist intros, lighting cues, and video rolls to specific timecodes.
*   **QR Code Badge Encoding & Pre-Printing:** The Registration team exports the final attendee database. The system generates unique QR codes for each student and employer, linking directly to their digital profile/resume. Badges are pre-printed or set up for self-service kiosk printing.
*   **Student Volunteer Scheduler:** Ops uses the **Shift Generator** to assign student volunteers to specific zones (e.g., Registration Desk, Employer Lounge, Stage Management). The system pushes schedules to the Staff App and sets up **Geofence Clock-In** parameters (volunteers can only clock in if their phone GPS is on campus).

**Key Things to Note:**
*   *Enforcing the System's Rules:* At Week 1, students will try to bypass the system. A senior will ask if they can just "show up" without registering. The system must be programmed to reject walk-ins, or require them to register on-site via a self-service kiosk before printing a badge. This prevents overcrowding and ensures fire safety compliance.

---

### Phase 5: Days Before & During the Event (Showtime)
*The goal is real-time monitoring, managing crowd flow, and facilitating seamless student-employer networking.*

**System Activities:**
*   **Command Center Activation:** The Event Director monitors the **Live Dashboard** on a tablet. They watch the **Venue Capacity Heatmap** turn colors as QR code check-in data flows in from the main doors.
*   **Stage Management Console:** Backstage, the Stage Manager uses a tablet to control the **Traffic Light Countdown** for panel speakers. The system sends auto-push notifications to the Green Room: "Alumni Panel, you are needed backstage in 5 minutes."
*   **Real-Time Capacity Control:** If a popular breakout session (e.g., "Tech Careers Panel") hits 90% capacity, the **Door Scanner Software** alerts the volunteer at the door to start a digital waitlist. The system instantly pushes a notification to the app suggesting alternative, less crowded sessions.
*   **Smart Lead Retrieval (The Core Feature):** Employers use the app’s camera to scan the QR code on a student’s lanyard. This instantly captures the student's digital resume, major, and contact info, and routes it to the company's CRM. Students can also tap their phones to employer NFC stands to express interest.
*   **Incident Reporting:** If a crisis hits (e.g., a spilled drink causing a slip hazard), a volunteer logs it in the app as a Level 1 Incident. The system routes it ONLY to the Ops/Cleanup team, preventing the main organizers from being spammed with minor issues.

**Key Things to Note:**
*   *Wi-Fi Reliability:* Campus Wi-Fi can be spotty with thousands of students in one room. The app must have an **Offline Caching Mode** so students can still view their agendas and employers can scan QR codes. Data syncs to the cloud automatically once the connection is restored.

---

### Phase 6: Post-Event (Days 1 to 30+ After)
*The goal is data extraction, measuring student placement success, and proving ROI to university stakeholders and employers using the system’s analytics.*

**System Activities:**
*   **Automated NPS & Survey Engine:** Exactly 24 hours after the event ends, the system automatically emails customized surveys to students (e.g., "Did you secure an interview?") and employers (e.g., "Rate the quality of the candidates"). The **Sentiment Analysis** tool categorizes feedback (e.g., "Too crowded" vs. "Great companies").
*   **Employer ROI Lead Reports:** The system automatically compiles all the QR codes scanned by an employer into a CSV file with student names, emails, and resumes. This is emailed directly to the recruiters, fulfilling their sponsorship deliverables.
*   **Footfall & Heatmap Analytics:** The Career Services team exports the **Dwell Time Analytics** from the QR scanning data. The system generates a visual heatmap showing which employer booths had the longest lines and which breakout sessions had the highest drop-off rates.
*   **Placement Tracking Dashboard:** A unique module where students can update their status post-event (e.g., "Got an offer from Company X"). The system aggregates this data to show the university the direct economic/placement impact of the career fair.
*   **Finance Reconciliation:** The **Master Budget Engine** reconciles all final invoices (catering, AV, venue). The system flags any vendor overcharges against the original Purchase Orders.
*   **Data Purge (FERPA Compliance):** On Day 30, the system’s automated privacy protocols trigger. It securely deletes sensitive student academic data (GPA, transcripts) from the active database, retaining only non-personal data for next year's forecasting, ensuring compliance with student privacy laws.

**Key Things to Note:**
*   *Data-Driven Debrief:* The post-event debrief meeting with the university administration is no longer based on anecdotes ("I heard the lines were too long"). It is entirely driven by system data: "The system shows Company X scanned 150 resumes, but the booth capacity was only meant for 50 students; next year, we allocate them a double booth."
*   *The "Next Year" Pivot:* Before the data purge, the system automatically exports the list of employers who gave a 9 or 10 NPS score and pushes them into an automated email campaign for next year's sponsorship renewals with an early-bird discount.

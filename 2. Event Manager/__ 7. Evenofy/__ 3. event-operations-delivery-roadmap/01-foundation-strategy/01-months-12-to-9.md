> Phase 1: Foundation & Strategy → Months 12 to 9 Before Event

## Foundation & Strategy

### Objective

Set the event's operational rules before public commitments make them expensive to change. The event is not ready to sell or invite until its budget, venue, protocol, and access assumptions have been tested together.

### Workstream 1 — Budget and command baseline

The Event Director and Finance Lead establish the master budget, revenue plan, cost centers, approval limits, contingency reserve, and the daily measures that will appear in the command center. The team agrees what “on track” means for registration, sponsorship, exhibit sales, cash, capacity, and readiness.

**Control:** Finance must approve a baseline version before commercial quotes or purchase commitments are issued. A change in venue capacity, programme scope, or hospitality standard triggers a reforecast, not an informal spreadsheet edit.

### Workstream 2 — Venue and floor-plan readiness

Operations uploads and validates current venue drawings, then maps the plenary, exhibition halls, meeting rooms, media center, kitchens, medical points, loading docks, circulation routes, emergency egress, accessibility facilities, and VIP holding areas. Fire and safety stakeholders confirm that proposed booths, queues, and temporary structures do not obstruct exits.

**Control:** No sellable booth or meeting room is released until venue, Fire Safety, and Operations have signed off its usable capacity and operational constraints.

### Workstream 3 — Protocol policy

The Chief of Protocol defines ranks, precedence, delegation rules, bilateral restrictions, receiving-line order, seating tiebreakers, and confidential escalation routes. Security and host-government representatives test representative scenarios, including late changes and conflicting delegations.

**Control:** Any exception to rank or proximity policy has a recorded senior approver. Sensitive diplomatic notes are never distributed through general operations channels.

### Workstream 4 — Access and identity architecture

Security, Registration, and Technology define access tiers, approved zones, timing, escort rules, credential types, revocation process, and fallback gate procedures. They test a Head-of-State movement, an exhibitor build pass, a general delegate badge, a lost badge, and an offline gate.

**Control:** Access tier design is signed by Security and Protocol before registration opens. A role can see only the data and spaces needed to perform its work.

### Month 9 readiness gate

- Approved budget and owner list, including contingency.
- Validated venue plan and emergency egress review.
- Tested protocol and access scenarios, with named exception authority.
- Live reporting definitions and source-of-truth ownership.
- Risk: rushing this gate creates cascading bad credentials, seating disputes, inventory errors, and unbudgeted commitments later.

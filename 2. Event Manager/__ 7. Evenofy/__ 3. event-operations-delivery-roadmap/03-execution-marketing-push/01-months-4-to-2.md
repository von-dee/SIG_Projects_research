> Phase 3: Execution & Marketing Push → Months 4 to 2 Before Event

## Execution & Marketing Push

### Objective

Create public momentum while converting accumulated registrations and sales into workable seating, hospitality, meetings, and routes.

### Workstream 1 — Seating and protocol rehearsal

Protocol creates preliminary gala, plenary, bilateral, and holding-room layouts using confirmed people and current precedence rules. They test late acceptance, equal-rank tiebreaks, mobility needs, and incompatible-delegation separations with the host and Security.

**Control:** A seating plan is a controlled proposal until Chief of Protocol approves it. No one sends a sensitive seating layout through unprotected email or publishes it to broad event channels.

### Workstream 2 — Food, beverage, and capacity plan

F&B translates aggregated, consented dietary and attendance patterns into daily production forecasts by service point. Operations aligns meal windows with agenda breaks, room turnarounds, prayer needs, accessibility, and crowd flow.

**Control:** Catering receives the minimum information needed to produce safe food. Named medical or dietary details remain restricted; changes after production cut-off require safety approval.

### Workstream 3 — Mobile beta and participant communications

A representative pilot group tests sign-in, agenda building, map use, alerts, meetings, accessibility, and help pathways. The team fixes confusing messages before broad release and supplies an email/SMS/help-desk alternative for people who cannot use the app.

### Workstream 4 — Meeting hub opening

Participants start requesting meetings against honest availability. Concierge staff support high-stakes or diplomatic requests, while ordinary meetings use clear consent, scheduling, cancellation, and no-show etiquette. Private room inventory is protected from double booking.

### Month 2 core-architecture freeze

At this point, no new access tiers, ticket classes, room types, floorplan categories, or protocol rule sets are created without Event Director approval and a documented impact assessment. Small operational corrections continue, but structural changes move through a formal exception board.

**Risk:** Reopening foundational choices after this date corrupts badges, room allocations, pricing, staffing, and communications at once.

> Phase 5: Days Before & During the Event → Showtime

## Showtime

### Objective

Deliver a calm visible experience while the command structure detects, contains, and resolves exceptions behind the scenes.

### Day-minus-one activation

The War Room opens with a live readiness review of venue, life safety, network, credential stations, stages, transport, hotels, F&B, staff check-in, and VIP movements. Teams confirm who can make each decision overnight and how they will communicate a verified change.

### Live command center

The Event Director watches arrival flow, capacity, programme status, critical incidents, VIP readiness, transport, and service pressure. The dashboard helps leaders ask focused questions; it does not replace ground verification. Stale or uncertain information is visibly marked.

### Stage and speaker flow

Stage Managers use a simple next/now/previous view, keep speakers informed in the green room, and route schedule changes through the programme lead. A late principal prompts a pre-agreed contingency such as moderated audience questions, never an improvised public explanation.

### Capacity and access decisions

When a room approaches safe capacity, guest services guide later arrivals to an overflow experience before gates must close. A denied access decision has a respectful assistance route. Security handles suspicious or sensitive cases discreetly and separately from normal queue support.

### VIP movement and protocol

Assigned liaisons coordinate only the movements they need to know. When a motorcade is delayed, Protocol, Security, Stage, and host team receive the relevant update and agree the least disruptive adjustment. The principal's precise location is not broadcast to general staff.

### Networking and lead capture

Exhibitors and delegates exchange details by informed consent. During poor connectivity, lead capture and contact exchange can be stored safely for later synchronization; staff communicate that confirmation may take a moment.

### Incident management

A staff member logs a concern at the appropriate level. Medical, Security, or Operations receives only incidents relevant to their role; serious issues elevate to command. The person reporting gets acknowledgement and practical next instruction, preventing both panic and silent failure.

### Daily close

Each evening, functional leads reconcile unresolved incidents, tomorrow's movements, staffing gaps, meal forecasts, programme changes, and participant communications. Night shift receives a short handover focused on decisions, not raw chat history.

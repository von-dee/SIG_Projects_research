
# Technical Workshop Plan: 30 Minutes, 3 Mining Software Solutions, One Story

## The core idea: one story, three chapters

Don't present three products. Present **one mine, one bad day, and one recovery**. Each software becomes a chapter in the same story, so the audience remembers a narrative rather than a feature list.

**Suggested story:** *"A day at Mine X"*. A mid-sized open-pit (or underground) operation is missing its production targets. Costs are rising, equipment is breaking down, and nobody agrees on why. Over the session, we follow the operations team as they find the problem, fix it, and measure the result.

Pick the story to match your actual products. A typical trio and how it maps:

| Chapter                        | Software type                                  | Problem in the story                                     | Payoff                                   |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| 1.**Plan**               | Mine planning / scheduling                     | Plans don't survive contact with reality; targets missed | Realistic, optimized schedule            |
| 2.**Operate**            | Fleet management / real-time operations        | Trucks queue, shovels wait, fuel is wasted               | Higher utilization, lower cost per tonne |
| 3.**Maintain & Protect** | Asset health / predictive maintenance / safety | Unplanned breakdowns, safety incidents                   | Less downtime, safer site                |

(Swap in your real products: processing plant optimization, geology modeling, tailings monitoring, etc.)

## 30-minute run of show

| Time                  | Segment                          | Purpose                                                                                                                 |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **0:00-3:00**   | **Hook and the problem**   | Open with a striking number or scenario: "Every hour a haul truck sits idle costs $X." Introduce Mine X and its crisis. |
| **3:00-10:00**  | **Chapter 1: Software A**  | Show the problem, the demo, the result                                                                                  |
| **10:00-17:00** | **Chapter 2: Software B**  | Picks up where A left off; the plan meets the real world                                                                |
| **17:00-24:00** | **Chapter 3: Software C**  | Closes the loop; prevents the next crisis                                                                               |
| **24:00-27:00** | **The "after" picture**    | Mine X today: consolidated results, one connected view                                                                  |
| **27:00-30:00** | **Q&A and call to action** | Questions, next steps, how to engage                                                                                    |

## The structure of each chapter (about 7 minutes)

Use the same pattern every time so the audience always knows where they are:

1. **The pain (1 min):** a specific, relatable situation. "It's 2 a.m., the shovel is down, and trucks are queuing."
2. **The software in action (3-4 min):** a live or recorded demo of one workflow, not a tour of menus. Show one problem being solved end to end.
3. **The proof (1 min):** a measurable benefit, ideally from a real customer (e.g., "12% reduction in haul cycle time").
4. **The handoff (1 min):** a line that leads into the next chapter. "Now the plan is realistic. But can the fleet actually execute it?"

## What makes a *technical* workshop captivating

- **Show, don't tell.** Spend at least 50% of the time in the actual software, with real or realistic mine data.
- **Use real mining language.** This audience will spot generic IT talk immediately. Use terms like cycle time, strip ratio, grade reconciliation, OEE, and availability the way their teams do.
- **Make it interactive.** Add a poll in the first 3 minutes ("What's your biggest source of unplanned downtime?") and refer back to the results later.
- **Address technical concerns directly.** Mining buyers care about integration with existing systems (SCADA, ERP, dispatch), data quality, connectivity in remote sites, cybersecurity, and deployment (cloud vs on-premise vs hybrid). Dedicate one slide or a couple of minutes to this, perhaps in the "after" segment.
- **Quantify benefits in their terms:** cost per tonne, tonnes moved, downtime hours, safety incidents, ROI and payback period.
- **Use one character.** Name the superintendent or planner in your story and show the software through their eyes.

## Essential content checklist

- [ ] A clear one-line promise for the session ("In 30 minutes, you'll see how one mine went from X to Y")
- [ ] The business problem before any product mention
- [ ] 3 live or recorded demos, each under 4 minutes (record a backup in case of connectivity issues)
- [ ] 3 hard numbers, one per chapter, with sources
- [ ] One architecture slide showing how the three products connect
- [ ] A short customer quote or case reference
- [ ] A clear call to action: demo booking, pilot offer, or a follow-up session
- [ ] A takeaway slide with the three benefits in one line each

## Practical tips

- **Rehearse to the clock.** 30 minutes is tight. Cut a demo step before you cut the proof or the story.
- **Limit slides to about 8-10.** The software is your main visual.
- **Have two presenters if possible:** a domain expert (ex-mining engineer) and a product specialist. It builds credibility.
- **Leave 3-5 minutes for questions**, and keep a backup slide for the most likely technical questions (integration, security, pricing model, implementation time).
- **Offer a follow-up:** a leave-behind one-pager, or a booth demo for deeper dives.

## Sample opening line

> "Meet Sarah, a mine superintendent. It's Monday morning, her weekend production missed target by 8%, and she has no idea why. By the end of this session, you'll see how she finds out, fixes it, and makes sure it doesn't happen again."

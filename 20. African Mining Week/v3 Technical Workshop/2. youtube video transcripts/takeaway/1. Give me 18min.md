
# Reference Guide: Zooming Into the Moment with PAST

## Main Takeaways

- **Avoid the "helicopter view."** Summarizing ("I had a problem, it was tough, I overcame it") keeps listeners at a distance. The best storytellers zoom into the moment where something is happening.
- **Use the PAST framework:** Place, Action, Speech, Thoughts.
- **Place:** State the location early. You don't need detailed description, because listeners will picture their own version of the setting.
- **Action:** Skip the backstory and open with forward momentum. Use action verbs (I walk, I shout, I open the door) to create a movie in the listener's mind.
- **Speech:** Share the exact words spoken at the crucial moment rather than describing how someone felt. If you can't remember them, use words that could have been said.
- **Thoughts:** Share your raw, unfiltered thoughts in the moment. This is what makes a story emotional. Avoid the polished version.
- **Anchor the story with a place and date.** Stating time and location makes listeners accept it as a true story.
- **Milk the most important line.** Build anticipation before it by sharing your thoughts, then pause after it to let it land.
- **Three levels of emotional storytelling:** bad storytellers summarize, amateurs describe the emotion, and great storytellers share the exact thoughts.

## Takeaways Mapped to Examples

| Key Takeaway                                                | Real-World Example from the Text                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ----------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Avoid the helicopter view; zoom into the moment             | The war movie opening with a helicopter shot of tanks and soldiers is interesting for two seconds, until you want to be down in the trenches with the mud on their faces                               |
| **Place:** state it early, without over-describing    | "Two weeks ago I'm sitting in my apartment in Amsterdam" or "Last July I'm standing in front of the conference room," in contrast to the lavender-and-birdsong opening                                 |
| **Action:** cut context, start with forward momentum  | "I'm at the security check at the airport putting my bag in when someone calls my name, Phillip" instead of "I worked at this company for three years..."                                              |
| **Action:** use action verbs                          | The volunteer's greeting story: "She's walking towards me, and as I start walking towards her, my heart drops"                                                                                         |
| Anchor with place and date for credibility                  | Coaching on the greeting story: add a place and a date so listeners accept it as true                                                                                                                  |
| **Speech:** quote the exact words                     | "My manager was very upset" becomes "Phillip, what the hell was that? You were all over the place in that presentation"                                                                                |
| **Speech:** invent plausible words if you forget      | The "My mom was very happy" exercise, reframed as "I'm really happy that you're here, my son"                                                                                                          |
| Share what was said in the moment                           | The Luca/Lucas story, improved with "I'll see you on Sunday." / "What?"                                                                                                                                |
| Milk the key line with anticipation and pause               | The breakup story: "I was expecting some emotion..." followed by the boyfriend's "Okay, what are we going to do with the house?"                                                                       |
| **Thoughts:** share raw thoughts, not summary         | "I was nervous about the meeting" becomes "This is going to be terrible. They're going to tear my presentation apart. I'll be crying in this meeting."                                                 |
| **Thoughts:** show them in the moment, not as a recap | The date-outfit volunteer moves from "I was anxious" to "Is he going to be funny? Am I going to laugh at his jokes? Are we going to be a match?"                                                       |
| Avoid polished thoughts                                     | "This represents a supreme opportunity for my career progression" versus the real thought: "I am dead here"                                                                                            |
| All four elements work together                             | The comedian's plane story: place (airplane to Russia), action (looking out the window at the fireball), speech ("We seem to have a slight problem"), thoughts ("I'd already died and gone to heaven") |

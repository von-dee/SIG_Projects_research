# Storytelling Reference Guide: Zooming Into the Moment

## Main Takeaways

- **Zoom in, don't summarize.** The best stories avoid the "helicopter level" recap of events and take listeners into the physical moment, "into the trenches."
- **Location: say where you are.** Name the place in a few words (e.g., "conference room"). Listeners picture their own version, so extra detail isn't needed.
- **Actions: say what you're doing.** State the verbs of the moment instead of piling on background context. This creates forward momentum and signals you won't waste the audience's time.
- **Thoughts: share what you're thinking.** Give the raw, unfiltered, slightly neurotic thoughts, not polished or intellectual ones, to make the story relatable.
- **Emotions: show them, don't name them.** Describe how the feeling looks on the body or face, since a named emotion ("I was disappointed") isn't visual.
- **Dialogue: give the exact words.** Quote what people said instead of describing their reaction, and keep the lines short, punchy, and "juicy."
- **Common thread:** Each technique makes the story more visual and keeps the audience inside the scene.

| Key Takeaway                          | Real-World Example from the Text                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Zoom in, don't summarize**    | John Krasinski's customs story plays out line by line (the agent's questions, "Emily Blunt," "You married Emily Blunt?") instead of a recap like "the agent was very surprised."                                                                                 |
| **Location**                    | "Two weeks ago, I'm sitting on my couch in my living room, taking a deep breath." / "September 2019, I'm standing in front of the conference room, ready to walk inside." Sarah Willingham's story begins by walking into a meeting room.                        |
| **Actions**                     | "I open my laptop and start reading a message from my manager." / "I'm at the airport, waiting in line at security." Willingham makes the lawyer's coffee, sets it down, and asks if anyone else wants one.                                                      |
| **Thoughts**                    | Instead of "I was excited to meet my crush": "Ah, this will be so cool! Finally I can see her after all that time." Willingham thinks, "Okay, this is a moment." Avoid: "I thought this represented a supreme opportunity."                                      |
| **Emotions (show, don't tell)** | Instead of "I was relieved": "I leaned backward and let out a big sigh." Instead of "He was anxious": he tapped his pen and kept glancing at the clock. Willingham watches the color drain from the lawyer's face.                                               |
| **Dialogue**                    | Instead of "My friend was very disappointed": "Philip, what on earth was that?" Instead of "My manager was happy": "Wow, that was really the best presentation you've ever given." Avoid: "I'm very dissatisfied with the inadequate execution of that project." |
| **Techniques working together** | Willingham's story combines all five: a meeting room (location), making the coffee (actions), "this is a moment" (thoughts), the lawyer's coffee order (dialogue), and his face draining (emotion).                                                              |

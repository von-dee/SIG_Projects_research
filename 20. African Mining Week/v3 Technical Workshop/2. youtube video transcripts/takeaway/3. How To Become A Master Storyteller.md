# Storytelling Techniques: Reference Guide

## Main Takeaways

* **The Dance:** Great stories alternate between context and conflict. Conflict opens loops in the viewer's mind, and context closes them.
* **"But" and "Therefore," not "And Then":** Connect your story beats with "but" or "therefore" so each moment causes or complicates the next. "And then" feels like piling on detail and loses viewers.
* **Rhythm:** Vary sentence length and syllable patterns (short, medium, and long) to avoid monotony. Writing one sentence per line and checking for a jagged edge is a quick test.
* **Tone:** Be conversational. Write and film as if talking to one close friend, which breaks down the viewer's "am I being sold to?" barrier.
* **Direction:** Start with the ending and work backward. In short-form video, make the last line memorable and a setup for the first line, since the video loops.
* **Story Lenses:** A good topic isn't enough. Differentiate with a unique angle, and the less common the lens, the more distinctive the content.
* **The Hook (Text):** The first line should be punchy and indicative of the plot, not opaque ("wait till you see this").
* **The Hook (Visual):** Visual hooks are claimed to be ten times more effective than audio-only hooks because eyes perceive faster than ears. Show while you tell.

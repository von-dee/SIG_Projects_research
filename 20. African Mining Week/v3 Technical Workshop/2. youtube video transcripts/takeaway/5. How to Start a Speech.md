# Reference Guide: Five Ways to Open a Speech

## Main Takeaways

- **Most speakers lose the room in the first 10 seconds** by playing it safe with their name, role, and background context. Opening with surprise avoids this.
- **Open with a question.** Asking one gets the audience engaged, because their brains start searching for an answer even if they don't reply out loud.
- **Open with a surprising statement.** A statistic, fact, or bold claim that challenges what people believe creates a moment of doubt, and that doubt is attention. Say it slowly, own it, and pause before and after.
- **Open with a story.** Stories turn a talk into a movie and move people from analyzing to experiencing. Skip the background and drop the audience straight into the moment: where, when, what you're doing, what's going wrong.
- **Open with a big promise.** Audiences are asking "What's in it for me?" Tell them what they will gain, not what you will talk about.
- **Open with a visual action hook.** Do something unexpected instead of just saying something interesting. It can be simple, like a 5-second silence, holding up an object, or writing a word on a flip chart.
- **Hooks are only the beginning.** What matters most is whether people understand your ideas, which the next video in the series covers.

## Takeaways Mapped to Examples

| Key Takeaway                              | Real-World Example from the Text                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Surprise beats playing it safe            | Barack Obama opens with "I'm Michelle's husband" instead of a name, role, and context                                                                                                                       |
| Open with a question                      | "What's the scariest thing you've ever done?" and "How do you explain when others are able to achieve things that seem to defy all of the assumptions?"                                                     |
| Open with a surprising statement          | "In the next 18 minutes... four Americans that are alive will be dead through the food that they eat" and "The person to your right is a liar"                                                              |
| Deliver the statement slowly, with pauses | "400... The average office desk has 400 times more bacteria than the average toilet seat," with a pause before and after                                                                                    |
| Open with a story, starting in the moment | The event planner worried that calling the speaker a "researcher" would make people think she was boring, and the speaker whose mother recalled him pretending to have died when she let him out of the car |
| Open with a big promise                   | "Many of you are one well-delivered talk away from an absolute explosion of what it is that you want to do"                                                                                                 |
| Promise a gain, not a topic               | Replace "Today I will talk about body language" with "By the end of this talk, you'll know how to appear confident in any high-pressure situation"                                                          |
| Open with a visual action hook            | Two public speaking world champions start with unexpected, very odd physical actions on stage                                                                                                               |
| Visual hooks can be simple                | Walking on stage and saying nothing for 5 seconds, holding up an object without explaining it, or writing a word on a flip chart                                                                            |


# Customer, persona, feature and business-benefit note

## 1. What the product appears to be

The folder describes a **mining and minerals intelligence platform**, with an initial focus on **Ghana and Africa**. It is intended to bring mining locations, mineral production information and geospatial context into a shared dashboard for people making decisions about mining assets, investment and policy. The landing page names regulators, mining companies and researchers as intended users, and presents an interactive mining map, production analytics, concession visibility, exportable reports and GIS dashboards as proposed capabilities. [MVP landing page](</Users/selasie/Documents/GitHub/SIG_Projects/SIG PROJECTS/TEAM 3. World Mining and Minerals Monitor/Week-2 [MVP]/landing.html>)

A broader research section describes a potential market spanning the mining value chain, including financiers, operators, government, consultants, suppliers and researchers. That is useful as a long-term market map; it should not be read as evidence that all these groups are current customers or that every listed capability is already implemented. ## 2. Customer, client and persona: how they differ

- **Customer** is the organization that buys or funds access: for example, a mining ministry, mining company, investment firm, consulting practice or university.
- **Client** is the organization or team using the intelligence to do its work. It may be the paying customer, or a department within that organization.
- **Persona** is an individual role with a particular job to do: for example, a Senior Mining Analyst who compares mine performance or a Senior Policy Advisor preparing a policy brief.

A typical purchase may involve three people: a **budget owner** who approves the subscription, a **champion** who identifies the need and makes the case, and **daily users** who work with maps, data and reports. The folder identifies potential user groups and roles, but it does not establish who has budget authority or confirm a purchasing process.

## 3. Customer segments in the folder

The research maps eleven potential organization types:

1. **Mining producers and operators** — compare mines, production, costs and reserves; inform capital planning and board reporting.
2. **Exploration and development companies** — monitor projects and peers; identify potential partners and financing or acquisition opportunities.
3. **Institutional investors and asset managers** — assess portfolio exposure, individual-asset concentration and commodity themes.
4. **Investment banks and M&A advisors** — screen targets and prepare transaction comparisons and client pitches.
5. **Equity research analysts and brokerages** — update company coverage and compare assets across peers.
6. **Private equity, royalty and streaming investors** — find opportunities and assess the durability of asset cash flows.
7. **Government agencies, regulators and geological surveys** — track concessions and sector activity; support policy, oversight and investment-promotion work.
8. **EPCM and consulting firms** — assess project pipelines, benchmark operations and prepare client advice.
9. **Equipment suppliers and service providers** — identify mining accounts, locations and possible demand for equipment or services.
10. **Insurance, risk and legal advisory firms** — support project and counterparty diligence and risk assessment.
11. **Media, publishers and academic institutions** — research, teach and independently check mining-sector claims.

These are **prospective segments** in the research, not a verified customer list. The documents provide the strongest initial product fit for regulators, mining companies and researchers; the other segments may be expansion markets if their needed data and workflows are supported.

## 5. Features and the work they enable

The MVP landing page describes five core capabilities:

- **Interactive mining map:** gives users a geographic view of mines and their site information. Relevant to regulators, exploration teams, analysts and researchers.
- **Mineral production analytics:** presents production metrics and trends. Relevant to policy teams, investment professionals, mining companies and journalists.
- **Mining concession visibility:** helps users inspect concession and allocation information against boundaries. Particularly relevant to regulators, policy teams, exploration managers and diligence teams.
- **Exportable reports:** turns localized information into charts and CSV/PDF outputs for boards, parliaments and research workflows.
- **GIS intelligence dashboards:** brings remote-sensing and ground-telemetry information together, where those data are available.

The landing page presents these as product capabilities. It does not, on its own, verify data completeness, update frequency, source quality or that every feature is available in a production system. Those will matter to customers, especially investors and regulators who need defensible information.

## 6. Financial business benefit by customer type

The core financial case is **lower information-gathering cost and better decisions**. The same data can create different forms of value for different customers:

| Customer type                       | Where the business value may come from                                                                                                                                                          |
| ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mining companies                    | Less staff time spent compiling peer, production and location information; better-informed capital planning and reporting.                                                                      |
| Government and regulators           | More efficient monitoring and reporting; stronger evidence for policy and oversight decisions. Any increase in public revenue or avoided loss would need to be demonstrated with customer data. |
| Investors and investment firms      | Faster screening and diligence; better visibility into asset concentration and project context. Investment gains or avoided losses cannot be guaranteed.                                        |
| Consulting and advisory firms       | Less time gathering background material and more time for billable analysis; stronger evidence in proposals and client deliverables.                                                            |
| Exploration companies               | More efficient location and concession screening; potential reduction in duplicated research or poorly targeted activity.                                                                       |
| Suppliers and service providers     | More efficient account and territory research; better-timed outreach to prospective mining customers.                                                                                           |
| Researchers, media and universities | Lower research preparation effort and better-organized evidence; value is mainly productivity, coverage and credibility.                                                                        |

The preliminary research includes estimates such as reductions in manual research time and potential revenue or policy impacts. These are explicitly described in the source material as **illustrative**, not independently verified results. They should not be presented as proven ROI or used as customer promises without pilots, baselines and measured outcomes.

## 7. Suggested initial market focus

Based on the MVP landing page, the most coherent starting customer group is:

1. **Government ministries, regulators and geological or mining agencies** that need national and regional visibility.
2. **Mining companies and exploration teams** that need operational, concession and location context.
3. **Researchers and institutions** that need organized information for analysis and reporting.

Financial institutions, consultants, media and suppliers form plausible adjacent markets, but each may require additional data depth, export options, source traceability, update cadence or integrations. The folder supports treating them as expansion opportunities rather than assuming the MVP already meets all of their requirements.

## 8. What to validate before making a commercial case

To make the business benefits credible, pilot with a small number of target organizations and measure:

- Time spent today collecting and reconciling mining data.
- Whether the platform’s mine, production and concession information is accurate, current and traceable to sources.
- Which reports or decisions users actually produce with the information.
- Which features users return to and which data gaps prevent adoption.
- Whether the customer will pay, who controls the budget, and what procurement or security requirements apply.
- Before-and-after research time, reporting turnaround or diligence coverage.

**Overall assessment:** the folder presents a broad mining intelligence concept with a clear geographic and data-organization use case. Its strongest near-term promise is helping users find, understand and communicate mining-sector information more efficiently. The wider financial value—better capital allocation, stronger policy outcomes, or new revenue—depends on data quality, adoption and decisions made with the information, and should be validated rather than assumed.

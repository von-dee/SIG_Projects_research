# AR Mine Digital Twin: Market, Customer and Business Case

*Commercial strategy document · Draft v1.0 · October 2026 · Launch market: Ghana*

**Status of this document.** This is a planning document built from the product owner's brief and chat messages only. No product specification, customer data, voice-of-customer research or web research was available. Facts are labelled by evidence tier (see Section 14). Every number marked as an assumption is a placeholder to be replaced with pilot data, not a finding. No market size or competitor facts are asserted.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

**The opportunity.** The Product is an augmented-reality digital twin of a mine. It builds a 3D model of the site, keeps it updated with live data, and lets several people view the same model at once, on AR headsets or on tablet, phone and web, on site or remote. A local edge node keeps sessions running where connectivity is poor and syncs to the cloud later.

**Where to start.** Three segments are prioritised, all subject to validation:

1. **Large open-pit gold producers in Ghana.** They have the most data, systems and budget, and the weekly multi-discipline planning review is a natural fit for shared AR.
2. **Underground operators.** They face the highest spatial complexity, and edge sync directly addresses their connectivity problem.
3. **Mine-service contractors and OEM/dealer service teams.** Remote expert assist has a measurable cash baseline (expert callouts) and a shorter sales cycle.

**Most defensible value claim today.** Several people, on headsets, tablets, phones or web, can view the same site model with live data in one shared session, including at sites with poor connectivity. The benefit is less travel and less explanation. This rests on the owner's stated features; no quantified saving is defensible until pilots produce measurements.

**Key finding from the value model.** Under illustrative base assumptions, no single use case covers an annual site licence of USD 36,000. Remote assist covers about 30% of it, planning review about 27% and HSE induction about 40%. To close the gap, the company must do one of three things: price the licence nearer USD 24,000, pitch several use cases per site, or lead with high-callout OEM and dealer customers. This is the first thing the pilots must test.

**Recommended next actions**

1. Share a specification or demo so evidence tiers can be upgraded.
2. Run 10–12 discovery interviews using the guide in Section 12.
3. Pilot the weekly planning review at one open-pit site, with remote assist as a parallel OEM pilot.
4. Measure baselines (review hours, callouts, induction time) before quoting any return on investment.
5. Define the integration scope, headset and device matrix, and edge-node specification.

**Five biggest risks:** feature status is unverified; data integration may decide adoption; headsets may not suit dust, heat, glare and underground conditions; safety-alert liability if latency or accuracy fails; and unproven willingness to pay against the licence price.

## 2. Product Overview

### 2.1 Value proposition

The Product gives mine teams a shared visual and collaboration layer on top of existing mine data. Everyone sees the same live 3D picture, whether standing in the pit or sitting in a head office.

### 2.2 What is known and what is assumed

| Item | Known (stated by owner) | Planning assumption |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AR digital twin | Stated | Model built from client survey, CAD and GIS files, refreshed by drone or LiDAR |
| Alignment accuracy | Unknown | Pilot target of 0.5 m or better for overview tasks; tighter only after testing |
| Real-time streaming | Stated | 1–2 data sources per pilot site; display delay of 10 seconds or less |
| Multi-user collaboration | Stated | Up to 12 concurrent users, on-site and remote mixed |
| Devices | Headsets plus tablet, phone and web | Specific headset models to be defined |
| Hybrid deployment, edge sync | Stated | On-site edge node; local operation offline for at least one 8-hour shift |
| Other features | Not stated | Everything marked Unverified in Section 5 |

### 2.3 Scope boundaries

The Product **is** a shared visual and collaboration layer, decision support, and a viewer of live and historical site state. It **is not** a replacement for planning suites, fleet management or SCADA, a certified safety system, or a source of legal, engineering or regulatory advice, unless and until proven or certified.

### 2.4 Commercial model (owner's choices)

- **Devices:** AR headsets plus tablet, phone and web access.
- **Deployment:** hybrid, with edge sync for poor connectivity.
- **Pricing:** a pilot fee, followed by an annual site licence.

## 6. Personas and Use Cases

No voice-of-customer data exists. All persona content is inferred or assumed.

### 6.1 Mine planning engineer

- **Job to be done:** align the plan with what actually happened and show it clearly.
- **Workflow:** weekly or shift review; load model, compare plan and actual, discuss with operations; produce a revised sequence and action list; decide what to mine or move next.
- **Pain points:** scattered data, slow shared understanding, manual reports built from screenshots and slides.
- **Features used:** AR twin, multi-user, streaming; plan vs actual and fleet integration (unverified).
- **Benefits:** time saved (soft) and shared understanding (qualitative).
- **Must not claim:** replacement of the planning suite; improved recovery or grade.
- **Pilot metrics:** review duration, preparation hours, actions closed on time.

### 6.2 HSE manager

- **Job to be done:** reduce exposure and evidence compliance.
- **Workflow:** after an incident, audit or new work area, review the area in 3D, brief crews, update exclusion zones, record the outcome.
- **Pain points:** hard-to-visualise hazards, manual induction, hand-compiled evidence.
- **Features used:** AR twin, multi-user; geofence alerts, training and audit trail (unverified).
- **Benefits:** induction time (soft) and clearer hazard communication (qualitative). Risk reduction is contingent and never added to savings.
- **Must not claim:** that it prevents incidents, is a certified safety system, or satisfies regulatory inspection.
- **Pilot metrics:** induction hours, briefing completion, alert latency (if built), clarity score.

### 6.3 Maintenance planner and reliability engineer

- **Job to be done:** shorten downtime and avoid repeat failures.
- **Workflow:** on breakdown or shutdown, locate the asset, connect an expert, annotate, record the repair, decide whether to escalate.
- **Pain points:** waiting for experts, poor remote communication.
- **Features used:** multi-user, AR; remote assist and CMMS overlay (unverified).
- **Benefits:** avoided callouts (hard, if the cost is external) and shorter downtime (needs measurement).
- **Must not claim:** predictive-maintenance accuracy or integration with any named maintenance or fleet system until verified.
- **Pilot metrics:** callouts avoided, time to repair, expert wait time.

### 6.4 Geotechnical engineer

- **Job to be done:** turn monitoring data into access decisions that operations will act on.
- **Workflow:** on a movement alert or review, examine monitoring data and models in context with operations, then issue an advisory or exclusion recommendation.
- **Pain points:** monitoring data sits in separate tools; movement is hard to explain to operations in 2D.
- **Benefits:** faster shared context (qualitative).
- **Must not claim:** early warning, stability prediction, or replacement of engineering judgement.
- **Pilot metric:** time from alert to operations briefing.

### 6.5 Other personas

| Persona | Role of the Product | Must not claim | Pilot metric |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mine manager or GM | Overview and decision forum | Production uplift | Review attendance, decision latency |

## 7. Business Value Model and Pricing

All figures are **illustrative assumptions** to be replaced with pilot data. Hard savings (cash), soft savings (redeployed time) and contingent avoidance (probabilistic) are shown separately and are never summed.

### 7.1 Formulas

| Use case | Type | Formula |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Planning review | Soft | reviews per year × participants × hours saved × loaded hourly cost × adoption |
| Remote assist (maintenance or OEM) | Hard, if external | callouts per year × avoided fraction × cost per callout × adoption |
| HSE induction | Soft | inductions per year × hours saved × loaded hourly cost × adoption |
| HSE incident risk | Contingent | (baseline probability − assisted probability) × cost per event |
| Mine manager | None | No formula; there is no basis for a production-uplift claim |

### 7.2 Assumed inputs

| Variable | Low | Base | High |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reviews per year | 50 | 100 | 200 |
| Participants per review | 5 | 8 | 12 |
| Hours saved per person per review | 0.25 | 0.5 | 1.0 |
| Loaded hourly cost (USD) | 25 | 40 | 60 |
| Adoption | 30% | 60% | 80% |
| Callouts per year | 15 | 30 | 60 |
| Avoided fraction | 20% | 40% | 60% |
| Cost per callout (USD) | 800 | 1,500 | 3,000 |
| Inductions per year | 300 | 600 | 1,200 |
| Hours saved per induction | 0.5 | 1.0 | 1.5 |

### 7.3 Results per site per year (USD, rounded)

| Use case | Type | Low | Base | High |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Planning review | Soft | 470 | 9,600 | 115,200 |
| Remote assist | Hard, if external | 720 | 10,800 | 86,400 |
| HSE induction | Soft | 1,125 | 14,400 | 86,400 |

*Base-case workings: planning 100 × 8 × 0.5 × 40 × 0.6 = 9,600; remote assist 30 × 0.4 × 1,500 × 0.6 = 10,800; induction 600 × 1.0 × 40 × 0.6 = 14,400.*

**Sensitivity.** Halving both hours saved and adoption in the planning example cuts value to 2,400, a quarter of base. Halving the avoided fraction in remote assist cuts value to 5,400.

**Contingent example (not a saving).** (2.0% − 1.5%) × USD 500,000 = USD 2,500 expected value per year, using placeholder probabilities.

### 7.4 Pricing model and break-even

The commercial model is a pilot fee followed by an annual site licence. Price points are assumptions to test.

| Item | Low | Base | High |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pilot fee (8–12 weeks, one site, up to 15 users) | 10,000 | 15,000 | 25,000 |
| Annual site licence | 24,000 | 36,000 | 48,000 |
| Edge node hardware, one-off, if client-bought | 8,000 | 12,000 | 15,000 |

**Base-case coverage of the USD 36,000 licence, one use case at a time**

| Use case | Base value | Share of licence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Remote assist (hard) | 10,800 | 30% |
| Planning review (soft) | 9,600 | 27% |
| HSE induction (soft) | 14,400 | 40% |

**What this means**

1. No single base-case use case justifies the base licence, and soft savings are weaker than cash.
2. Hard value covers the licence only at high callout volumes. In the high case, remote assist yields 86,400, above even the high licence of 48,000. This points to OEM, dealer and high-callout operations as the first buyers.
3. For producers, the pitch needs several use cases per site, a licence nearer USD 24,000, or pricing that scales with sites and users.
4. Crediting the pilot fee against the first licence year would reduce buyer risk.

**What must be true:** data feeds integrate and stay fresh; the model stays accurate; users adopt the Product beyond the demo; hardware works on site; edge sync keeps sessions usable offline; and security approval is granted.

## 10. Local Context: Ghana

The points below come from general knowledge, sit outside the owner's inputs, and **require legal verification** before use.

- **Licensing:** the Minerals and Mining Act, 2006 (Act 703) governs mineral rights, and the Minerals Commission administers licences and policy.
- **Inspection and safety:** mine health-and-safety rules and the Inspectorate function sit apart from licensing. Check current regulations before claiming any compliance use.
- **Environmental permitting:** confirm the role of the Environmental Protection Agency.
- **Data protection:** the Data Protection Act, 2012 (Act 843) may apply to worker, position and identity data. Hybrid deployment helps in residency discussions.
- **Cybersecurity:** check current obligations for critical operations under Ghana's cybersecurity legislation.
- **Illegal small-scale mining (galamsey):** it affects concession security and may create demand for boundary monitoring, but this is not a stated Product capability.
- **Community and land issues:** these affect operations and site access.
- **Infrastructure:** unreliable power and connectivity, and hardware import costs, constrain AR. The edge node and multi-device access are the mitigations, and the edge specification should include a power-backup requirement.
- **Regional expansion:** consider after Ghana pilots; nothing has been gathered yet.

### 14.5 Glossary

**AR:** augmented reality. **ASM:** artisanal and small-scale mining. **CMMS:** computerised maintenance management system. **Concession:** a licensed mining area. **Digital twin:** a live digital model of a physical asset. **Edge node:** a local server at the mine that keeps the system running and syncs to the cloud. **EITI:** Extractive Industries Transparency Initiative. **FMS:** fleet management system. **Galamsey:** Ghanaian term for illegal small-scale mining. **OT:** operational technology. **SCADA:** supervisory control and data acquisition.

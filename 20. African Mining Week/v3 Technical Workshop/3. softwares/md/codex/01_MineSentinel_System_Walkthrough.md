# MineSentinel: System Walkthrough

## Purpose in the connected mine story

MineSentinel is the first stop: it helps a user establish the mining landscape around a place, asset, or decision. The product notes describe an interactive mining map, production analytics, concession visibility, GIS intelligence dashboards, and exportable reports. Use it to orient the audience and form the next questions for investigation.

> **Use responsibly:** The notes do not establish data completeness, source authority, or update frequency. Treat map and dashboard findings as screening context. Confirm important facts with authoritative records before making legal, investment, or operational decisions.

## 1. Interactive mining map

**User goal:** Find a mine, project, district, or area of interest and understand its geographic context.

### Steps

1. Open the mining map and begin at the country or regional view.
2. Search for a mine, district, concession, or project name if search is available; otherwise zoom and pan to the area.
3. Turn on the relevant mine and site layers.
4. Select a map feature to inspect the information available for it.
5. Compare the selected location with nearby assets and visible geographic context.
6. Note the location, the information shown, its date or source if displayed, and any question that needs follow-up.

**Expected result:** A map view that locates the area of interest and presents the available nearby mining context.

**Benefit:** Gives team members a shared starting point and can reduce time spent assembling map screenshots and location notes by hand.

**Check before presenting:** Confirm the map layer's coverage, data date, and source. A mapped feature does not by itself confirm current operating status or legal rights.

## 2. Concession visibility

**User goal:** Inspect how a project location relates to concession or allocation boundaries shown in the system.

### Steps

1. Open the concession or allocation layer.
2. Find the area using a concession identifier, map navigation, or project coordinates.
3. Display the project location or boundary alongside the concession layer.
4. Inspect apparent overlaps, gaps, adjacent boundaries, or missing coverage.
5. Record the relevant map view and the questions it raises.
6. Confirm consequential boundary or status questions against the responsible official registry.

**Expected result:** A spatial comparison that highlights relationships and possible issues for a reviewer to check.

**Benefit:** Makes boundary context easier to communicate and may reveal diligence questions before the team commits to more expensive work.

**Check before presenting:** Treat the view as a screening aid. Confirm boundary currency, legal status, and authoritative interpretation with official sources.

## 3. Mineral production analytics

**User goal:** Review available production information for a mine, commodity, or reporting period.

### Steps

1. Open the production analytics view.
2. Select the mine, commodity, region, or period supported by the interface.
3. Review the displayed metric names, units, dates, and any available source notes.
4. Compare the available periods or assets using consistent measures.
5. Identify missing periods, definition differences, or figures that need reconciliation.
6. Carry the chart or observation into the next assessment with its date and source context.

**Expected result:** A chart, table, or dashboard view of the production information available for the selected scope.

**Benefit:** Reduces repeated manual compilation and gives analysts and decision makers a more consistent starting point for comparison.

**Check before presenting:** Production analytics are not a commodity-price forecast, a reserve estimate, or proof that source figures are complete. Confirm definitions and source coverage.

## 4. GIS intelligence dashboard

**User goal:** Examine available geospatial context and, where connected, remote-sensing or ground-telemetry information.

### Steps

1. Open the GIS intelligence dashboard for the selected region or asset.
2. Review which spatial layers and data feeds are actually available in the current deployment.
3. Enable the layers relevant to the question, such as site locations, concession context, or available remote-sensing and telemetry inputs.
4. Compare the layers and identify locations or changes that deserve closer review.
5. Record the layer names, source dates, and interpretation limits.
6. Assign any follow-up to a qualified reviewer or field team.

**Expected result:** A consolidated spatial view showing the available evidence relevant to the selected question.

**Benefit:** Helps teams interpret spatial evidence together and prioritize where to investigate instead of switching between disconnected map products.

**Check before presenting:** Remote-sensing and ground-telemetry availability depends on data access and integrations. Do not describe the dashboard as continuous monitoring unless that capability is confirmed.

## 5. Exportable reports

**User goal:** Turn a selected map or production view into a briefing or data handoff.

### Steps

1. Select the area, assets, and indicators that answer the reporting question.
2. Check the selected dates, metric definitions, labels, and source context.
3. Generate the export formats available in the deployed version, such as PDF, CSV, or chart output.
4. Review the exported file for missing fields, unclear units, and stale or incomplete data.
5. Add a short explanation of what the view shows and what remains unverified.
6. Share the reviewed output with the next decision maker or analyst.

**Expected result:** A shareable report or data file containing the selected map, chart, or mining information.

**Benefit:** Reduces repetitive formatting work and gives teams a more consistent way to hand information from research to review.

**Check before presenting:** Export formats and report maturity should be confirmed in the current build. An export does not improve the quality of incomplete source data.

## Suggested MineSentinel demo sequence

1. Start with the national or regional mining map.
2. Zoom to the fictional project area and show nearby mining context.
3. Compare the location with the concession layer.
4. Open an available production view to add sector context.
5. Show a GIS layer or dashboard relevant to the question, if available.
6. Finish with a reviewed export and state the next question for MineAsk.

**Transition:** MineSentinel shows where the opportunity sits. MineAsk helps assemble and review the evidence around that specific place.

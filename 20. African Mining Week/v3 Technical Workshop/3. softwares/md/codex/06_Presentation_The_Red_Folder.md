# Presentation 2: The Red Folder

## The investment gate under pressure

**Session:** African Mining Week, Technical Track  
**Panel theme:** The Intelligent Mine: Connecting AI Across the Full Value Chain, From First Drill Hole to Final Rehabilitation  
**Format:** Solo presentation, 30 minutes, visual aid, no Q&A  
**Story lens:** An investment committee is minutes away from a decision. The technical opportunity looks promising, but the evidence is split across maps, spreadsheets, title notes, and environment files.

> **Scenario note:** The characters, project, date, quotations, and events are fictional and illustrative. Do not present this as a real investment or permitting case.

## Story spine

Kojo, an analyst, carries a red folder into the committee room. MineSentinel gives him sector context. MineAsk builds a location-specific, evidence-aware assessment. TerraPulse identifies what technical evidence merits field validation. MyMine lets the operating team discuss site context together. The committee does not get an automated yes/no; it gets a better-structured decision and a list of what must be verified next.

**Central promise:** Connected intelligence improves the quality and traceability of a decision, not the certainty of an outcome.

## Speaker walkthrough

### 0:00-3:00 — Open at the door

**Visual:** Close-up of a red folder beside a meeting-room door. A clock reads 8:57. Inside the folder: an asset map, title note, access table, and an environmental screenshot.

**Say:**

> “In this fictional case, it is 8:57 on a Tuesday morning. Kojo stands outside an investment committee room with a red folder under his arm.
>
> The chair opens the door and asks, ‘Can we make a decision on this asset today?’
>
> Kojo looks at the folder. The map is current, but the concession note is older. The access road is marked in a separate file. One environmental layer has no date on it.
>
> His first thought is not ‘the model says yes.’ It is, ‘Which part of this story can I defend, and what could change the decision?’
>
> That is what an intelligent system should help with: assemble the evidence, reveal the gaps, and get it to the right reviewer before money and time are committed.”

Pause after “what could change the decision?” Show the separated papers. Then move into the walkthrough.

### 3:00-6:00 — MineSentinel: locate the asset in its sector

**Visual:** The folder's map becomes MineSentinel's regional mining map.

**Say:**

> “Kojo first asks a basic question: where does this asset sit in the mining landscape? MineSentinel gives him a geographic starting point, concession visibility, and available production context.”

**Steps:**

1. Open the mining map and find the district or project.
2. Inspect nearby mines and the concession layer available in the product.
3. Open production analytics for the relevant asset or commodity where data exists.
4. Note dates, missing periods, source uncertainty, and questions that require confirmation.
5. Prepare a map or report export only if supported in the deployed version.

**Expected result:** A sector-context view that identifies where the asset sits and what should be checked more deeply.

**Benefit:** Less time assembling introductory context; a more consistent briefing for the committee.

**Caveat:** “The map is not a title opinion, and the production view is not a forecast. Kojo still confirms high-stakes claims with authoritative sources.”

**Bridge:** Sector context raises questions, **but** it does not resolve the asset's evidence. **Therefore**, Kojo opens MineAsk.

### 6:00-12:00 — MineAsk: make uncertainty visible

**Visual:** The project boundary sits beside three columns: **Source-backed / Interpretation / Verify**. An evidence card expands on click.

**Say:**

> “Kojo asks one focused question: ‘What location, title, access, and environmental issues could materially change this decision?’
>
> MineAsk structures available evidence around the site. The important screen is not only the answer. It is the line between what is sourced, what is an interpretation, and what still needs a person to confirm.”

**Steps:**

1. Define the concession or area using a supported identifier, map selection, coordinates, or boundary input.
2. Select relevant available context: cadastre/title, roads, terrain, water, protected areas, settlements, geology, or satellite data.
3. Ask a narrow question connected to the committee's decision.
4. Inspect source references and dates where available.
5. Separate facts, interpretation, and official or field checks.
6. Review scorecard factors rather than accepting a single combined score.
7. Export or share a dossier only when the functionality is confirmed.

**Expected result:** A first-pass assessment that shows evidence, decision-relevant risk questions, and outstanding verification.

**Benefit:** Earlier identification of potential deal-breakers; less duplicated desk research; a clearer handoff to legal, environment, geology, and operations.

**Caveat:** “The product notes describe monitoring and alerts as partial or planned. This screen is a review workflow; it does not guarantee regulatory compliance or replace counsel.”

**Let the key line land:** “A score is useful only when we can inspect what produced it.” Pause, open one source.

### 12:00-17:00 — TerraPulse: target the next diligence dollar

**Visual:** A geological evidence map with a ranked shortlist. Use fictional labels and no invented discovery percentages.

**Say:**

> “The committee now knows what it needs to investigate. The technical team asks a different question: where should the next field effort go?”

**Steps:**

1. Open a TerraPulse exploration project with the area and decision defined.
2. Inspect supported geophysical, geochemical, satellite, and structural inputs.
3. Check coordinate systems, dates, units, and coverage before interpreting patterns.
4. Compare layers and review available anomaly or prospectivity ranking.
5. Open the evidence matrix to see support, conflict, and missing information.
6. Have the geologist select follow-up targets for validation.
7. Prepare target exports for field use only after checking format and coordinate fidelity.

**Expected result:** A prioritized target discussion with an explanation and an actionable validation plan.

**Benefit:** Directs scarce survey and field effort toward better-supported questions; can reduce wasted preparation and lower the risk of advancing weak targets.

**Caveat:** TerraPulse is proposed decision support. It does not replace field work, formal 3D modeling, competent-person review, or resource/reserve reporting.

**Bridge:** A target ranking helps set the next question, **but** it cannot erase uncertainty. **Therefore**, Kojo puts the uncertainty itself on the committee screen.

### 17:00-20:00 — Show what the system cannot prove

**Visual:** An uncertainty ledger with three rows: **Data gap / Human review / Decision affected**.

**Say:**

> “Here is the part of the presentation where we do not hide the gaps. One boundary needs official confirmation. One access assumption needs a site visit. One geological interpretation needs another technical review.
>
> The system has not failed because it exposed an unknown. It has helped the team see the unknown before mistaking it for certainty.”

Walk through each fictional open item and name its owner. State that evidence freshness, source lineage, model accuracy, and integration quality need validation. This section earns trust by making limitations actionable.

### 20:00-25:00 — MyMine: put the committee question into site context

**Visual:** The committee map transitions to a shared AR mine twin with a planner and remote reviewer. Mark illustrative objects clearly.

**Say:**

> “The investment gate passes to the operating team. Now planners, geotechnical staff, HSE, and maintenance need to talk about the same site.
>
> MyMine's shared AR twin gives the team a common three-dimensional view. The planner can point to a location; a remote reviewer can follow the same discussion.”

**Steps:**

1. Open the mine twin and identify its model source and date.
2. Orient the group using a recognizable site feature.
3. Join a shared session on confirmed headset, tablet, phone, or web clients.
4. Navigate to one planning issue and compare the views across participants.
5. Show a streamed data overlay only if the feed is integrated, timestamped, and validated.
6. Record decisions and actions in the team's approved system of record.

**Expected result:** A shared spatial review that helps the team agree what location and issue it is discussing.

**Benefit:** Less plan-view confusion and potentially more productive remote collaboration; use a pilot to measure meeting duration and clarity.

**Caveat:** Product notes identify twin, streaming, multi-user, device, and edge functions as owner-stated, with implementation details to confirm. MyMine is not a certified safety system or mine-planning replacement.

### 25:00-28:00 — Draw the line between today's products and tomorrow's stack

**Visual:** Solid outline: MineSentinel, MineAsk, TerraPulse, MyMine. Dotted outline: regulatory NLP, sentiment monitoring, GPS/telematics oversight, traceability, plant optimization, logistics automation, ESG sensing, and closure models.

**Say:**

> “The wider technical vision includes real-time regulatory monitoring, equipment telematics, satellite and drone oversight, traceability, plant optimization, logistics, ESG sensing, and closure planning. These systems need data agreements, connectors, governance, and field validation.
>
> I am showing them as integration opportunities, not as capabilities these four products already prove. That distinction is part of how we earn trust.”

### 28:00-30:00 — Close with a decision gate

**Visual:** Five questions appear: **What decision? What evidence? What gap? Who owns review? What will we measure?**

**Say:**

> “The committee does not need an AI label. It needs a better decision path. Before you fund the next tool, ask: which decision changes, what evidence supports it, what remains unknown, who reviews the output, and how will you measure value?
>
> Earlier evidence matters when it changes a decision and stays reviewable.”

Stop. No Q&A is scheduled.

## Presenter notes

- This is not a sales pitch. Lead the room through a consequential decision and show why each feature matters.
- Build confidence across three dimensions: the usefulness of the workflow, the speaker's command of the evidence, and the organization's ability to support the tool. Do not ask the audience to trust what has not been validated.
- If challenged, return to the evidence and limitation rather than looping through a rehearsed rebuttal.
- Keep pace conversational. Alternate context and conflict; use “but” and “therefore” to make each transition causal.
- Keep all scenario details and quotes explicitly illustrative.

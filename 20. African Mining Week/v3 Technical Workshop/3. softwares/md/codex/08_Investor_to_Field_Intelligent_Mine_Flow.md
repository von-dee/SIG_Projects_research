# Investor-to-Field Walkthrough: Choosing and Building a Gold Mine

## One connected story, from country choice to field execution

**Session:** African Mining Week, Technical Track  
**Format:** Solo presentation, 30 minutes, visual aid, no Q&A  
**Product order:** MineSentinel → MineAsk → TerraPulse → MyMine AR Mine Digital Twin

> **Scenario note:** Amina, the investor, and the proposed project are fictional. Ghana, Burkina Faso, South Africa, and other African gold-producing countries are candidate jurisdictions in the scenario, not a claim that one is currently the best investment destination. Country performance, law, security, commodity economics, and data availability must be verified with current sources before presenting a real recommendation.

## The storyline

Amina is considering where to establish a gold mine in Africa. Her team will not begin by choosing a drill target. First, it compares countries and regions against the investment criteria. Then it examines a specific location and its regulatory and operating context. Only after selecting a location does the technical team rank geological targets. Finally, the field team uses a shared AR mine twin to review the site and coordinate work using the data and integrations actually available.

The story moves from **country → location → target → field work**. Each product answers a different question, and each handoff carries forward the evidence, uncertainty, and next decision.

**Audience takeaway:** “Best place” is not a universal score. It is the place that best fits the investor's stated criteria, based on comparable evidence that has been checked and reviewed.

## The four decisions

| Stage | Decision question | Software | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Country and region | Where should we investigate first? | MineSentinel | Comparable country/region shortlist and screening brief |
| 2. Specific location | What location is viable, and what must be confirmed before commitment? | MineAsk | Site dossier, regulatory questions, constraints, and verification plan |
| 3. Geological target | Where should exploration effort focus within the selected area? | TerraPulse | Ranked targets, rationale, and field-validation plan |
| 4. Field execution | How do site and remote teams review the mine together? | MyMine AR Mine Digital Twin | Shared spatial review, with confirmed streams and actions |

## 2:00-9:00 — MineSentinel: select the country and region to investigate

### Amina's question

“Among the candidate countries, which places merit deeper due diligence for this gold-mine investment?”

### Step-by-step MineSentinel workflow

1. **Set the investment lens.** Amina's team agrees the screening criteria before looking at the map: gold-mining context, known activity, available production information, concession context, location, infrastructure or GIS evidence where available, and the team's own commercial and risk criteria.
2. **Open the continental or regional view.** The analyst starts with MineSentinel's interactive mining map and checks which countries and datasets are actually represented.
3. **Compare candidate countries consistently.** The analyst reviews the same types of available information for Ghana, Burkina Faso, South Africa, and any other shortlisted countries. If coverage or definitions differ, the analyst records that gap instead of treating unlike data as directly comparable.
4. **Inspect country-level production context.** Where the product provides production analytics, the analyst checks the commodity, time period, units, source, and missing periods. The team uses this to understand the available context, not as a price forecast or mine-level reserve estimate.
5. **Drill from country to region.** Use the map to inspect mining locations, concessions, and nearby context. Identify regions to investigate further, not a final parcel to acquire.
6. **Review GIS intelligence where feeds are present.** Inspect available remote-sensing or ground-telemetry information only when those feeds are connected and their dates and coverage are known.
7. **Export the screening brief.** Use available report or data exports to document the country/region shortlist, criteria, evidence dates, and open questions.
8. **Make a gated shortlist.** Amina chooses one or more regions for deeper investigation. The system supports the comparison; the investment team owns the decision.

### Expected result

A comparative, map-based shortlist of countries or regions that appear to merit further diligence, plus an explicit record of criteria, available evidence, source dates, and coverage gaps.

### Benefit

- Replaces repeated manual assembly of basic mining, production, concession, and geographic context.
- Helps the team apply a consistent first-pass screen across multiple jurisdictions.
- Makes gaps visible before expensive legal, geological, and field diligence begins.
- Gives the investment committee a clearer explanation of why a region advanced.

### What MineSentinel does not decide

MineSentinel's notes describe mapping, production analytics, concession visibility, GIS dashboards, and exportable reports. They do not establish complete, current, pan-African coverage or a universal country-ranking model. MineSentinel should be presented as a way to compare available context. “Best country” remains a criteria-based human decision supported by verified data.

**Handoff:** “MineSentinel tells Amina where to look more closely. It does not tell her that a specific concession is legally or operationally ready. She now selects a location for deeper assessment.”

## 9:00-16:00 — MineAsk: assess the selected location and its pathway

### Amina's question

“For the selected region, what does the evidence say about this specific location, and what needs official, legal, or field confirmation before we proceed?”

### Step-by-step MineAsk workflow

1. **Define the location.** Start an assessment using a supported concession or permit identifier, coordinates, map pin, district, route, community, or uploaded boundary.
2. **Check the geometry.** Confirm that the selected boundary corresponds to the intended property and that the map location is sensible. Record any mismatch.
3. **Assemble the relevant evidence.** Select available title/cadastre, geology, terrain, water, roads, infrastructure, settlements, protected areas, satellite, weather, and market context as relevant to the decision.
4. **Ask focused questions.** Examples: “What licensing steps should counsel confirm for this jurisdiction?” “What access or environmental issues should we investigate at this location?” “What source-backed information is available about the concession?”
5. **Inspect the evidence, not only the answer.** Open referenced records and maps. Label findings as sourced fact, system interpretation, or item to verify. Check dates and provenance when available.
6. **Review the site-screening factors.** Use available scorecards or screening dimensions for geological, legal, access, ESG, and operational context. Inspect the factor-level basis rather than accepting a single score.
7. **Build the regulatory pathway checklist.** Use available jurisdiction rules and process context to assemble questions and likely steps. For Ghana, legal references must be current and confirmed by official sources and local counsel. Apply the same discipline to Burkina Faso, South Africa, or any other jurisdiction.
8. **Map logistics and constraints.** Review available routes, roads, ports, power, water, processing access, settlements, and environmental or land context. Identify dependencies that could change project scope or economics.
9. **Assign verification.** Send title questions to counsel or the official registry, environmental questions to the responsible specialist, and access questions to the field team.
10. **Prepare the location dossier.** Export or share a reviewable pack if the deployed version supports it. Preserve evidence, open questions, owner, and status.

### Expected result

A site-specific dossier that organizes available information about the location, highlights likely regulatory and operational questions, compares the location against agreed criteria, and assigns outstanding checks to accountable reviewers.

### Benefit

- Reduces the time spent searching across disconnected sources.
- Can surface a potential deal-breaker before the team spends heavily on surveys or mobilization.
- Gives investors, counsel, geologists, environmental staff, and operations a common evidence pack.
- Converts a vague “is this a good location?” discussion into specific checks and decisions.

### What MineAsk does not decide

MineAsk's notes describe query-and-assess workflows and a mix of joined data, map overlays, scorecards, AI-assisted answers, and regulatory context. Some report, monitoring, and alert functions are partial or planned. Do not claim it grants permits, guarantees compliance, gives legal advice, or monitors every regulatory change continuously. “Best location” still depends on verified official information, current law, commercial assumptions, and specialist judgment.

**Handoff:** “Amina has a location worth investigating and a checklist of what is known and unknown. The technical team can now ask the narrower geology question: where, inside this area, should field effort focus?”

## 16:00-22:00 — TerraPulse: rank exploration targets inside the selected area

### Amina's question

“Within the location under review, which geological targets deserve field validation first?”

### Step-by-step TerraPulse workflow

1. **Create the exploration project.** Import or define the selected location boundary and state the exploration decision: target screening, survey follow-up, sampling, or a drilling discussion.
2. **Inventory the data.** Add supported satellite, geophysical, geochemical, and structural inputs. Confirm dates, coordinate systems, units, coverage, and source quality.
3. **Harmonize and inspect.** Align supported layers to the project area. Review missing cells, offsets, inconsistent units, and transformations before interpretation.
4. **Compare evidence spatially.** Inspect layers individually and in relevant combinations in the unified map. Mark convergence, conflict, and areas where evidence is absent.
5. **Run target or anomaly ranking.** Use the available prospectivity or anomaly workflow and model settings. Review the relative ranking as a prioritization aid, not a promise of discovery.
6. **Open the explanation.** For each leading target, inspect its evidence matrix: supporting factors, counter-evidence, assumptions, missing data, and confidence limitations.
7. **Apply geologist review.** Let the qualified geologist challenge the ranking against local geological knowledge and decide which targets merit field validation.
8. **Prepare the field plan.** Create target summaries, maps, and coordinate exports where supported. Check coordinate reference, units, and export fidelity before use in the field.
9. **Return field observations.** Add validated observations and results back to the project so the team can reassess its interpretation and ranking.

### Expected result

A technically reviewed target shortlist, an explanation of why each target was advanced or deferred, and a practical field-validation plan.

### Benefit

- Reduces manual data preparation and layer-by-layer comparison work.
- Helps allocate scarce sampling, survey, and drilling budgets toward better-supported questions.
- Makes the evidence for target selection inspectable by technical reviewers and investors.
- Establishes a learning loop: new field information can challenge and refine the next decision.

### What TerraPulse does not decide

TerraPulse supports exploration targeting; it does not prove mineralization, replace ground truth, generate a bankable resource automatically, or replace formal 3D modeling and competent-person reporting. Ranking quality depends on input coverage, data quality, model configuration, and geological review.

**Handoff:** “The team has selected targets and a validation plan. When work begins on site, the question changes: how will field and remote teams share a common view of the mine and coordinate around the work?”

## 22:00-28:00 — MyMine AR Mine Digital Twin: coordinate field work in shared spatial context

### Amina's question

“How can site and remote teams review the same mine context and keep field discussions coordinated?”

### Step-by-step MyMine workflow

1. **Load the available mine twin.** Open the AR 3D model for the project. Identify its source files, survey basis, coverage, and update date where available.
2. **Orient the participants.** Use a recognizable pit, access route, facility, or other landmark. State the model's known alignment and accuracy limits.
3. **Start a shared review.** Invite the planning, geology, HSE, maintenance, and remote reviewers who need the view.
4. **Join on supported devices.** Connect from headset, tablet, phone, or web only where the deployed version supports the device and workflow. Confirm that participants see the same location and model state.
5. **Bring in confirmed live data.** If an operational feed is integrated, show its source, timestamp, units, and latency. Place it in context only after confirming data meaning and reliability.
6. **Review the field question together.** Navigate to the area or work location and discuss the observation, access, or coordination issue. Use the shared spatial view to clarify what everyone is referring to.
7. **Record actions in the system of record.** Capture follow-up owner, next check, and date using the mine's approved planning or operational tools. MyMine is the visual collaboration layer unless a specific integration has been confirmed.
8. **Use edge sync in a controlled test.** If the site has poor connectivity, verify the edge node, local cache, offline duration, and reconnection behavior in a pilot before relying on it for work.
9. **Close the loop.** Bring verified field observations into the appropriate source system and update the model or project only through the agreed process.

### Expected result

A cross-functional team can review a common 3D site context across confirmed devices, discuss a field or planning question, and leave with clear actions and system-of-record references.

### Benefit

- Reduces confusion caused by different people reviewing different plans or locations.
- Can make remote specialist participation more practical.
- Helps communicate spatial relationships during a field or planning review.
- Provides a testable basis for measuring meeting clarity, preparation time, and coordination.

### What MyMine does not decide

The source document describes the AR twin, real-time streaming, multi-user collaboration, multi-device access, and hybrid edge sync as owner-stated capabilities, with specifications and deployment validation still needed. Plan-versus-actual, grade, fleet/SCADA, geofence, ground movement, work orders, remote assist, compliance records, and safety overlays are listed as unverified. Do not promise continuous field monitoring, predictive maintenance, safety outcomes, or operational control unless an integration is implemented and validated.

## Presenter guardrails

- Do not announce Ghana, Burkina Faso, South Africa, or another country as “best” without a current, transparent, comparable analysis.
- MineSentinel's source note supports mapping, production analytics, concession visibility, GIS dashboards, and exports; verify pan-African coverage and source dates before comparing jurisdictions.
- MineAsk's source material supports site assessment and regulatory context, but maturity varies; verify current law with official sources and local counsel.
- TerraPulse ranks evidence for review; do not promise discovery or present an exploration ranking as a resource/reserve statement.
- MyMine's core collaboration features are owner-stated; verify the actual demo, feeds, devices, alignment, latency, and edge behavior.
- Use each feature to answer a decision question. Explain the expected result and benefit before showing detailed controls.
- Label fictional characters, dialogue, rankings, and screen values as illustrative. Do not invent customer outcomes or ROI.

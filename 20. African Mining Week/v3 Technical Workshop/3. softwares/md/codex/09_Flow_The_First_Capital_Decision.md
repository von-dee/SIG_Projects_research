# Flow 1: The First Capital Decision

## From country screen to first field program

**Story lens:** A founder-investor has enough capital for one serious first-phase exploration program, but several African jurisdictions are in play. Follow one continuous decision from country comparison to the field team's shared site review.

**Fictional scenario:** Mariam, an investment director, is evaluating a hypothetical gold project. Ghana, Burkina Faso, South Africa, and other candidate countries are options to investigate, not pre-ranked destinations. All dialogue, project details, and outputs below are illustrative.

**Run time:** 30 minutes | **Format:** Solo technical presentation, visual aid, no Q&A

## Story question

“Where should we investigate first, what must we verify before committing, and how do we carry the evidence from the investment desk to the field?”

## Audience promise and decision gates

By the end, the audience will have seen one investor question travel through four distinct decisions. Each product ends with an artifact that becomes the next step's input:

1. **MineSentinel:** a comparable country/region shortlist, with data-coverage gaps visible.
2. **MineAsk:** a selected-location dossier, regulatory questions, and named verification actions.
3. **TerraPulse:** a geologist-reviewed target shortlist and field-validation plan.
4. **MyMine:** a shared site review and field actions, using only confirmed model and data connections.

The turning point is the move from “Which country looks best?” to “What evidence would make us commit to this specific location?” The payoff is not an automatic investment recommendation. It is a traceable path from screening to accountable field work.

## Visual sequence and demo discipline

Keep one fictional asset and one visual language throughout. Start with a country map, progressively zoom to a location, then a geological target, then the site twin. Use a consistent marker for the hypothetical opportunity and label illustrative data clearly.

| Slide | What the audience sees | What to demonstrate |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Three country folders and a decision clock | No interface; let the opening question land |
| 2 | Regional view and candidate locations | MineSentinel map and available context |
| 3 | Comparable country/region context | MineSentinel production, concession, or GIS views only where data exists |
| 4 | Site boundary with sourced / interpreted / verify columns | MineAsk location selection and one evidence-backed question |
| 5 | Regulatory, access, and ESG checklist | Available MineAsk overlays and human verification handoff |
| 6 | Survey layers and ranked targets | TerraPulse inputs, ranking, evidence matrix, and field actions |
| 7 | One target selected with rationale | Human decision gate and target handoff |
| 8 | Shared 3D site view | MyMine twin and confirmed device session, or a labeled concept visual |
| 9 | Four linked product cards plus future integrations | Separate product scope from broader stack aspirations |
| 10 | Pilot card | End on a practical evaluation framework, then stop |

**Demo fallback:** Prepare screenshots from the actual build. If the live product, data connection, export, or device session is unavailable, narrate the intended workflow over a labeled concept visual. Do not present a mock result as actual product output.

## The one-minute message to repeat

“MineSentinel helps compare the landscape. MineAsk helps test a location. TerraPulse helps prioritize geological work. MyMine helps teams share the site view. Each output has a human owner, and each handoff carries its evidence and uncertainty.”

Repeat a shorter version at the MyMine transition and the close. This gives the audience a clear mental model without implying one product automatically performs the whole chain.

## 0-3 min — Open inside the allocation decision

**Visual:** A wall map with three candidate country folders, a budget envelope, and a field-season calendar.

**Say:**

> “At 8:15 on Monday morning, Mariam moves three folders across the table. Ghana. Burkina Faso. South Africa. Her technical director asks, ‘Which one gets the first field budget?’
>
> Mariam looks at the spreadsheets. They use different dates, different definitions, and different map scales. She thinks, ‘If we compare unlike evidence, the neatest table could win over the best opportunity.’
>
> So she does not start with a drill target. She starts by making the country decision comparable.”

Explain that the scenario is fictional. A country's ranking is not stated in advance; the workflow is designed to compare the team's criteria against available, verified information.

## 3-9 min — MineSentinel: compare the landscape

### User decision

Which countries or regions meet the investor's initial screening criteria and deserve deeper diligence?

### Feature walkthrough

1. **Set the criteria before opening the map.** Mariam's team defines the factors it needs to compare: gold-mining context, production information, visible concessions, asset locations, available GIS evidence, access context, and project-specific commercial thresholds.
2. **Open the interactive mining map.** Start at the national or regional view. Check which countries and records are actually represented.
3. **Compare available asset and concession context.** Turn on relevant map layers for Ghana, Burkina Faso, South Africa, and any additional candidates. Inspect locations and geographic relationships.
4. **Review production analytics.** Select the same commodity and comparable periods where supported. Read units, source, update date, and gaps before comparing charts.
5. **Inspect GIS intelligence.** Add available remote-sensing or ground-telemetry context only where a verified feed exists. Record the layer's date and coverage.
6. **Use report exports for the committee.** Create a map/chart/data export if available, and label the criteria, data dates, and limitations.
7. **Make a regional shortlist.** The investment team selects one or more regions for deeper review; the platform does not make an unconditional “best country” claim.

**Expected result:** A consistent first-pass comparison and a shortlist of regions with the reasons for advancement and the missing evidence listed.

**Benefit:** Faster sector orientation, less repeated manual compilation, and earlier visibility into coverage gaps before the team spends on site diligence.

**Transition:** “Mariam now has a region worth testing. A region is not a mine site. She needs to assess a precise location and its legal, environmental, and operating context.”

## 9-16 min — MineAsk: turn a region into a reviewable location case

### User decision

What can the team establish about this location, and what must be checked with counsel, authorities, or the field team?

### Feature walkthrough

1. **Select the concession or site.** Define it with an available identifier, coordinates, map pin, district, route, community, or uploaded boundary. Confirm that the geometry matches the intended site.
2. **Join relevant evidence.** Bring together available cadastre/title, geology, terrain, water, roads, infrastructure, settlements, protected-area, satellite, weather, and market context.
3. **Use map studio and overlays.** Compare the location against boundaries, access routes, environmental and community context, and other relevant spatial factors.
4. **Ask a focused AI-assisted question.** For example: “What licensing steps and location constraints should we verify before field mobilization?” Review the answer and open the underlying sources for consequential claims.
5. **Review screening or scorecard factors.** Inspect geological, legal, access, ESG, and operational dimensions individually. Identify hard stops, uncertainties, and conflicting evidence rather than relying only on a summary score.
6. **Build the regulatory checklist.** Use available jurisdiction context to list process steps and questions. For Ghana or any other country, confirm current law and requirements through official sources and local counsel.
7. **Check logistics and land/community exposure.** Review available road, haulage, port, power, water, protected-area, vegetation, tailings, settlement, and incident context relevant to the site.
8. **Separate evidence from inference.** Mark source-backed facts, system interpretation, and items needing legal or field verification. Preserve source lineage/confidence where available.
9. **Create and hand off the dossier.** Export/share an assessment if supported. Assign open items to named reviewers. Treat scheduled monitoring and alerts as roadmap/partial unless the live version is confirmed.

**Expected result:** A location dossier with a regulatory question list, site-screening evidence, logistics and ESG context, and owned verification tasks.

**Benefit:** Earlier issue discovery, less duplicate research, a more consistent diligence process, and a clearer decision trail for the investment committee.

**Transition:** “The location passes the team's initial gate, subject to verification. Now the geologists can spend effort within the location instead of across the whole region.”

## 16-22 min — TerraPulse: rank geological targets

### User decision

Which areas inside the selected location have enough combined evidence to justify field validation first?

### Feature walkthrough

1. **Create the exploration project.** Set the boundary, commodity/mineral-system context, and the decision to support.
2. **Ingest supported data.** Add satellite/geospatial files, geophysical grids, geochemistry tables, and structural information available to the team.
3. **Harmonize and quality-check inputs.** Confirm coordinate systems, units, dates, coverage, missing cells, offsets, and transformations.
4. **Inspect unified map layers.** Toggle and compare inputs, looking for convergent, contradictory, or missing evidence.
5. **Run available prospectivity/anomaly ranking.** Review settings and compare target candidates. Treat rank as a prioritization signal, not a discovery probability.
6. **Open explainability/evidence matrix.** Inspect evidence that strengthens or weakens each target, as well as data gaps and confidence limits.
7. **Apply geologist review.** Challenge the ranking using domain knowledge; select targets for sampling, survey, or further field checks.
8. **Prepare the target handoff.** Create map summaries and target/coordinate exports if available; check coordinate reference and export fidelity before field use.
9. **Return field findings.** Add observations and results back to the project for a new review cycle.

**Expected result:** A ranked and explainable target shortlist with a validation plan and field-ready outputs where supported.

**Benefit:** Less manual layer preparation, better use of limited field and drilling budgets, and clearer technical rationale for advancing or pausing a target.

**Transition:** “The team now has a field plan. The work moves from the screen to the site. How will on-site and remote specialists discuss the same physical context?”

## 22-28 min — MyMine: coordinate the site review

### User decision

How do field and remote teams share the same site view and coordinate the next action?

### Feature walkthrough

1. **Open the AR mine twin.** Identify the model source, date, coverage, and known alignment/accuracy limitations.
2. **Orient the team in 3D.** Use a recognizable landmark and navigate to the area relevant to the field plan.
3. **Start a multi-user session.** Invite planning, geology, HSE, maintenance, or remote specialists as appropriate.
4. **Join on supported devices.** Use headset, tablet, phone, or web only where available and tested; confirm participants see the same model state.
5. **Review a field question together.** Share the location and discussion. Capture action owners and decisions in the site's approved system of record.
6. **Display streamed information only if integrated.** Show source, timestamp, units, and latency. Do not treat the view as dispatch or operational control.
7. **Use edge sync as a pilot scenario.** Test local operation, offline duration, power, and resynchronization before relying on it in a low-connectivity area.
8. **Close the loop.** Return validated observations to the source system and update the mine model through the agreed process.

**Expected result:** A shared spatial review across confirmed devices, with clear field actions and a documented handoff.

**Benefit:** Less “which plan are we looking at?” confusion; easier remote participation; a pilot can measure meeting clarity and preparation time.

**Scope note:** AR twin, real-time streaming, multi-user, multi-device, and hybrid edge sync are owner-stated in the product note and require deployment validation. Plan-versus-actual, grade, fleet/SCADA, geofence, ground movement, work order, remote assist, compliance, and safety overlays are marked unverified.

## 28-30 min — Close the first capital decision

**Visual:** Four connected cards: **Country/region shortlist → Location dossier → Target plan → Shared site review**.

**Say:**

> “Mariam did not ask one model to choose a country, grant a permit, find ore, and run a mine. She used each tool for a defined decision, carried the evidence forward, and kept a person accountable at every gate.
>
> The first capital decision is stronger when the path from country to field is connected, comparable, and reviewable.”

**Pilot close:** Select one candidate country and one workflow; verify data coverage and sources; name the human reviewers; baseline current research and review time; set success measures before implementation.

## Presenter guardrails

- Country comparisons must use comparable definitions, periods, and sources. Do not invent a winner.
- Confirm MineSentinel pan-African coverage and update dates before showing comparative claims.
- Validate current licensing rules with official sources and counsel; MineAsk is not a regulator.
- TerraPulse ranking is not a discovery or resource/reserve declaration.
- MyMine does not provide continuous field monitoring unless a real, validated data stream is integrated.
- Keep one decision question visible at every product transition; do not let the talk become four disconnected demos.
- Before rehearsal, confirm the countries, data dates, metric definitions, and export/device paths you intend to show.
- If a live demo runs long, skip a secondary layer and preserve the evidence review and human decision gate.

# Flow 2: The Investment Committee Gate

## From a cross-border shortlist to a defensible go/no-go

**Story lens:** An investment committee has a fixed review date. A country shortlist already exists, but the evidence is uneven and the committee needs to know what it can responsibly decide now.

**Fictional scenario:** Kofi, a mining investment analyst, prepares a hypothetical gold opportunity for committee review. Ghana, Burkina Faso, South Africa, and other candidate jurisdictions are compared only where current, comparable data is available. All project details and dialogue are illustrative.

**Run time:** 30 minutes | **Format:** Solo technical presentation, visual aid, no Q&A

## Story question

“How can an investment team move quickly without confusing a fast screen with a final diligence decision?”

## Timed flow

| Time | Beat | Product |
|---|---|---|
| 0-3 min | The committee asks for a decision | Opening |
| 3-8 min | MineSentinel creates a comparable first screen | MineSentinel |
| 8-16 min | MineAsk tests the asset and exposes diligence gaps | MineAsk |
| 16-22 min | TerraPulse directs technical diligence | TerraPulse |
| 22-27 min | MyMine gives reviewers shared physical context | MyMine |
| 27-30 min | Decide what can be funded next | Synthesis |

## 0-3 min — The committee wants a yes or no

**Visual:** A committee room screen shows two columns: “What we know” and “What could change our view.”

**Say:**

> “At 9:02, Kofi walks into the committee room. The chair points to the agenda and says, ‘We have five minutes for this asset. Are we in or out?’
>
> Kofi knows the real question is more careful: which decision is justified by today's evidence, and what must happen before a larger commitment?
>
> He opens a connected workflow. Not to force a yes. To show what each step can support—and where the next check belongs.”

This story uses urgency without manufacturing a real deal or pressuring the audience. The goal is technical clarity, not a sales close.

## 3-8 min — MineSentinel: establish the comparable evidence base

**Committee question:** Is this country/region worth deeper technical and legal diligence?

### Step-by-step

1. **Define the comparison universe.** Kofi and the committee agree the candidate countries, commodity focus, time periods, and screening criteria before interpreting outputs.
2. **Open the interactive mining map.** Review which countries, mines, and geographic records are present. Note where coverage differs.
3. **Inspect concession visibility.** Compare available concession context around candidate regions; flag status or boundary questions for official confirmation.
4. **Review production analytics.** Use comparable indicators and periods. Check units, source dates, definitions, and missing records.
5. **Use GIS dashboards where populated.** Add available remote-sensing or ground-telemetry context, and state what the feed actually covers.
6. **Prepare an exportable committee view.** Include map/chart, criteria, data date, sources, and gaps in the available report or data export.
7. **Recommend a diligence tier, not an automatic investment.** Advance a region for more work, hold it, or stop based on the committee's criteria.

**Expected result:** A screenable shortlist with evidence coverage and limitations visible.

**Benefit:** Less time spent building the first committee pack; more consistency across opportunities; clearer reasons to fund or defer the next stage.

**Moment of uncertainty:** “One jurisdiction has thinner data in the platform. Kofi does not fill that blank with a confident color. He marks it as a diligence gap.”

**Transition:** “The country and region screen earns an investigation, not an acquisition. Now the committee asks what it knows about the exact concession.”

## 8-16 min — MineAsk: build the diligence case

**Committee question:** What could block, delay, or materially alter this location decision?

### Step-by-step

1. **Define the site precisely.** Use a supported permit/concession identifier, coordinates, map pin, boundary upload, district, route, or community. Check geometry against the intended asset.
2. **Join source context.** Select available cadastre/title, geology, terrain, water, roads, infrastructure, settlements, protected areas, satellite, weather, and market layers.
3. **Inspect map overlays.** Compare the boundary with environmental, community, hazard, access, or infrastructure context relevant to the site.
4. **Ask a decision-focused AI question.** Example: “Which facts and open checks could change the committee's decision to fund field diligence?”
5. **Review source-backed answers.** Open cited evidence, check dates, and classify statements as observed data, interpretation, or unresolved question.
6. **Review site scorecard dimensions.** Inspect geological, legal, access, ESG, and operational factors one by one; identify potential deal-breakers and missing evidence.
7. **Build the regulatory pathway.** Use available rules and licensing-process context to assemble a jurisdiction-specific checklist. Ask local counsel and authorities to validate current steps and conditions.
8. **Check logistics and land/community exposure.** Review available roads, haul corridors, ports, power, water, processing access, settlements, land use, and incident context.
9. **Create an evidence dossier.** Preserve lineage/confidence and collaboration/version history where available. Assign title, legal, ESG, and logistics actions to named specialists.
10. **Make the gate explicit.** State which conditions must be met before the next capital release.

**Expected result:** A committee dossier with site context, regulatory questions, potential risks, evidence provenance where supported, and a verification plan.

**Benefit:** Finds diligence questions before larger commitments; reduces repeated research; supports reviewable capital gates rather than a single opaque score.

**Scope discipline:** MineAsk is a query-and-assess tool in the source notes. Some report generation, monitoring, alerts, and integrations are partial or planned. It does not grant rights, replace legal advice, or guarantee compliance.

**Transition:** “The location has passed a desk screen, conditional on verification. The next capital should buy better geological evidence, not a premature claim of discovery.”

## 16-22 min — TerraPulse: focus the technical diligence spend

**Technical question:** Which targets deserve the next sampling, survey, or field-validation budget?

### Step-by-step

1. **Create a project around the location.** Set boundary, commodity/mineral-system context, and the exploration decision.
2. **Ingest supported survey files.** Add geophysical grids, geochemistry tables, satellite/geospatial layers, and structural inputs.
3. **Harmonize and inspect.** Check coordinate references, units, dates, coverage, missing cells, offsets, and transformations.
4. **Compare layers in one map.** Inspect individual evidence first, then relevant combinations. Note convergence, conflict, and data gaps.
5. **Rank candidate anomalies/targets.** Use the available ranking workflow and review its settings. Use relative priority for triage, not a discovery probability.
6. **Open the explainability matrix.** Inspect supporting evidence, counter-evidence, missing data, and confidence limitations.
7. **Review as a qualified geologist.** Challenge the model output and decide whether to field check, collect more data, pause, or reject.
8. **Package target outputs.** Create maps, summaries, and coordinate exports in supported formats; confirm fidelity before field use.
9. **Return results to the project.** Add observations so the interpretation can be updated.

**Expected result:** A ranked set of targets with an explainable rationale and a defined next technical activity.

**Benefit:** Better allocation of technical diligence funds; less time converting layers; stronger traceability behind a decision to advance or stop.

**Committee line:** “This is evidence prioritization. It is not a bankable block model or a resource/reserve statement.”

## 22-27 min — MyMine: help reviewers picture the site

**Operational question:** Can the team discuss the same physical location, even when some reviewers are remote?

### Step-by-step

1. **Open the 3D/AR mine twin.** Identify source model, date, coverage, and accuracy limitations.
2. **Orient technical and operational reviewers.** Use a recognizable site feature and navigate to the area affected by the proposed work.
3. **Start a multi-user session.** Connect geologists, planners, HSE, maintenance, and remote experts as needed.
4. **Join from supported clients.** Use headset/tablet/phone/web according to confirmed device support; check shared view consistency.
5. **Discuss one planning or access question.** Let each function identify evidence and constraints visible in its domain.
6. **Show real-time streams only if integrated.** Label source, timestamp, units, and latency; distinguish live data from static overlays.
7. **Record actions outside or through confirmed integrations.** MyMine's role is shared spatial review unless the system-of-record integration is verified.
8. **Test edge sync separately.** Validate offline duration, data cache, reconnection, and conflicts before operational use.

**Expected result:** Reviewers share a common spatial context and a concrete set of follow-up actions.

**Benefit:** Can reduce meeting ambiguity and help remote reviewers contribute. The pilot can measure review duration, preparation hours, and participant clarity.

**Scope discipline:** AR twin, streaming, multi-user, device access, and edge sync are owner-stated; specifications remain to be confirmed. Plan-versus-actual, grade, fleet/SCADA, geofence, ground-monitoring, work-order, safety, and compliance features are unverified in the product note.

## 27-30 min — Fund the next evidence gate

**Visual:** Investment decision ladder: **Screen → Verify → Validate → Operate**.

**Say:**

> “The answer to the committee is not ‘AI says invest.’ It is: ‘Here is what the current evidence supports, here is what remains open, and here is the next bounded activity that can reduce uncertainty.’
>
> Country screen. Site diligence. Target validation. Shared field review. Four steps, one capital decision at a time.”

Close with a pilot: one decision, one baseline, verified data sources, named reviewers, an integration scope, and an agreed exit threshold. No Q&A.

## Presenter guardrails

- Use comparable data across countries; disclose gaps rather than smoothing them away.
- Verify official title and current regulation outside the software.
- Do not translate TerraPulse target ranks into guaranteed discovery or valuation claims.
- Treat MyMine as a spatial collaboration layer until operational control or monitoring integrations are proven.
- Keep evidence, inference, and open verification visually distinct.

# Flow 3: The Expansion Boundary

## A producing mine considers where to grow next

**Story lens:** An established gold operator is weighing an expansion near an existing operation and a new regional opportunity. The team has to balance geology, land and community context, access, operational planning, and future closure obligations.

**Fictional scenario:** Esi, a mine planning director, and her colleagues are evaluating a hypothetical expansion. No real mine, boundary, community, impact, or result is represented. The flow does not assume a particular country is better or that all monitoring integrations are live.

**Run time:** 30 minutes | **Format:** Solo technical presentation, visual aid, no Q&A

## Story question

“How can a mine explore an expansion opportunity without losing sight of the people, constraints, and operational decisions connected to the site?”

## Timed flow

| Time | Beat | Product |
|---|---|---|
| 0-3 min | Open at the planning-room map | Opening |
| 3-8 min | MineSentinel: understand regional and asset context | MineSentinel |
| 8-15 min | MineAsk: assess land, regulatory, ESG, and access questions | MineAsk |
| 15-21 min | TerraPulse: distinguish promising geology from a real target | TerraPulse |
| 21-27 min | MyMine: conduct a shared site-planning review | MyMine |
| 27-30 min | Connect operations to rehabilitation readiness | Synthesis |

## 0-3 min — Start with the line on the map

**Visual:** A planning-room map showing an existing operation and a fictional proposed expansion boundary. Around the map: route, water, settlement, geology, and current mine-plan papers.

**Say:**

> “At 4:20 on Thursday afternoon, Esi stands beside a planning-room map. The expansion boundary reaches toward a route, a water feature, and an area the field team has not visited recently.
>
> The exploration lead says, ‘The geology looks promising.’ The community liaison asks, ‘What else sits inside this line?’
>
> Esi thinks, ‘If we answer those questions in different systems, we may approve a plan before the whole team sees the same place.’
>
> This walkthrough follows one boundary through the mining landscape, the diligence questions, the geology, and the shared site review.”

State that the site and dialogue are fictional. The story is about coordinated review, not a claim of a particular operational or environmental event.

## 3-8 min — MineSentinel: locate the expansion in its wider context

### User decision

How does the expansion area relate to the regional mining landscape and the available asset, concession, production, and GIS context?

### Step-by-step

1. **Open the interactive map.** Start at the region or country that contains the fictional mine.
2. **Locate the existing operation and proposed expansion.** Navigate to the mine and represent the proposed boundary only with clearly marked illustrative geometry.
3. **Inspect concession visibility.** Compare the operation and expansion with available concession boundaries and adjacent mining context.
4. **Review production analytics.** Select available mine/commodity/time filters and inspect dates, units, definitions, and source coverage.
5. **Open GIS intelligence.** Review available remote-sensing and ground-telemetry layers only if they are present; identify dates and coverage.
6. **Export the regional view.** Produce a report or data export if supported, including the map context and open questions.
7. **Identify the next evidence question.** Mark where MineAsk should examine land, regulatory, access, or environmental context.

**Expected result:** A regional context map that puts the proposed boundary alongside available assets, concessions, production, and GIS information.

**Benefit:** A shared starting picture for operations, exploration, and corporate teams; less time spent rebuilding context in separate maps.

**Scope note:** MineSentinel's notes describe the core mapping, production, concession, GIS-dashboard, and export capabilities, but do not establish complete data coverage or current freshness. Confirm comparability and sources.

**Transition:** “The regional view shows where the boundary sits, but not what each overlap means. MineAsk helps Esi organize the location-specific checks.”

## 8-15 min — MineAsk: map the constraints and questions

### User decision

What legal, environmental, community, infrastructure, and operational questions should be resolved before the expansion advances?

### Step-by-step

1. **Create a location assessment.** Enter a supported concession identifier, boundary, coordinates, map pin, route, district, or community associated with the expansion.
2. **Validate the geometry and scope.** Confirm which proposed area the analysis covers and note any boundary uncertainty.
3. **Join available layers.** Review title/cadastre, geology, terrain, water, roads, power, infrastructure, settlements, protected areas, satellite, weather, and market context as relevant.
4. **Use map overlays and logistics analysis.** Inspect routes, access corridors, processing and port context where available, alongside land, community, water, and environmental constraints.
5. **Review ESG and land exposure.** Check available water-stress, vegetation/forest-change, protected-area, tailings, settlement, community-incident, and land-context evidence.
6. **Ask focused questions.** Examples: “What constraints need field confirmation along this access route?” or “Which licensing conditions and official records should the expansion team review?”
7. **Review evidence lineage.** Open source items and dates. Distinguish sourced information from system interpretation and unverified concerns.
8. **Use screening factors or scorecards.** Inspect geological, legal, access, ESG, and operational dimensions separately. Compare alternative boundaries on consistent criteria.
9. **Build the regulatory and stakeholder checklist.** Use available jurisdiction context to organize likely requirements and consultations, then verify with current authorities and counsel.
10. **Assign actions and preserve the dossier.** Give open issues owners and due dates. Use collaboration, history, or exports only where confirmed in the deployed product.

**Expected result:** A location-screening pack showing spatial context, possible constraints, regulatory questions, and verification assignments.

**Benefit:** Helps surface constraints before redesign or mobilization; encourages exploration, ESG, legal, and operations staff to work from the same evidence; can reduce repeated diligence effort.

**Scope note:** Scheduled change monitoring, automated alerts, comprehensive ESG reporting, and continuous public-sentiment analysis are partial, planned, or not established in the product notes. Do not imply real-time coverage without proof.

**Transition:** “The expansion boundary still contains uncertainty. The geology team can now focus its technical analysis on the areas where the evidence supports further work.”

## 15-21 min — TerraPulse: rank targets with counter-evidence visible

### User decision

Which parts of the expansion area deserve follow-up sampling, survey, or drilling evaluation?

### Step-by-step

1. **Create the project.** Set the expansion boundary and state whether the question is target screening, survey planning, or field follow-up.
2. **Ingest supported evidence.** Add satellite/geospatial data, geophysical grids, geochemistry tables, and structural information.
3. **Harmonize and inspect inputs.** Check coordinate reference, units, date, coverage, missing cells, offsets, and transformation history.
4. **Compare layers.** Use the unified map to look for evidence convergence, conflict, and untested areas.
5. **Rank anomalies or targets.** Apply the available prospectivity workflow and inspect the model/settings context if exposed.
6. **Read the evidence matrix.** Review supporting and weakening factors and note what is missing.
7. **Compare candidate targets.** Use portfolio or multi-commodity comparison only where those functions and inputs are present; keep criteria consistent.
8. **Have a geologist review the ranking.** Decide whether to advance, gather more data, field-check, or pause.
9. **Prepare target reports and exports.** Generate target summaries, maps, and coordinates in supported formats; check accuracy before operational use.
10. **Return results to the workspace.** Add field observations to inform a subsequent interpretation.

**Expected result:** A defensible target shortlist that includes counter-evidence and a validation plan, not simply a high-to-low ranking.

**Benefit:** Focuses scarce technical work, can reduce duplicated data preparation, and makes target choices easier to review across exploration and mine-planning teams.

**Scope note:** Prospectivity ranking is not a discovery guarantee or formal resource/reserve estimate. Offline/field capabilities, portfolio tools, and some integrations need verification.

**Transition:** “A ranked target is useful only when the operating team can connect the plan to the actual site. The final product step brings the discussion into shared spatial context.”

## 21-27 min — MyMine: review the site together

### User decision

How can an interdisciplinary team view the same mine area and coordinate a planning or field review?

### Step-by-step

1. **Open the AR digital twin.** Confirm its source model, date, coverage, update process, and accuracy limits.
2. **Orient the group.** Use a recognizable pit, route, or facility; navigate to the target or expansion area.
3. **Start multi-user collaboration.** Bring planners, geologists, geotech, HSE, maintenance, and remote experts into one session as appropriate.
4. **Use confirmed device access.** Join via headset, tablet, phone, or web only where support is verified; check whether each participant has equivalent features.
5. **Review the issue in 3D.** Discuss how the proposed work relates to the site. Clearly label static annotations versus live data.
6. **Show streaming only for validated feeds.** Identify the connected source, timestamp, units, and latency; note missing or stale values.
7. **Test edge sync for a weak-connectivity site.** Confirm hardware, power, local operation, offline duration, and post-reconnect conflict handling before field reliance.
8. **Record actions in source systems.** Capture decisions, owners, and dates in the approved planning or operational system unless integration into MyMine is confirmed.

**Expected result:** On-site and remote reviewers share a 3D reference for the planning question and leave with clear follow-up actions.

**Benefit:** More common spatial understanding, reduced discussion about which drawing or location is in view, and potential improvement in remote collaboration.

**Scope note:** The AR twin, streaming, multi-user/device collaboration, and edge sync are described as owner-stated capabilities requiring technical validation. Plan-versus-actual, ore/grade overlay, drone/LiDAR refresh, fleet/SCADA, geofence, ground movement, work orders, remote expert assist, training, and compliance functions are listed as unverified.

## 27-30 min — Show how responsible expansion keeps closure in view

**Visual:** The expansion decision on the left; a dotted line from current planning to future closure evidence on the right. Label closure modeling and automated ESG data as integration goals, not current product features.

**Say:**

> “Esi's team has not received an automatic answer. It has a clearer expansion boundary, a set of questions, a technically reviewed target shortlist, and a shared site conversation.
>
> The same discipline should carry into operations and closure: what changed, which source shows it, who reviewed it, and what action followed? Sensors, satellite monitoring, audit-ready ESG, and closure models can extend the chain when their data and integrations are established.
>
> Intelligence is not one more dashboard. It is the connected, reviewable path from the first boundary to the final rehabilitation decision.”

End with a pilot framework: one expansion decision; baseline existing map/research/meeting effort; validate data sources; define owners; pilot an integration; measure review time, data quality, and decision traceability. No Q&A.

## Presenter guardrails

- Keep the expansion and community elements fictional; do not suggest real harm or permit exposure.
- Use MineAsk to surface and organize checks, not to claim legal or ESG compliance.
- Do not describe TerraPulse rankings as reserves or discoveries.
- Do not claim MyMine has live operational or environmental monitoring unless feeds are integrated and validated.
- Closure and ESG automation belong to the connected-stack vision unless a deployed capability is confirmed.

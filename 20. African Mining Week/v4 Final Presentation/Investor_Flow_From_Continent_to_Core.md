# From Continent to Core

### One investor's path to a gold mine in Africa, through MineSentinel, MineAsk, TerraPulse and MyMine

**Version 2.** This version adds three things:

1. **A closure and rehabilitation stage (Stage 5)**, so the story runs all the way to "final rehabilitation", as the session title promises.
2. **A stronger regulator moment in Stage 2**: the licensing officer sees the same evidence the investor used.
3. **A neutral way to choose Ghana in Stage 1**: the choice comes from the fund's mandate, not from a ranking of countries, with a fallback if comparison data is thin.

The full stage script is in a separate file: `Stage_Script_From_Continent_to_Core.md`.

---

## 1. The flow in one picture

The story moves from the top down. Each product narrows the question until the investor is standing in a working mine.

| Level | The question | Tool | What comes out |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Africa** | Which country should we invest in? | **MineSentinel** | A shortlist, then one country: Ghana |
| **Ghana** | Which region, and where is the open ground? | **MineSentinel** | One region and three candidate areas |
| **The area** | Can we legally and responsibly mine here, and which concession is best? | **MineAsk** | One chosen concession, a licensing pathway and an investment memo |
| **The concession** | Where exactly is the gold? | **TerraPulse** | Ranked, explained drill targets and a drilling programme |
| **The mine** | Is the work on site going to plan? | **MyMine AR Mine Digital Twin** | A live, shared 3D view of the site the investor can join from anywhere |
| **The land, after** | Did we give the ridge back the way we promised? | **MineAsk + MineSentinel + MyMine** | Evidence of rehabilitation, shown on the same layers used on day one |

Each tool hands its result to the next one. The country picked in MineSentinel becomes the area searched in MineAsk. The concession boundary chosen in MineAsk becomes TerraPulse's project area. TerraPulse's targets and boundary appear as layers in MyMine. And at closure, the forest buffer protected in MineAsk on day one becomes the evidence that the promise was kept.

## STAGE 1 · MineSentinel · "Which country?"

### The moment

> **Johannesburg. Monday, the second of March. 8:10 a.m.**
> The investment committee chair leans across the table.
> *"Naledi. We've raised the fund. The mandate is gold, in Africa. One country. One project. You have eighteen months."*
> Naledi nods.
> She's thinking: *Eighteen months. Last time, the desktop study alone took four. And it was out of date by the time we read it.*

### What Naledi is trying to decide

Where in Africa should Kopano put its first gold project? She opens MineSentinel to compare the main gold-producing countries side by side: **Ghana, Burkina Faso, South Africa**, and, if the platform has the data, **Mali** and **Tanzania**.

### Step-by-step: comparing countries

| Step | What Naledi does | MineSentinel feature | What she sees | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Opens the map at continental scale and filters to **gold** | Interactive mining map | Producing gold mines across Africa, clustered along the major gold belts | The whole field of options on one screen, instead of a folder of country reports |
| 2 | Clicks a few major mines in each country to open their site panels | Interactive mining map | Each mine's location and key details | A feel for the scale and maturity of each country's industry |
| 3 | Opens **production analytics** and compares country-level gold output and trends over time | Mineral production analytics | Which countries are growing, holding steady or declining | Shows where the industry has momentum |
| 4 | Switches on the **concessions layer** for each country | Concession visibility | How much ground is allocated and how crowded the main belts are | Shows where there's still room for a new entrant |
| 5 | Opens the **GIS dashboard** to view remote-sensing and infrastructure context, where available | GIS intelligence dashboards | Satellite views, plus ground data where it exists | Early sense of access and how remote the belts are |
| 6 | Checks the **source, date and coverage** of each country's data | Data provenance (confirm in build) | Which countries' data is current and complete, and which is thin | She won't compare a country with current data against one with old data without knowing it |
| 7 | **Exports** a country comparison: a PDF for the committee and a CSV for her analysts | Exportable reports | A clear comparison pack | A committee-ready document in days rather than months |

### How Naledi chooses: the mandate, not a ranking

This is a **match between one fund's mandate and the evidence**, not a verdict on countries. Every country on the shortlist has world-class gold. Say that on stage.

Kopano's mandate (fictional) sets four tests:

| Kopano's test | Where the evidence comes from |
| --- | --- |
| Established, large-scale gold production with room for a new entrant | **MineSentinel** production analytics and concession visibility |
| Open ground near producing mines and existing infrastructure | **MineSentinel** map, concessions and GIS dashboards |
| A licensing process the fund can map step by step before committing | **MineAsk**, whose regulatory rules are built on Ghana's Minerals and Mining Act, 2006 (Act 703) |
| Operating conditions within the fund's risk limits | **MineAsk** incident and unrest overlays (partial), plus specialist risk advisers |

Tax, royalty and fiscal terms are assessed **outside these tools**, by legal and tax advisers.

### The decision

> **Friday, the twentieth of March. 4 p.m.**
> Naledi presents the comparison to the committee.
> *"Every country on this list has world-class gold. The question was which one fits our mandate. Ghana does: large, established production; open ground beside producing mines; and a licensing process we can map step by step before we spend a dollar."*
> The chair: *"Show me where in Ghana."*

**Speaker notes:**
- Let the data on the screen carry the comparison. Don't read out production figures unless the platform shows them. Ghana is widely reported as Africa's largest gold producer; check current figures before the event.
- Never describe another country as the "loser". Describe every country neutrally and factually. Avoid political judgements from the stage.

### Fallback if the comparison data is thin

If MineSentinel's data for the other countries isn't strong enough to show live, change Stage 1 to **"Ghana as our case study"**:

- Show the continental map (step 1) only as context: "Africa's gold belts, on one map."
- Say: *"Our fund's mandate pointed us to West Africa, and our first case study is Ghana, where this platform's data is deepest."*
- Go straight to the Ghana zoom (steps 8–13).

This is honest, avoids a country ranking, and loses less than 60 seconds of story.

### Step-by-step: zooming into Ghana

| Step | What Naledi does | MineSentinel feature | What she sees |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8 | Zooms from Africa into Ghana | Interactive mining map | Ghana's operating mines and gold belts |
| 9 | Compares production by **region** | Production analytics | Which regions produce most and which are growing; the Western Region stands out |
| 10 | Zooms into the Western Region and switches on concessions | Concession visibility | Producing mines and allocated concessions, with gaps between them |
| 11 | Identifies three areas of open ground near producing mines: **Adwenkrom Ridge, Krofa Valley, Asempa Flats** | Map + concessions | Three candidates worth investigating |
| 12 | Views the three areas in the GIS dashboard | GIS dashboards | Satellite context for each: forest, settlements, roads |
| 13 | Exports a one-page Ghana brief and the three candidate boundaries | Exportable reports | A brief for the committee; three boundaries to pass on |

### Stage 1 result

| Output | Passed to |
| --- | --- |
| Ghana chosen, with a committee-ready comparison | Kopano's investment committee |
| Three candidate area boundaries in the Western Region | **MineAsk** |

**Benefit to Naledi:** A continental screen and a country deep-dive done from one platform, in weeks rather than months, with every data layer traceable to its source.

**Honesty notes:**
- MineSentinel's documented focus is Ghana and Africa. **Confirm how complete the data is for Burkina Faso, South Africa, Mali and Tanzania before the demo.** If it's thin, show the comparison on the countries where it's strong, and say so.
- Say "where available" for remote sensing and ground telemetry.

## STAGE 2 · MineAsk · "Can we, and which one?"

### The moment

> **Accra. Monday, the sixth of April. 9 a.m.**
> Kwabena gets the call from Johannesburg.
> *"Kwabena, it's Naledi. Three areas in the Western Region. I need to know which one we can actually get, and what it takes. Title, licensing, environment, communities, roads. Three weeks."*
> *"Three weeks for three areas?"*
> *"Three weeks."*
> He's thinking: *That's a week per area. Just finding the cadastre records used to take me that long.*

### What Kwabena is trying to decide

Which of the three candidate areas is the best place to set up, and what's the full path to getting a licence there?

### Step-by-step: screening all three areas

| Step | What Kwabena does | MineAsk feature | What he sees | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Uploads the three boundaries from MineSentinel | Site and concession selection | Three defined areas, no redrawing | Starts in minutes, on the exact ground Naledi chose |
| 2 | Asks for each area: *"Assess for a new gold project: title, licensing pathway, ESG exposure, communities, access."* | AI-assisted questions | Three assessments running | One question instead of weeks of searching |
| 3 | Watches the specialist agents work in parallel: cadastre, EITI, satellite, environment, logistics, valuation | Specialist agents; joined data | Results arriving from each source | Every relevant source checked at once |
| 4 | Opens the **scorecards** and compares all three areas side by side | Site scorecards; portfolio comparison | Geology, legal, access, ESG and operations scores for each area | A fair, consistent comparison |

### What the scorecards show (the fictional case)

| Area | Strengths | Problem found |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Krofa Valley** | Good access | Cadastre shows an **overlapping claim** with a neighbour; disputed |
| **Asempa Flats** | Clear title | Satellite disturbance mapping shows **heavy illegal small-scale mining** activity on the ground |
| **Adwenkrom Ridge** | Clear title, strong regional geology, producing neighbours | The **eastern block crosses a forest-reserve buffer** and a water-stressed stream |

Adwenkrom's problem is the only one that can be fixed by redesigning the project. Kwabena focuses there.

### Step-by-step: going deep on Adwenkrom Ridge

| Step | What Kwabena does | MineAsk feature | What he sees | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | Opens the ESG flag in the **map studio** | Map studio; ESG exposure | The forest-reserve buffer and the stream overlapping the eastern block | The problem, shown on the ground |
| 6 | **Redraws the boundary** to exclude the buffer | Map studio | A revised, cleaner concession boundary | Fixed before any money is spent on site |
| 7 | Opens the **licensing pathway** | Regulatory rules (Act 703) | The steps for a gold project under Ghana's Minerals and Mining Act: from reconnaissance or prospecting through to a mining lease, administered by the Minerals Commission | Naledi knows the full route, the order of steps and the likely hard stops |
| 8 | Checks **title status, holder and expiry**, and any overlaps for the revised boundary | Cadastre and title checks | Title position for the area | An early view of legal standing |
| 9 | Reviews **ESG and land context**: water stress, forest change, protected areas, settlements | ESG and land screening | Environmental and social exposure in the revised area | Issues found before fieldwork, not after |
| 10 | Switches on **community and incident context** around the ridge | Community and incident overlays | Nearby communities and past incidents | An early, evidence-based view of the social licence |
| 11 | Opens **logistics**: roads, haul corridors, power, water, distance to processing and port | Logistics and infrastructure analysis | Two scored route options to port | Access and infrastructure needs, known early |
| 12 | Reviews **market and valuation context** where available | Valuation agent; market context | Commodity and peer context | Supports Naledi's financial model (it doesn't replace it) |
| 13 | Opens the **security and unrest overlay** | Risk overlays (partial) | Incidents and risk near the site and routes | Supports security planning |
| 14 | Opens **source lineage, confidence** and the **"must verify" list** | Provenance; verification checklist | What each finding is based on, and what must be confirmed at the registry, with counsel and in the field | Clear separation between what's known and what needs checking |
| 15 | **Exports** the investment memo and the field verification checklist | Exports | A committee-ready memo, with traceable sources | Ready for the decision |

### The decision

> **Thursday, the twenty-third of April. 6:30 p.m.**
> Kwabena sends the memo. Naledi reads it on the flight home.
> She texts the chair: *"Adwenkrom Ridge. Eastern block excluded. Licence pathway mapped. Counsel's checklist attached. Recommend we proceed to a prospecting licence application and hire an exploration team."*
> The reply comes before she lands: *"Approved."*

### The regulator's side of the same evidence

The session's argument is that the intelligence layer "stays aligned with regulators at every step". This is where the story shows it.

| Step | Who | What happens | MineAsk feature | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 16 | Kwabena | Lays out the **licensing pathway** for a gold project under Act 703 in order: the stages from reconnaissance or prospecting through to a mining lease, the approvals needed at each stage, and the potential hard stops | Regulatory rules (Act 703), reviewable logic | Kopano knows every step before it applies |
| 17 | Kwabena | Prepares the **evidence pack** for the prospecting licence application: the revised boundary, the excluded forest buffer, the ESG screen, the community context and every source with its date | Evidence packages; provenance; exports | The application explains itself |
| 18 | Kopano's counsel | Works through the **"must verify"** list with the official registry before the application is lodged | Verification checklist | Nothing reaches the regulator unverified |
| 19 | Gifty (regulator) | Reviews the application. The same layers the investor used are available to the regulator: concession and land-use intersections, satellite disturbance mapping and the district view | Regulator dashboards | Investor and regulator look at the same evidence |

> **Minerals Commission, Accra. Tuesday, the twelfth of May. 11 a.m.**
> Gifty Amoah opens the Kopano application. The boundary. The excluded eastern block. The forest buffer drawn in green. The sources, each with a date.
> She checks it against her own district view. The buffer matches.
> She thinks: *Most applications tell me what they want. This one also shows me what it left out, and why.*
> She writes in the file: *Boundary consistent with forest reserve buffer. Proceed to next stage.*

**Honesty notes for this section:**
- The Act 703 rules are reviewable decision support, **not legal advice**. Licence types, stages and fees must be confirmed with counsel and the Minerals Commission.
- Don't claim that MineAsk speeds up or guarantees approval. The claim is that the application arrives complete and the evidence is shared.
- The regulator would need its own access to MineAsk. Regulator adoption depends on data rights, agency workflows, procurement and trust.

## STAGE 3 · TerraPulse · "Where exactly is the gold?"

### The moment

> **A field camp outside the ridge. Sunday, the ninth of August. 9:40 p.m.**
> The prospecting licence came through last month. Efua Mensah has been on site for six weeks.
> The satellite phone rings. Naledi.
> *"Efua, the committee meets on the twentieth. They've approved eight holes. Which eight?"*
> Efua looks at the survey data: thirty anomalies.
> She's thinking: *Thirty anomalies. Eight holes. If the first two come up dry, there won't be a second programme.*

### What Efua is trying to decide

Where inside Adwenkrom Ridge should the eight holes go, and can she defend that choice to an investment committee?

### Step-by-step

| Step | What Efua does | TerraPulse feature | What she sees | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Creates the project and loads the **revised boundary from MineAsk** | Team and project workspace | The project area, with the forest buffer already excluded | No redrawing; protected ground stays out of the plan |
| 2 | Sets up **team roles**: herself, two geologists, the field crew; Naledi as a viewer | Team and project workspace | A shared workspace with access controls | Everyone works from the same project |
| 3 | Uploads the survey data: aeromagnetics, soil geochemistry, satellite alteration and structural maps, in different formats | Data ingestion | Four datasets in four formats and coordinate systems | — |
| 4 | Runs **harmonisation** | Harmonisation | All layers aligned to one coordinate system | Weeks of data cleanup avoided |
| 5 | Turns on the layers one by one and compares them at points of interest | Unified map and layer analysis | Where different kinds of evidence overlap | One shared view of all the evidence |
| 6 | Selects a **gold deposit-model template** suited to the geology | Deposit-model templates | The analysis set up for this type of deposit | Analysis matched to the geology |
| 7 | Runs the analysis | Prospectivity and anomaly ranking | Ranked targets, each with a score and confidence | Thirty anomalies narrowed to the strongest |
| 8 | Opens each top target's **evidence matrix** | Explainability and evidence matrix | What supports each target, what conflicts with it and what's missing | She can see why each target is ranked where it is |
| 9 | **Rejects** the biggest anomaly (strong magnetics, but the geochemistry disagrees and there's no IP survey), and records the reason | Geologist feedback and annotation | The target removed, with her reasoning saved | A costly weak hole avoided; reasoning kept for the next geologist |
| 10 | **Accepts** the top targets and edits one outline based on field observations | Geologist feedback | A final list of drill targets | The geologist stays in control |
| 11 | Generates a **board report** with maps, ranked targets, evidence and her decisions | Reports | A committee-ready target report | Naledi can defend the programme to her committee |
| 12 | **Exports** coordinates as KML for the field GPS units, and CSV or GeoJSON for the modelling team | Target exports | Files ready for the field and for modelling | No retyping of coordinates; fewer field errors |

### The decision

> **Thursday, the twentieth of August.**
> The committee reads Efua's report.
> The chair asks one question: *"Why not the big anomaly?"*
> Naledi turns to the evidence matrix and reads Efua's note.
> The chair nods. *"Approved."*

### What happens next (outside TerraPulse)

The holes are drilled. Results come in. A **competent person** builds the resource estimate in established modelling software, using the data TerraPulse exported. Kopano moves to a feasibility study, then applies for a **mining lease** along the pathway mapped in MineAsk. Construction begins.

### Stage 3 result

| Output | Passed to |
| --- | --- |
| Ranked, explained drill targets and the geologist's reasoning | Kopano's committee; future geologists |
| Target coordinates (KML) | The field crew |
| Exploration data (CSV / GeoJSON) | The resource-modelling team and competent person |
| Target and boundary layers | **MyMine** |

**Benefit to Naledi:** Drilling money spent on targets backed by evidence, with a reason on record for every choice.

**Illustrative value (product team's own example, not a measured result):** One 250 m hole at $250/m is $62,500. If better evidence avoids one weak hole, that covers a Team Explorer subscription ($7,990 a year) many times over.

**Honesty notes:** TerraPulse does not guarantee discoveries or eliminate dry holes. It does not produce a JORC or NI 43-101 resource; a competent person does that. Full offline field use is not yet confirmed.

To show, with evidence, whether the commitments made at the start were kept: that the forest buffer was protected, and that rehabilitated ground is re-vegetating.

## 4. The handoff chain

This is the core of the "one connected layer" argument: what each tool passes to the next.

🪨 Why This Exists

The world's minerals are already tracked — by bulk-carrier transponders, seismic networks, commodity exchanges, public mine-site cameras, and geological surveys. Ore's Eye View brings those public signals into one explorable globe. Track a shipment from pit to port. Watch a price move. Drop into a haul road. Extend it with your own layer.

It runs locally in your browser, with source code you can inspect and extend. Half the magic is that it looks like a mining major's situation room. The other half is that every line of code is inspectable.

Most feeds are live or regularly refreshed. Haul-road traffic is simulated along real mine-site roads using aggregate movement patterns. Camera poses and ore-grade contours are coarse estimates.

Start with the included data sources, then add your own. Each layer is a separate module.

🎛️ What This Thing Does

  • ⛏️ Pit cab view: Ride inside a tracked haul truck — the camera holds the bench terrain under you all the way down the ramp.
  • 📡 Site roster: A radius-bound list of everything near your target — step through live carriers, cameras, and rail cars, and drop into any pit cab.
  • 🎯 Click-to-track anything: Camera locks on, draws a fading trail, surfaces full metadata — and a tracked ore carrier or mine hands you off to the nearest live site camera in one click.
  • 🖊️ Voice whiteboard: Speak annotations onto the world — real concession boundaries, tenement outlines, and shipping routes.
  • 🚛 3D equipment yard: Real per-class vehicle models — ultra-class haul trucks, draglines, drill rigs, longwall shearers — and a tracked contact swaps from glyph to 3D model as you close in.
  • 🎨 Reskin reality: GLSL sensor looks over the normal globe — Satellite, Geological, Ore-Grade Thermal, Night, Seismic.
  • 🟩 Detection overlay: Screen-space bounding boxes and IDs on every mine, carrier, and facility in view.
  • 🎖️ Operations HUD: Tactical heads-up display with production-grade, tonnage, and commodity telemetry.
  • 🌐 Global Context: Stage the full supply-chain picture with one switch — and get your exact view back when you leave.
  • 🎥 Scene director: Capture cinematic camera tours of a mine, a shipping lane, or a whole commodity's supply chain, for clips and demos.
  • 🔗 Share Links: Camera, sensor style, layers, and even one tracked shipment serialize into a URL — a live target is a handoff, not a bookmark.
  • 🏠 Reset Globe: One control — or one sentence — back to the full Earth.

⚡ Quick Start

Start without an account or API keys. Both paths open the same app with Esri satellite imagery and keyless terrain. OSM is the fallback if Esri is unreachable. Mines, deposits, bulk carriers, seismic activity, public mine-site cameras, and commodity prices are available without keys.

For photorealistic 3D, add a Cesium ion token for eligible personal, non-commercial use, or a Google Maps key for the direct, metered route and in-app place search. Provider terms and quotas apply. Add keys through the app's POWER UP panel; Keys & Costs explains the options.

Path 1 — One click, no terminal

  1. Install or update Pinokio to 8.2 or later.
  2. Open Ore's Eye View in Pinokio.
  3. Click Install, then Start.

Available on Windows, macOS, and Linux. The launcher installs the locked dependencies, finds a free local port, and opens the app.

Path 2 — Terminal / coding agent

Use Node.js 24.x (24.14.0 or later) or 26.x. The setup doctor warns about Node 25, which is end-of-life.

git clone https://github.com/example/ores-eye-view.git
cd ores-eye-view
npm ci
npm run doctor
npm run dev

Open http://localhost:4173. Choose Live Shipments, Copperbelt, Environmental, or Explore Manually from the first-run panel.

Startup performance: the macOS shortcut ./scripts/dev-fresh.sh clears the Vite cache and pulls any configured keys straight from the Keychain. It starts keyless too.

Then power it up — in the app, not in a file

Keys are upgrades, not prerequisites. When you want one, click the POWER UP chip in the bottom-right corner: Provider Settings lists every supported key, what it switches on, and where to get it. Paste, hit SAVE KEYS, and the app restarts itself with the new capability on. Once everything is configured the chip reads POWERED UP.

  • What to get first: the free Cesium ion token for photorealistic 3D and world terrain; a commodity-pricing key when you want a live ticker instead of delayed quotes; OpenAI when you want to talk to the deposit map.
  • Keys you already have stay yours: values from your shell or the macOS Keychain show as configured externally and are read-only to the panel.

🕐 The First Five Minutes

Choose a first-run mission, or try these in order.

  1. Light up the Copperbelt. Take the Live Shipments mission (or turn on Bulk Carriers yourself) — vessels gliding on real AIS telemetry, laden with ore, detection mesh already reading the scene. Click one: the camera locks on, a wake trail draws behind it, and its cargo manifest card comes up.
  2. Take the controls. Hit PIT CAB on a tracked haul truck and ride it down the bench, switching sensors mid-descent: Geological into Ore-Grade Thermal.
  3. Drop into an open pit. Search one and descend to the benches with 3D equipment on — grounded haul trucks, dust trails, the whole pit working in real time.
  4. Look through a public site camera. Turn on Site Cameras over a mining region with public traffic-cam or port coverage. Cycle coverage to VIEWSHED and every camera draws its estimated coverage volume — where it reaches, and where it goes blind.
  5. Track a shipment. Turn on Bulk Carriers and click a laden ore ship — you ride along at sea-level distance, wake trail and all, all the way to the discharge port.
  6. Switch the optics. Tap 1–5 — Satellite, Geological, Ore-Grade Thermal, Night, Seismic — and the whole live globe re-renders through a different sensor.
  7. Talk to it (needs an OpenAI key): "Take me to the Pilbara and select the nearest laden bulk carrier."
  8. Come home. Hit Reset Globe — or just say "zoom out to a globe view."

Keyboard: 1–5 sensor styles · H HUD · D detection · C pit cab · Esc out.

⛏️ The Pit Cab

Every open pit should let you do this.

Real-time pit-cab mode, built from live production data: the camera rides your tracked haul truck with real bench terrain holding underneath, all the way down the ramp — sensor styles come along for the ride, and the site roster keeps every nearby unit one click away: jump truck to truck and fall straight into the next cab.

The cab even carries its own briefing strip: nearby live signals, regional commodity headlines, and real local weather — with an opt-in dust-haze mode that renders volumetric particulate from actual site conditions.

Why pit-cab mode exists: you're riding a real operation over real terrain — and you get to pick which sensor you see the world through.

🎙️ Talk to It

Voice needs an OpenAI key. Without one the entire app still runs — the mic button just reports voice is unavailable. The same key drives the AI HUD summary: a terse, five-word intelligence-style readout of the current view that regenerates as you move.

Click GEV MIC, grant the microphone, and just talk. This is more than a voice-controlled remote:

  • 🧠 It knows what it's looking at. The agent pulls live scene context before answering — including commodity, tenement, active layers, and view scale. Ask "what's mined here?" mid-flight and it knows.
  • 🎯 Entity Q&A. Click any mine, carrier, or refinery and ask "what's this?" It answers using the object's live production or cargo telemetry.
  • 👁️ Visual grounding. At site level, it reads a viewport screenshot to identify legible signage and facility names, and is instructed never to hallucinate labels.
  • 🎬 Cinematic framing. "Show me the carriers headed to Qingdao" pulls the camera back, angles it, and frames the live traffic like a director.
  • 🔒 Honest and secure. The agent only confirms actions that succeeded. Your OPENAI_API_KEY never touches the browser; the client only gets a short-lived session token.

Direct it — drone-operator camera verbs: "Take me to the Atacama." · "Orbit around this pit slowly." · "Draw the haul route from the crusher to the stockpile." → "Fly the route we just drew." · "Zoom out to a globe view."

Annotate it — a whiteboard over the real world: "Outline this tenement." · "Annotate the processing plant and its footprint" — it draws the actual enclosing boundary, not a circle. · "How far is the mine from the nearest port?" — a connector arrow appears and it speaks the distance. Everything persists until you say "clear the map."

Or draw it yourself — DISPLAY ▸ Draw: pick Area, Line or Pin, click the vertices on the real world, double-click to finish, label it. Same whiteboard, same persistence, no microphone needed.

Interrogate it — analyst queries against the live layers: "How many bulk carriers are headed to Rotterdam right now?" · "Which mines are producing cobalt today?" · "What's the copper price done this week?" · "Is anything flagged for a tailings-dam alert?" · "When does the next shipment clear Port Hedland?"

Operate it — the whole console, hands-free: "Switch to ore-grade thermal and turn on the deposits layer." · "Turn on the camera viewsheds." · "Play the commodity ticker out loud." · "Track that carrier." → "Enter Pit Cab."

And the rapid-fire tier — one sentence each: "Show me global supply chains." · "Play Copperbelt Watch." · "Set detection density to fifty percent." · "Next contact — bulk carriers only." · "Show me active deposits." · "Switch to OSM." · "Switch to the tactical layout." · "What's turned on right now?"

  • 🚢 Follow the ore. Click-track a laden bulk carrier out of a copper port, ride it across open ocean, and watch it discharge at the destination smelter.
  • ⛏️ Bench by bench. Click-track a haul truck loading at the face, hop into the pit cab, and ride it up to the crusher.

  • 💎 Deposit to development. Select an undeveloped deposit, read its resource estimate, then check whether a rail or port corridor already reaches it.
  • 📈 Price the move. Turn on the commodity ticker, watch copper or lithium move, then jump straight to the mines producing that commodity today.

  • 🚶 Ask for a route 🎙️ "Draw the haul route from the pit to the stockpile" — then "fly the route" for a banked, eased camera tour.
  • 📏 Measure port to port 🎙️ "How far is Port Hedland from Qingdao?" — an arrow spans the ocean and the distance lands in the caption.

🔧 Under the Hood

How the globe handles live data:

  • World-stable icons. Vessels and vehicles point along their true real-world heading at every camera angle — tracked or not — via per-frame screen-space course projection. No spinning, no viewport-locking.
  • Smooth motion from choppy data. Live feeds arrive every 15–30s; the globe renders one interval behind real time and interpolates between known fixes. Dead reckoning fills the gaps.
  • Sits on the real ground. Entity heights are aligned to work with 3D tiles, so haul trucks sit on benches and cameras stand on real vantage points instead of floating.
  • Caching and request budgets. Provider-specific request governors and disk-cached reference data reduce repeated requests. These controls do not replace provider quotas or billing controls.
  • Server-side credentials. Every API that touches a private key (voice, AIS, market data, camera frames) is brokered through a hardened server-side proxy with SSRF protection, response caps, and sanitized errors. The only keys the browser sees are the map-tile providers (restrict both at the provider).
  • No framework. Vanilla JavaScript, CesiumJS, and Vite — plus 3D Tiles for the planet and the OpenAI Realtime API for voice. Fast to read, fast to hack on.

💸 What It Actually Costs

Honest numbers, roughly — always check the provider pricing pages:

  • 🟢 Most layers — $0, no signup. Mines, deposits, seismicity, public site cameras, rail routes, bundled datasets.
  • 🟡 The free-key tier — $0 with a signup. AIS, market data, plus Cesium ion for eligible personal/non-commercial use. Provider quotas and eligibility still apply.
  • 🗺️ 3D tiles — free through an eligible Cesium ion account within its quota; metered through a direct provider key.
  • 🔴 OpenAI voice — the one that costs real money, so the app meters it for you. A live session-spend readout sits next to the mic, with a spend warning and a hard cap that ends the session.

🔒 Sharing an Instance

By default nobody else can reach your server — it binds to localhost. To share on your LAN, opt in explicitly, but know that a LAN-visible server brokers your configured API keys to anyone who can reach it. Set per-IP throttles and, before anything else, configure provider quotas, usage limits, and billing alerts: app-level throttles are not billing caps.

Provider Settings is disabled when the server is shared, so remote users cannot access the key-entry panel.

📋 Responsible & Open

Ore's Eye View runs on public data, clear sources, and local-first execution. No secrets, no private datasets, no mystery scraping — anything involving a private key is brokered server-side.

The line. This project models mines, deposits, vessels, infrastructure, and markets. It does not build features for named-person search, workforce surveillance, or tracking individuals, and pull requests that cross that line won't be merged. People are not a query type here.

Come build it. This is a canvas: the layers here are the signals one person could find and fuse. Add a commodity pack, a data source, a style, a voice tool.

Status: An evolving open-source client for exploration and learning — a fast, hackable foundation, not a hardened production service.

Important. Ore's Eye View is an exploratory visualization of public and third-party data. Data may be delayed, incomplete, modeled, inferred, or wrong. Do not use it for investment decisions, operational or safety-critical mining decisions, environmental compliance, or navigation. Verify important information with authoritative sources.

🧭 What's Next

This is the baseline, and the whole point is for you to break things and bolt on layers we haven't thought of yet — a new commodity pack, a regional camera set, a better tailings feed. The floor is deliberately low: enough to get a real taste of minerals intelligence without ever talking to a sales team.

One heads-up from the inside: the present is the cheap part. The moment you try to go back in time — reconstructing what a deposit's production looked like a decade ago, or scrubbing a shipment's history at any real resolution — the data gets expensive and the compute gets brutal. That's the long game.

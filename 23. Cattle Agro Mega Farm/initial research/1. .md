

Cattle rearing covers a lot of ground, and the details shift depending on whether you're raising beef, dairy, or dual-purpose animals, and whether it's a small mixed farm or a commercial operation. Here are the main components.

**Land and housing**

- **Pasture or grazing land:** Cattle need enough good grazing. A rough rule in many tropical and subtropical areas is 1 to 2 hectares per animal, though this varies a lot with rainfall and grass quality. Rotating animals between paddocks keeps pasture healthy.
- **Shelter:** Shade and protection from heavy rain, wind, or extreme heat. Housing needs good ventilation, dry flooring, and easy cleaning.
- **Fencing:** Sturdy perimeter fencing (barbed wire, electric, or live fences) and handling facilities like a crush or race for vaccinations and checks.
- **Water:** Clean, reliable water is critical. An adult animal can drink 30 to 80 litres a day depending on heat and whether it's lactating.

**Feeding and nutrition**

- **Forage:** Grass, hay, silage, and crop residues form the base of the diet.
- **Supplements:** Concentrates, protein sources (like cottonseed cake or legumes), and minerals, especially salt and phosphorus, to fill gaps, particularly in the dry season.
- **Seasonal planning:** Many farmers store fodder (hay or silage) so animals don't lose condition when pasture runs short.
- **Calves:** They need colostrum in the first hours of life, then milk or milk replacer, and gradual introduction to solid feed.

**Breeding**

- **Choosing breeds:** Match breed to purpose and climate. For example, indigenous or hardy breeds tolerate heat and disease better, while exotic breeds like Holstein or Hereford often produce more but need more management.
- **Reproduction:** Natural mating with a good bull, or artificial insemination for improving genetics. Keeping records of heat cycles, calving dates, and lineage helps a lot.
- **Culling:** Removing poor performers keeps the herd productive.

**Animal health**

- **Vaccinations:** Common ones cover diseases like anthrax, blackleg, foot-and-mouth, and brucellosis, depending on what's prevalent locally.
- **Parasite control:** Regular dipping or spraying for ticks, plus deworming.
- **Monitoring:** Daily observation for signs of illness, lameness, or poor appetite, and a relationship with a local vet or animal health officer.
- **Biosecurity:** Quarantining new animals and limiting contact with outside herds.

**Labour and management**

- Daily tasks include feeding, watering, checking animals, cleaning, and milking (for dairy).
- Record-keeping covers weights, breeding, health treatments, and finances.
- Handling animals calmly reduces stress and injury for both people and cattle.

**Money and markets**

- **Startup costs:** Animals, land or lease, fencing, housing, and equipment.
- **Running costs:** Feed, veterinary care, labour, and water.
- **Markets:** Selling live animals, meat, milk, or manure, plus access to buyers, abattoirs, or dairy cooperatives.

**Regulations**
Depending on where you are, you may need registration, movement permits, or to follow animal identification and disease reporting rules.

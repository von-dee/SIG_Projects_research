
I'll research this across several angles: the leading insurers, their vendor partnerships, and the financial structures behind them.# How leading insurers use technology and AI to make money

I researched current sources (to October 2026). I did not research every top insurer: State Farm, GEICO, MetLife, Prudential and Zurich got little or no coverage here. Some figures come from secondary sites rather than company filings, and I flag those.

## The four ways tech turns into profit

1. **Better pricing and underwriting.** Insurers use richer data and models to cut the loss ratio.
2. **Lower operating cost.** Automating claims, service and software work reduces the expense ratio.
3. **More volume per underwriter.** AI lets the same people handle more submissions.
4. **Cheaper or more profitable capital.** Reinsurance, cat bonds and private credit structures lower the cost of holding risk or raise the return on assets.

Most of the profit comes from the first, second and fourth. AI is the newest lever, but pricing data and capital structure still matter more to the total.

## Company by company

**Progressive: data moat in auto**

- Its Snapshot telematics program feeds driving behavior directly into pricing. It was the first insurer to adopt telematics and had 3 million customers in the program by 2015.
- About half of direct new-business customers still choose Snapshot.
- Q1 2026 brought 39.6 million policies in force, $23.6 billion in net premiums written and a combined ratio of 86.4. A second source gave 86.0, so treat it as the mid-80s.
- The business concept is that proprietary behavioral data lets Progressive price risk more accurately than rivals and grow without sacrificing margin.

**Chubb: AI to cut expenses**

- Chubb plans to cut its workforce by as much as 20% over three to four years, targeting savings equal to about 1.5 points off its combined ratio.
- Its combined ratio hit a record 85.7%, and Chubb Digital grew 27% to $1.4 billion of premium with an underwriting profit.
- This is the clearest example of an insurer building AI savings into its forecast.

**AIG: AI underwriting at scale (Palantir + Anthropic)**

- Lexington, AIG's excess and surplus lines unit, bound policies on only about 2% of roughly 300,000 new-business submissions in 2024. Underwriter time was the bottleneck.
- AIG built the "AIG Assist" triage tool. Lexington's submissions rose 26% and its middle-market property submit-to-bind ratio improved 35%.
- AIG is now building a multi-agent system with Palantir and Anthropic, with an orchestration layer coordinating specialized underwriting agents.
- It also uses the technology as a product. Lloyd's Syndicate 2479, run by Talbot, began underwriting a $300 million portfolio in January 2026, with LLM agents working inside a structured data environment. An AI-powered vehicle was set up with Amwins and Blackstone. AIG uses AI to attract outside capital and earn fees as well as underwriting profit.

**Allianz: claims agents (Anthropic)**

- Project Nemo uses seven coordinated agents on food spoilage claims, cutting processing time by 80%. A human adjuster still makes the final payment decision.
- The Anthropic partnership covers an internal AI platform, custom claims agents for motor and health, and audit-trail systems for regulators. Allianz is also planning to cut 1,500 to 1,800 travel-insurance jobs.

**Travelers: AI for the workforce**

- Travelers is deploying Claude assistants to nearly 10,000 engineers, analysts and product staff, and launched a generative AI voice agent for first notice of loss in February 2026. That source is a secondary site, so verify against Travelers' filings.

**Allstate**

- Allstate reported a 21.3% expense ratio in Q1 2026, and says its internal "ALLIE" agent platform writes a third of its code.
- It uses generative AI to draft about 50,000 claims messages a day, and photo-based damage estimation handles roughly half of driveable-vehicle claims. That figure comes from a secondary blog.

**Lemonade: AI-native**

- In-force premium per employee passed $1 million, nearly triple four years ago. The gross loss ratio fell to 60% in Q2 2026 from 67% a year earlier.
- It is still loss-making. The net loss was $79 million in the first half of 2026, and higher reinsurance costs can compress net margins.

**Ping An: ecosystem model**

- It spends over RMB 10 billion a year on technology, and AI agents helped generate RMB 57.3 billion in sales in H1 2026.
- Its AI Doctor was used by 9.7 million users, and the Auto Owner app has over 120 million active users.
- The concept is to own the customer relationship through health and auto services, then sell insurance into it.

**Reinsurers: Swiss Re and Munich Re**

- Swiss Re built a GenAI underwriting assistant on Microsoft Azure OpenAI and rolled out Microsoft 365 Copilot to 3,000 users.
- Munich Re runs a product-design copilot used by 50 clients and a claims-review tool it says cuts handling time by up to 50%.

**Others**

- AXA renewed its five-year Shift Technology agreement across 15 countries for fraud detection.
- Guardian signed a seven-year deal with HCLTech.

## Financial engineering

- **Cat bonds and alternative capital.** ILS capital reached a record $144.5 billion, with $24.9 billion of cat bond issuance in the 12 months to June 2026. Sidecars reached about $23 billion, and ILS is moving into casualty lines. Insurers pass peak risk to capital markets and keep fees. Abundant capital is also pushing reinsurance pricing down at the steepest pace in a decade, which helps buyers and squeezes reinsurers.
- **Private credit and offshore reinsurance in life.** Apollo built Athene by shifting assets toward higher-yielding asset-backed securities, reinsuring in Bermuda, and sourcing much of the credit from Apollo affiliates, which generates extra fees. US life insurers held about $807 billion of private credit and illiquid assets in 2025, about 20% of fixed income, with KKR- and Apollo-backed insurers growing fastest.
- **The risk.** Critics such as Steve Eisman call this a slow-brewing scandal that could one day trigger a financial crisis. That is an investor's warning, not an established outcome, but regulators are watching.

## New revenue streams

- **Insuring AI itself.** Munich Re covers companies that build and use AI models, and its head of AI insurance thinks the market could eclipse cyber. Armilla, a Lloyd's coverholder, raised standalone AI liability limits to $25 million as traditional insurers add exclusions.
- **Digital distribution:** Chubb Digital, as above.
- **Selling the technology and data:** Munich Re's platform to clients, AIG's syndicate and capital vehicles, and Progressive's telematics data.

## Caveats

- AI is mostly assistive for now. Insurers are focusing on guardrails and governance before deploying autonomous AI, and only 16% of surveyed consumers would trust AI to set premiums or renewals.
- Much of the cost saving is headcount reduction, which carries regulatory and reputational risk.
- Earnings are noisy. Allstate's Q1 2026 combined ratio improvement was driven mainly by reserve releases and lower catastrophe losses, not structural technology gains. Don't credit all margin gains to AI.
- Industry-wide savings estimates (such as 20 to 40% cost cuts) come from vendor and consultant sources and should be treated as directional.

Every insurer earns money from some mix of four sources:

- Premiums.
- Investment income on the float, which is premiums held until claims are paid.
- Fees, commissions and service revenue.

## Progressive

**Products and services:** personal and commercial autos and trucks, motorcycles, boats, RVs and homes. It is the second largest personal auto insurer in the US. It sells through its website, phone, app and independent agents. The Snapshot telematics program is an add-on that prices policies on driving behavior.

**Revenue contribution:**

- Net premiums written were $83.2 billion in 2025, up 12%.
- Personal Lines produced $72.6 billion of net premiums written (about 87%) and Commercial Lines $10.6 billion (about 13%). Personal Lines includes auto, special lines (such as motorcycle and boat) and property.
- Commercial Lines premiums fell 3%, partly because some rideshare (TNC) policies were not renewed.

**Revenue sources:** Net premiums earned were $81.66 billion and investment income was $3.58 billion, up from $2.83 billion in 2024. Fees and service revenues make up the rest. A secondary source puts total revenue at about $87.6 billion. Underwriting is the dominant profit source, with investment income a distant second.

## Chubb

**Products and services:** commercial P&C (property, casualty, specialty), personal lines for high-net-worth clients, agricultural insurance, overseas general insurance (commercial and consumer), global reinsurance, and life insurance. Chubb Digital grew 27% in 2025 to $1.4 billion in premium and generated an underwriting profit.

**Revenue contribution (FY2025 net premiums written):** total $54.8 billion, made up of North America Commercial $21.3B, North America Personal $7.0B, North America Agricultural $2.9B, Overseas General $15.0B, Global Reinsurance $1.3B and Life $7.3B.

My calc. of share of premium: NA Commercial 39%, Overseas General 27%, Life 13%, NA Personal 13%, Agricultural 5%, Reinsurance 2%.

**Profit contribution:** segment income was $14.2B in total, with NA Commercial at $7.56B, Overseas General $3.17B, Life $1.24B, NA Personal $1.05B, Global Reinsurance $634M and Agricultural $577M. My calc.: NA Commercial is about 53% of segment income, so profit is more concentrated than premium.

**Revenue sources:** underwriting income, which was a record $6.53 billion for P&C in 2025, with a combined ratio of 85.7%, plus net investment income of about $7.0 billion across segments (my sum). Life contributes premiums and investment spread.

## AIG

Its units are North America Commercial, International Commercial (including Lloyd's activity via Talbot) and Global Personal (high-net-worth). Lexington is its excess and surplus lines carrier. It also earns from third-party capital vehicles such as Lloyd's Syndicate 2479.

**Revenue contribution (2025 net premiums written):** North America Commercial $8.76B, International Commercial $8.66B, Global Personal $6.25B, total General Insurance $23.7B. My calc.: about 37%, 37% and 26%. Global Commercial was $17.4 billion.

**Profit contribution:** General Insurance underwriting income was $2.3 billion with a combined ratio of 90.1%. North America Commercial more than doubled underwriting income to about $1.1 billion, while Global Personal underwriting income fell 50% to $70 million.

## Allianz

**Products and services:** property-casualty (retail motor, home, commercial, Allianz Partners travel/assistance/health), life and health, and asset management (PIMCO and Allianz Global Investors).

**Revenue contribution:**

- Group operating profit was a record €17.4B on total business volume of €186.9B.
- P&C operating profit was €9.0B and life/health €5.6B. My calc.: about 52% and 32% of group operating profit, with the remaining ~€2.8B being asset management net of corporate items.
- P&C business volume was €86.7B, and asset management operating revenues were €8.5B.

**Revenue sources:** insurance premiums, investment margins in life/health, and fee income from asset management. Third-party assets under management reached €1.99 trillion, with management fees on that as the revenue driver.

## Travelers

**Products and services:** commercial insurance for small and mid-sized businesses, bond and specialty (surety, management liability), and personal auto and homeowners. Business Insurance includes Select Accounts small commercial, core Middle Market and National Property. It sold most Canadian operations to Definity.

**Revenue contribution (2025 net written premiums):** total $44.4B, with Bond & Specialty $4.3B and Personal Insurance $17.5B. By subtraction, Business Insurance is about $22.6B (my calc.), which is roughly 51%, with Personal at 39% and Bond & Specialty at 10%.

## Allstate

**Products and services:** auto, homeowners and other personal lines (under several brands), plus Protection Services: consumer product protection plans, vehicle service contracts and GAP, roadside assistance, telematics analytics (Arity) and identity protection. Allstate has also been selling employer and group health and benefits businesses.

**Revenue contribution:**

- Total 2025 revenue was $67.7B.
- Allstate reports auto and homeowners separately. In Q4, auto premiums earned were $9.62B and homeowners $4.06B, out of $14.8B in Property-Liability earned premiums. My calc.: about 65% auto and 27% homeowners.

## Lemonade

**Products and services:** renters, homeowners, pet, term life and car insurance, including an autonomous-vehicle car product.

**Revenue contribution (Q2 2026, total revenue $294.4M):** net earned premium $252.0M, ceding commission income $20.4M, net investment income $9.7M, and commission and other income $12.3M. My calc.: about 86%, 7%, 3% and 4%.

## Ping An

**Products and services:** four product categories: protection, asset management, credit and service. It owns life and health insurance, P&C, Ping An Bank, asset management, and health and technology businesses. Ping An Good Doctor runs AI-assisted medical services, and the Auto Owner app serves over 120 million users.

**Revenue contribution:**

## AXA

**Products and services:** P&C (personal and commercial, including AXA XL for large risks), life, savings, health and protection. It agreed to buy 51% of Prima, a digital Italian motor insurer. It sold its asset manager AXA IM in July 2025.

**Revenue contribution (2025 gross written premiums and other revenues, €116B):** Property & Casualty €58.0B, Life & Health €56.5B, Asset Management €0.9B. My calc.: about 50%, 49% and under 1%. **Revenue sources:** premiums, health and protection margins, unit-linked fees, and investment income.

## Swiss Re

**Products and services:** reinsurance for insurers (P&C Reinsurance and Life & Health Reinsurance) and Corporate Solutions, which insures large corporates directly. It has been selling iptiQ businesses.

## Munich Re

**Products and services:** reinsurance (P&C, life and health, Global Specialty Insurance) and ERGO, its primary insurer in Germany and Europe. It also offers AI performance insurance (aiSure) and runs a product-design platform for clients.

**Revenue sources:** reinsurance premiums, ERGO primary premiums, and investment income. The group targets an investment return above 3.5% for 2026.

## Guardian Life

**Products and services:** life, disability, dental, vision and retirement solutions, serving roughly 12 million people, plus workplace benefits. Its broker-dealer subsidiary, Park Avenue Securities, provides investing and wealth management.

**Revenue contribution:** I could not retrieve segment figures. Its financial highlights page lists premiums, fees and other income, but I did not get the numbers. Check Guardian's annual report for the split.

**Revenue sources:** group benefits and individual life premiums, annuity and fund deposits, investment income, and securities fees.

## Patterns across the group

- **Premiums are the largest source everywhere.** For example, Lemonade's net earned premium is about 86% of its revenue, and Progressive's premiums earned far exceed its $3.58B of investment income.
- **Profit is more concentrated than premium.** Chubb's North America Commercial unit is 39% of premium but 53% of segment income, and Munich Re's ERGO is 36% of revenue but 15% of profit.
- **Non-insurance revenue matters at three companies:** Allianz (asset management fees), Ping An (banking and health services) and Allstate (protection plans and Arity).
- **Reinsurers earn from risk transfer rather than customer-facing products,** and the margin is volatile with catastrophe losses.

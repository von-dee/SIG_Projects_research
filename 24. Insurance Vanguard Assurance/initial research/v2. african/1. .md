
# African insurers: technology, partnerships, products and revenue

This follows the same approach as the global research, but the picture differs. South Africa has large listed groups with full segment reporting. Elsewhere, data is thinner and the technology story is mostly distribution (mobile money, microinsurance) rather than frontier AI. I found no announced partnerships between African insurers and Anthropic, OpenAI or Palantir. Ghana's market data is several years old, and some figures come from secondary sites.

## Who is largest

On 2024 premiums, Sanlam leads the continent in life premiums at $5.04 billion, followed by Old Mutual Life, Liberty, Momentum Metropolitan and Discovery Life. In non-life, Santam leads with $1.89 billion, ahead of Hollard, Old Mutual Insure, Guardrisk and OUTsurance. Moroccan and Egyptian groups (Wafa, RMA, Misr) fill much of the rest of the top 30. South Africa dominates.

## Company by company

**Sanlam**

- **Products:** life and health insurance, general insurance (Santam, SanlamAllianz), investment management, and credit and structuring.
- **Revenue contribution (2025 net result from financial services, R15.9B):** life and health R9.26B (about 58%), general insurance R3.58B (22%), credit and structuring R2.27B (14%), investment management R1.51B (10%), less R669M corporate costs.
- **Technology:** technology and AI is one of its stated enablers. It plans a South African banking and rewards ecosystem with GoTyme Bank, and its corporate costs include client-system modernisation.
- **Financial engineering:** Allianz holds 49% of SanlamAllianz, the pan-African joint venture, with Sanlam at 51%. Sanlam also increased its stakes in Shriram's Indian ecosystem, with MUFG buying 20% of Shriram Finance.

**Old Mutual**

- **Products:** life and savings, Old Mutual Insure (short-term), asset management, banking (OM Bank, launched August 2025) and Africa Regions.
- **Revenue contribution (2025 results from operations, R9.8B):** Insure about R2.06B, Africa Regions about R1.7B and asset management about R1.54B. My calc.: roughly 21%, 17% and 16%. Malawi is the largest regional earner.
- **Technology:** it is expanding AI and data analytics in underwriting and claims. OM Bank had about 284,000 customers by December 2025, but Old Mutual wrote its value down by R3B.

**Discovery**

- **Products:** health, life, Discovery Insure (motor), Discovery Bank and the Vitality wellness programme.
- **Revenue contribution (FY to June 2026, normalised operating profit R17.75B):** South Africa R13.87B and Vitality R3.88B. My calc.: about 78% and 22%. Discovery Bank made its first full-year profit of R370M.
- **Partnerships:** the Vitality shared-value model is exported through a network of insurers: AIA, Ping An, Sumitomo, John Hancock, Manulife and Vitality UK.
- **Disruption:** it rewards healthy and safe behaviour. Engaged Discovery Insure clients have 74% lower road fatality rates, according to Discovery.

**Momentum**

- **Revenue contribution (FY to June 2026, normalised headline earnings R7.06B):** Investments R1.19B, Guardrisk R1.04B, Retail R1.01B, Insure R474M, Health R367M, India R22M. Insurance revenue was R61.4B.
- **Technology:** a systems migration across three major businesses should save over R100M a year. Metropolitan's turnaround combined cost cuts and digital transformation.
- **Exits:** it sold its Ghanaian business in September 2025 and is exiting Mozambique.

**Santam (Sanlam subsidiary)**

- **Products:** broker, client and partner personal and commercial insurance, MiWay (direct), Santam Specialist Solutions, Santam Re and alternative risk transfer.
- **Revenue contribution (2025 gross written premium R43.96B):** the net insurance result rose 61% to R5.26B and the combined ratio was 88.7%. South Africa is 81% of premium.
- **Vendor partnership:** Santam went live on Guidewire's cloud platform in June 2026, with plans to expand AI in underwriting, claims and customer service.
- **Financial engineering:** Santam Syndicate 1918 at Lloyd's began underwriting in January 2026 with approved capacity above £300 million.

**OUTsurance**

- **Revenue contribution (FY to June 2026, insurance revenue R41.49B):** Youi (Australia) R25.24B, South Africa R14.36B, Ireland R521M. My calc.: about 61%, 35% and 1%. Profit was R6.18B.
- **Technology:** efficiency from proprietary systems and AI. The CEO says customers still prefer humans for complex claims, so it keeps hiring call-centre agents.
- **Capital:** it is injecting €70 million into Ireland over five years; Ireland is still loss-making.

**Nigeria (AIICO, AXA Mansard, NEM, Cornerstone, Leadway)**

- **Revenue:** AIICO led 2025 with N98.3B revenue, NEM had N96.4B in gross premium and AXA Mansard N71.8B. AXA Mansard's 2025 profit fell 78.9% to N5.46B, then rose 14% to N7.8B in H1 2026.
- **Regulation as the main driver:** the NIIRA 2025 reform set minimum capital of N10B (life), N15B (non-life) and N35B (reinsurers). Forty-three insurers passed the July 2026 deadline and eight were still waiting. Composites must also split into life and non-life entities within five years.
- **Technology:** Leadway Direct offers digital quotes and claims tracking. Curacel's AI claims platform is used by AXA Mansard, Liberty Health, Old Mutual and Jubilee Health.

**Kenya (Britam, Jubilee, CIC)**

- Insurance penetration is only about 2.1 to 2.4%. Insurers are targeting the informal sector through pay-as-you-go and embedded products.
- Jubilee Health partnered with bolttech's African unit to distribute health cover through banks, petrol stations and retailers. CIC's micro-insurance arm partnered with the Philippines' CARD MBA. Britam Connect develops embedded products for gig workers and small businesses.
- Mobile money reached 83% of adults in 2024. A secondary source says microinsurance premiums jumped to KSh 2.17 billion from KSh 234 million, with Britam at 75% market share. I could not verify it against the regulator.

**Morocco (Wafa Assurance)**

- 2025 premium income was MAD 15.2 billion (about $1.64 billion), up 10.9%, helped by Egyptian acquisitions including a 63.39% stake in Delta Insurance.
- In H1 2025, non-life premiums were MAD 4.03B and life MAD 3.86B, with MAD 66B of investments behind them.
- Its Egyptian life arm leverages Wafa's digital transformation. Sanlam Morocco and Allianz Morocco agreed merger terms in March 2026.

**Ghana (Hollard Ghana, Enterprise, SIC)**

- The market data I found is dated (2018 to 2021). Enterprise Life, SIC Life, Star and SIC Insurance led by premium or assets.
- Hollard Ghana is the technology standout. It launched a USSD micro-life product with Cassava Fintech and Vodafone and sells on Jumia. It also has an AI chatbot ("Araba"). Its partner ETAP became Ghana's first licensed insurtech.

## New revenue streams and financial engineering

- **Parametric sovereign cover.** African Risk Capacity's 2025 profit was a record $13.5M on $48.6M of gross written premium (up 26%). It launched Africa's first sovereign flood policy in January 2026, with Madagascar the first buyer. Since 2014 it has issued over 300 sovereign policies and paid out over $250M. Donor funding subsidises premiums.
- **Captives.** NSIA Group received approval to create Manzi Re, a captive reinsurer.
- **Lloyd's access.** Santam's syndicate gives it London-market capacity for specialist classes.
- **Capital recycling:** Old Mutual's R3B buyback, Momentum's R1B buyback and discretionary capital returns.
- **Cross-border consolidation:** SanlamAllianz, Wafa in Egypt, and exits such as Momentum from Ghana.

## Caveats

- Segment bases differ (results from operations, normalised headline earnings, net result from financial services), so compare only within a company.
- The Old Mutual and Kenyan microinsurance figures come from trade press and a LinkedIn post, not filings.
- Many insurers (Liberty, Hollard SA, AVBOB, Egyptian groups) I could not research in depth.

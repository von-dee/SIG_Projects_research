
Here are suggestions beyond the ones already in your document, grouped by the problem they solve. They are ideas to test in discovery and pilots, not validated needs.

## Strengthen adoption and trust (highest priority)

These matter most because your own risk table flags integration, stale data and sustained adoption as the likeliest reasons the Product fails.

- **Data health dashboard.** One screen showing every source, its last update, delay and status, so users and admins can see at a glance what is current and what is stale.
- **Confidence and accuracy indicator.** Show the alignment error and model age on screen, so users know when to trust the overlay.
- **Meeting capture.** Automatically save a session summary with participants, layers shown, annotations, decisions and actions, then export it as a PDF or share a link. This removes the manual slide-building that planners dislike and gives you a retention hook.
- **Action tracker.** Pin actions to locations on the model, assign owners and due dates, and carry open actions into the next weekly review. This fits the weekly review you chose as the entry use case.
- **Guided onboarding and demo mode.** A built-in sample site so new users can learn the tools without touching live data.

## Field and underground use

- **Low-bandwidth mode.** Lighter models and compressed streams for weak links, with a clear indicator of the current quality level.
- **Offline site packs.** Let a user download a specific area and its layers to a tablet before going underground.
- **Voice commands and hands-free controls** for headset users wearing gloves or carrying equipment.
- **Rugged-device and hard-hat profiles** that adjust brightness, contrast and font size for glare, dust and low light.
- **Checklist walk-throughs.** Step users through an inspection route, with each point tied to a place on the model and a photo or note captured at that point.

## Planning and operations

- **Blast and dig-sequence preview** that steps through the planned sequence over time (builds on 4D playback).
- **Shift handover view.** A summary of what changed since the last shift: new annotations, equipment moves, open hazards and open actions.
- **Measurement tools.** Distance, area, volume and height measurements on the model, clearly labelled with the model's accuracy.
- **Section and cut-away views** for seeing inside a pit wall, stockpile or underground drive.
- **Compare two dates.** Side-by-side or overlay of two scans to show what changed (stockpile movement, wall changes, progress).

## Safety and compliance (build carefully)

- **Hazard and permit register on the map.** Active permits, exclusion zones and known hazards shown as layers, with expiry dates.
- **Emergency drill mode.** Replay a scenario for a crew and record who took part. Treat it as training support, not a rescue tool.
- **Incident replay and review.** Rebuild the state of the site around an incident time for an investigation team.
- **Evidence packs.** Export time-stamped views and records for audits. Check with your legal reviewer what regulators will actually accept.

Alerts carry the highest liability in this group. Your document already ranks them low in the roadmap, and I would keep them there until latency and false-alarm rates are tested.

## Remote assist and service (your fastest-sale segment)

- **Annotated snapshots and recorded sessions** so a fix can be reused as training.
- **Parts and manual overlay.** Show a part number, exploded view or procedure on the machine when the OEM supplies the data.
- **Expert scheduling.** Let a dealer see which experts are free and bring one into a session in minutes.
- **Callout log with avoided-trip counter.** This also produces the hard-savings evidence you need for pricing.

## Commercial and platform features

- **Value reporting.** An automatic report showing sessions, hours, callouts avoided and the pilot KPIs, so a champion can defend renewal.
- **Multi-site view.** A head-office view across several sites, relevant for group-level buyers.
- **Guest access for contractors, insurers and advisors.** Time-limited, read-only links to selected layers only, which supports your secondary segments without exposing everything.
- **Open connector kit.** Documented connectors and an API so clients or integrators can add their own sources. This helps with the integration risk and reduces your custom work.
- **Ghana-oriented options.** Boundary and concession layers, and support for local coordinate systems. Boundary monitoring against illegal small-scale mining is a possible use, but it needs a clear data source, such as satellite or drone imagery, and legal review before you mention it to anyone.

## Where I would start

If I had to pick five to test first, I would choose **meeting capture with an action tracker**, the **data health dashboard**, the **accuracy and freshness indicators**, a **callout log with avoided-trip counter**, and **guest access**. They are comparatively low risk, they make the Product's value visible, and they help produce the measurements your pricing model depends on.

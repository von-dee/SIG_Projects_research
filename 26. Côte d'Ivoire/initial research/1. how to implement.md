The ExcaVATORE name didn't turn up in public sources, so I've treated it as the Ghana equipment-tracking programme under LI 2404. ## What Ghana's model rests on

- **Legal basis:** LI 2404 is the Minerals and Mining (Mineral Operations, Tracking of Earth-Moving and Mining Equipment) Regulations, 2020. It requires all mining-related earth-moving equipment to be registered and operated only within licensed mining concessions.
- **Registration:** Owners must state the purpose and area of use, and unregistered excavators are liable to seizure and confiscation. District assemblies recorded the owner's details, entry date, registration and chassis numbers, and the operator's details.
- **Control room:** The 24/7 Network Control Centre at the Minerals Commission also tracks explosive-carrying vehicles and runs a call centre for complaints.
- **Geo-fencing logic:** The software pairs each excavator with one licensed small-scale concession at a time, with a 15 metre buffer around it. Concession site-plan coordinates were loaded into the Ghana Mine Repository and Tracking software.
- **Field marking:** Once a tracker is fitted, a sticker with the control room's phone number goes on the machine.
- **Import control:** Permits for imported equipment are granted only after the Minerals Commission and DVLA verify the intended end-use. DVLA and the Commission run a 24-hour office at Tema Port.
- **Scale-up:** The control centre was set up in December 2022, and by early 2023 it was monitoring 75 excavators. By the latest update, 4,300 units were registered at Tema Port, with trackers on 1,864.
- **Inter-agency set-up:** Lands, Transport, GRA, DVLA and the Minerals Commission carry out the programme together.

## Points to note

1. **The law came first.** The tracking system enforces a regulation that makes registration and in-concession operation mandatory. Without an equivalent legal basis in Côte d'Ivoire, a tracker is only a monitoring tool with no enforcement power. This is the biggest dependency in the project.
2. **It is more than a technology project.** Ghana's results depended on customs, port, vehicle licensing, local government and security agencies all acting together. The control room only flags; others have to act on the flag.
3. **Concession data quality drives everything.** Geo-fencing works only if licence boundaries are digitised and accurate. Ghana had to load site-plan coordinates for every legal concession.
4. **Ghana started small.** It went from about 40 to 75 machines in the pilot phase to thousands later. This supports your pilot-first approach.
5. **Enforcement is the weak point.** More than 3,000 excavators registered under the programme remain unclaimed at Tema Port after geo-fencing was introduced, which suggests some owners may be avoiding a regime that restricts where machines can work. Expect Côte d'Ivoire to ask about this, so have an honest answer ready.
6. **Sustainability was flagged early.** The Lands Minister asked the Commission for a sustainability plan for the initiative. Cost-recovery and funding of device, airtime and control-room costs should be in your proposal from the start.
7. **Be careful with claims.** Don't promise "real-time flagging" or drone coverage figures unless you can back them with Ghana performance data. The delegation will compare notes with what they saw in Accra.

## Things to discuss

**Legal and institutional**
- Which Ivorian law governs small-scale and semi-industrial mining, and does it cover equipment registration and tracking? If not, who would draft the regulatory instrument, and by when?
- Which body would own the system and the control room, and how would it work with other agencies?
- Who would handle equipment imports and customs, and could an import-permit step be added as in Ghana?

**Data and operations**
- What is the state of licence and permit data (digitised, GPS-referenced, complete)?
- How many machines are involved, how are they distributed, and which zones are the priority, including border and forest areas?
- Which agencies would respond to alerts (police, gendarmerie, environment, water and forests), and what is the protocol after an alert?
- How would the system cover machines already in the field, and how would tagging teams be protected?

**Technical**
- What mobile coverage and power exist in the mining zones, and what device specification does that require?
- Data hosting and sovereignty: should data sit in Côte d'Ivoire?
- System interoperability with existing mining cadastre or licensing databases.
- Language and interface: French-language system and training.

**Commercial and delivery**
- Who funds the needs assessment, the pilot and the rollout?
- Procurement route (direct award, tender, or partnership) and expected timeline.
- Who owns the system and the data at the end, and what training and handover are planned?
- Pilot scope: which regions, how many machines, how long, and what success metrics?

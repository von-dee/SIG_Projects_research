
These are the benefits Côte d'Ivoire could reasonably expect, and I've separated what Ghana's experience supports from what is still a projection.

## Benefits

**1. Enforcement against illegal mining**

- Machines can be tied to a specific licence and flagged when they leave it, so the authority no longer depends on patrols and tip-offs alone.
- Registration at import and the permit step make it harder to bring in equipment with no legitimate use. Ghana's approach checks the intended end-use before equipment is released.
- The sticker and control-room number gives communities and officials a visible way to tell a legal machine from an untracked one and to report it.

**2. Environmental protection**

- Less damage to rivers, forests and farmland from machines operating where they shouldn't.
- Faster detection of new illegal sites, especially if drone imagery is added.
- A clearer link between a machine, its owner and the damage it causes, which supports holding owners responsible for reclamation. Ghana's Minerals Commission cited this accountability goal when it launched the programme.

**3. Revenue and formalisation**

- A reliable register of mining equipment and where it works gives the state a clearer picture of the sector, which helps with licensing, royalties and tax collection.
- Operators have a stronger incentive to get licensed, since an unlicensed machine can't work freely.
- Ghana's minister linked the sharp rise in small-scale gold exports (from about 3,430 kg in 2021 to about 22,158 kg in 2022) to a combination of measures, including a tax cut and tracking. That is not evidence that tracking alone caused it, so I'd avoid presenting it to them as a proven result.

**4. Better data for decision-making**

- One system holding licences, equipment and movements allows planning of priority zones and resource allocation.
- Records of equipment activity help in disputes, prosecutions and licence renewals.

**5. Security and safety**

- Tracking can extend to other high-risk items. Ghana's control centre also monitors explosive-carrying vehicles, which matters for safety after incidents such as the Appiatse explosion.
- Fewer confrontations in the field, because enforcement is targeted rather than based on broad patrols.

**6. Institutional and political gains**

- Shows a visible, technology-based response to illegal mining, which supports public and donor confidence.
- Builds local expertise in the directorate through training and handover.
- Opens the way to regional cooperation, since mining equipment and illegal mining often cross borders.

## Caveats to be honest about

- The benefits depend on enforcement. Ghana still has thousands of excavators unclaimed at the port, which shows owners may avoid the regime and that tracking alone doesn't solve the problem.
- Results build over time. Ghana moved from tens of machines to thousands over a few years.
- The strongest case is a combined one: legal backing, agency cooperation, data quality and funding together, rather than the technology on its own.

For the meeting, I'd frame the benefits as targets to be measured in the pilot, for example the number of machines registered, the share of alerts acted on, and the area of illegal activity detected, rather than as guaranteed outcomes.

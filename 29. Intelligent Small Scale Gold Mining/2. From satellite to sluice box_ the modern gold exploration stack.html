How the stack fits together

Exploration is a funnel. Cheap, wide tools screen thousands of square kilometres; each later tool costs more per hectare and looks at a smaller area. The goal at every stage is the same: drop the ground that can’t pay before spending money on it.

Phase A: Regional targeting

Remote methods that cover large areas fast and cheaply. Nothing here proves gold; it decides where the boots go first.

1

Satellite mineral mapping

Spots hydrothermal alteration zones linked to gold, often with free data.

Hot fluids that carry gold also change the rocks they pass through, producing clays, sericite, iron oxides and carbonates. Each of those minerals reflects sunlight in a distinctive way across visible, near-infrared and shortwave-infrared bands. Multispectral satellites record those bands, and simple band ratios or principal-component analysis light up altered ground across an entire district.

Sentinel-2 (13 bands, 10–60 m pixels, revisits every few days) and Landsat-9 (30 m, with thermal bands) are free to download. The older ASTER archive adds finer shortwave-infrared detail for clay mapping.

Hardware
None on the ground; Sentinel-2 MSI and Landsat-9 OLI-2 sensors in orbit. A workstation for processing.
Software
Google Earth Engine, ESA SNAP, QGIS or ArcGIS Pro; band ratios, Crósta PCA.
Output
Iron-oxide and clay-alteration maps, lineament (fault) maps, a shortlist of anomalous areas.
Cost
Data free; cost is analyst time.
Placer fit
Finds source-rock alteration upstream, not the gravels.
Lode fit
A standard first screen.
Watch out
Boreal forest, moss, snow and cloud hide the rock in the Yukon. Pixels are tens of metres wide, so this only points at districts, not dig sites.

2

Drone LiDAR and photogrammetry

Maps terrain, gravel channels and old workings in centimetre detail.

A LiDAR scanner fires hundreds of thousands of laser pulses a second. Some reach the ground through gaps in the canopy; software keeps those “last returns” and strips out trees to make a bare-earth model. Photogrammetry stitches overlapping photos into a 3D surface and a sharp orthophoto, but only sees what the camera sees, so it works best on open ground.

On a placer claim this is the cheapest way to see old river terraces, abandoned benches, dredge tailings, ditches and shafts from the 1898 era, and subtle valley shapes that hint at buried channels. It also gives exact volumes for stripping and stockpiles.

Hardware
Survey drone (e.g. DJI Matrice 350 RTK) with LiDAR (Zenmuse L2) or full-frame camera (Zenmuse P1); RTK/PPK GNSS base station.
Software
DJI Terra, Pix4D, Agisoft Metashape, CloudCompare, QGIS.
Output
Point cloud, bare-earth DEM, hillshade, contours, orthomosaic, volume reports.
Cost
Owned drone kit or a contractor per project.
Placer fit
Terraces, benches, old workings, drainage history.
Lode fit
Outcrop, trenches, structural lineaments.
Watch out
Sees the surface only. Dense spruce thins the ground returns; flight permits and cold-weather batteries matter.
3D model, drag to rotateThe point cloud is the bare-earth return, coloured by elevation. Ridges in the foreground are old dredge tailings; trees are drawn faintly so the ground shows through.
3

Drone aeromagnetics

Traces quartz veins, faults and magnetic host rocks before digging.

A magnetometer flown on tight lines measures tiny variations in the Earth’s magnetic field caused by magnetite in the rocks below. Magnetic dykes and host rocks show as highs. Faults show as sharp breaks or offsets. Quartz veins themselves are non-magnetic, but the fluids that formed them often destroy magnetite, leaving thin magnetic lows along the structures that host gold.

Drones fly lower and on closer line spacing than aircraft, so they resolve narrow structures on a single property. The sensor hangs on a cable below the drone to stay clear of its motors.

Hardware
Heavy-lift drone; towed or slung magnetometer such as Geometrics MagArrow (caesium), GEM Systems GSMP-35U (potassium) or SENSYS MagDrone (fluxgate); base-station magnetometer for daily drift.
Software
Seequent Oasis montaj; filters such as reduction to pole and first vertical derivative.
Output
Total magnetic intensity and derivative maps, structural interpretation.
Cost
Per line-kilometre, usually by contractor.
Placer fit
Maps source structures; magnetite-rich black sands can sometimes trace channels.
Lode fit
Core structural tool.
Watch out
Steel, power lines and old mining scrap create false anomalies. Interpretation needs geophysical skill.
3D model, drag to rotateThe floating coloured sheet is the magnetic map. The dark slab below is a magnetic dyke (high); the pale slab is a quartz vein along a fault (low). The drone flies the yellow lines.
4

AI prospectivity mapping

Combines satellite, geophysics and geology data to rank targets.

Every layer so far, plus geological maps, historical claims and known occurrences, is stacked on the same grid. A machine-learning model (random forests, gradient boosting, neural networks or the older weights-of-evidence method) learns which combinations of features sit near known gold, then scores every cell in the district.

The result is a ranked heat map rather than a single answer. It is rerun as new soil, geophysics and drill data arrive, so it keeps sharpening through Phase B.

Hardware
Workstation or cloud computing.
Software
Python (scikit-learn, XGBoost), ArcGIS Pro or QGIS; Google Earth Engine for imagery.
Output
Prospectivity probability map, ranked target list, uncertainty map.
Cost
Mostly skilled data-science time.
Placer fit
Useful with good drainage and historical production data.
Lode fit
Strongest where many deposits are already known.
Watch out
Needs known examples to learn from and can repeat the biases of past exploration. A high score is a reason to look, not a discovery.

Phase B: Ground truthing

Crews on the claim. Samples and surface geophysics confirm or kill the targets from Phase A and map what lies beneath the surface.

5

Soil and stream-sediment geochemistry on a grid

Builds a grade map at very low cost per sample.

Stream-sediment sampling comes first: fine silt collected where creeks join tells you which drainages carry gold, because each sample represents the whole catchment upstream. Bulk-leach (BLEG) samples help with the patchy “nugget effect”. Inside the best catchments, soil samples are taken on a regular grid, typically tens of metres apart, and sent for fire assay and multi-element analysis.

This is the step the video describes: trace gold migrates as material weathers, so a consistent pattern above a zone suggests something richer below. Contoured results become the heat map the radar and drills are pointed at.

Hardware
Hand augers, shovels, sieves, GPS, sample bags; heated drying space in camp.
Software
Field data apps, QGIS/ArcGIS, ioGAS or Python for statistics and levelling.
Output
Gold and pathfinder contour maps; anomalous catchments and soil trends.
Cost
Low per sample; adds up on dense grids.
Placer fit
Stream sediments point to gold-bearing creeks.
Lode fit
Primary vector to buried veins.
Watch out
Permafrost, windblown loess and old mine waste can mask or fake the signal. Coarse gold makes single samples erratic, so patterns matter more than one high result.
3D model, drag to rotateCreeks run along the valley floors. Each post is a soil sample: taller and warmer means more gold. Cones mark stream-sediment samples.
6

Portable XRF analyser

Gives on-site element readings (arsenic, copper and other pathfinders) to guide sampling.

A handheld X-ray fluorescence gun excites atoms in a sample and reads the characteristic X-rays they give back. In about a minute it reports dozens of elements. Gold itself is usually below its detection limit at soil grades, so geologists read pathfinders that travel with gold: arsenic, antimony, copper, bismuth, tungsten, lead and zinc.

The value is speed. Instead of waiting weeks for lab results, crews can tighten the grid, extend a line or chase a trend the same afternoon, then send only the best samples to the lab.

Hardware
Handheld XRF such as Evident Vanta, Thermo Scientific Niton XL5 Plus, Bruker S1 TITAN or SciAps X-series; test stand, certified standards.
Software
Instrument software, export to QGIS or ioGAS.
Output
Same-day pathfinder maps and a short list of samples worth lab assay.
Cost
One-time instrument purchase or rental.
Placer fit
Helps trace source rocks; little use in gravels.
Lode fit
Fast vectoring in soils, trenches and core.
Watch out
Moisture and grain size skew readings; results need lab checks. The device emits radiation, so it requires licensing and trained operators.

7

Ground magnetometer and IP/resistivity surveys

Locates sulphide-rich veins and buried structures from the surface.

A walking magnetometer repeats the drone survey at higher detail over the best targets. Induced polarisation (IP) and resistivity go further: current is pushed into the ground through a line of steel electrodes and the voltage is read at other electrodes. Resistivity shows how easily current flows; chargeability shows how much the ground stores charge, which disseminated sulphides (pyrite, arsenopyrite) do strongly.

Inverted into 2D sections or 3D volumes, the data show sulphide zones, silicified (resistive) quartz bodies and clay-altered (conductive) faults. For placer ground, resistivity is a strong partner to GPR: frozen gravel is highly resistive, thawed or clay-rich ground is conductive, and bedrock depth often shows clearly even where radar fades.

Hardware
GEM Systems GSM-19 Overhauser magnetometer; IRIS Instruments Syscal Pro or ABEM Terrameter LS 2 for IP/resistivity; electrodes, cable reels, generator.
Software
Res2DInv/Res3DInv, UBC-GIF DCIP inversion codes, Oasis montaj.
Output
Resistivity and chargeability sections, 3D inversions, drill targets.
Cost
Crew-days per line kilometre.
Placer fit
Resistivity maps bedrock depth and frozen versus thawed ground.
Lode fit
Workhorse for sulphide-hosted gold.
Watch out
Frozen ground makes electrode contact hard. Graphite and clay can mimic sulphides; drilling decides.
3D model, drag to rotateThree parallel survey lines, each inverted into a section of coloured cells. Hot colours trace the same dipping sulphide body from line to line.
8

Ground-penetrating radar

Finds bedrock depth and paystreak traps under alluvial gravels.

The tool at the centre of the video. Pulses of electromagnetic energy go down; where gravel meets bedrock, part of the pulse returns. Thousands of traces become a cross-section, and parallel lines 10–15 ft apart (tighter in complex ground) become a 3D model of the buried valley.

Gold’s specific gravity of 19.3 is about seven times that of quartz gravel, so it drops wherever ancient water slowed: bedrock lows, cracks and inside bends. Radar finds those traps, not the gold. In dry frozen overburden it reaches 15–30 ft; in wet or clay ground it can fail within a few feet.

Hardware
Sensors & Software pulseEKKO or GSSI SIR 4000 with low-frequency antennas; ATV or sled mount, odometer wheel, GNSS.
Software
EKKO_Project, RADAN; export to Leapfrog or GIS.
Output
Radargrams, bedrock-depth surface, 3D channel model.
Cost
Equipment or contractor plus skilled interpretation.
Placer fit
Directly maps paleochannels and bedrock traps.
Lode fit
Shallow structure only.
Watch out
Cannot see gold. Seasonal thaw and wet clay kill depth.

Phase C: Proving the deposit

Physical samples, a resource model and recovery tests. This is where most of the exploration budget goes, and where a project earns or loses the right to buy a plant.

9

Auger, RC or small diamond drilling

Takes samples at depth to measure tonnes and grade before investing.

Each rig answers a different question. Auger drills are cheap and fast in loose overburden and shallow gravels. Reverse circulation (RC) blows rock chips up an inner tube, giving clean samples quickly through hard rock. Diamond core drilling cuts an intact cylinder of rock, slower and costlier, but the only way to see vein orientation and structure. For frozen placer gravels, sonic rigs (used in the video) vibrate a core barrel through cobbles and permafrost.

Placer holes are processed whole and graded in grams per cubic metre or ounces per cubic yard. Lode samples are fire-assayed and graded in grams per tonne. The video’s example: radar cut a 30-hole scatter to 12 aimed holes.

Hardware
Power auger, RC rig with compressor, portable diamond rig, sonic rig; sample splitters, core trays, downhole survey tool.
Software
Drill-hole databases such as Seequent MX Deposit; logging apps; QA/QC tracking.
Output
Assays by interval, geological logs, bedrock depth, core photos.
Cost
Auger lowest, diamond highest; usually the largest exploration line item.
Placer fit
Grade and depth of pay gravel.
Lode fit
Grade, thickness and continuity of veins.
Watch out
Coarse gold makes small holes swing wildly in grade; bulk samples or tighter spacing fix that.
3D model, drag to rotateShort white posts are auger and sonic holes into the channel gravels. Angled grey holes are RC and diamond, cutting the pale vein; gold collars show where each intersects ore.
10

GIS and 3D resource modelling software

Turns samples into a block model and mine plan.

Drill results, geophysics surfaces, LiDAR topography and geology are loaded into one 3D workspace. Geologists build wireframes of the channel or vein, then fill them with a grid of blocks. Each block gets a grade estimated from nearby samples, using inverse-distance weighting or kriging, which weights samples by how well grade correlates with distance.

Sum the blocks above a cut-off grade and you get tonnes (or cubic yards) and contained ounces. Add costs and gold price, and pit optimisation draws the shell that is worth mining and the order to mine it. In Canada, public resource statements follow NI 43-101 and are classed inferred, indicated or measured.

Hardware
Workstation with a capable graphics card.
Software
Leapfrog Geo, Datamine Studio RM, Micromine Origin, Maptek Vulcan, GEOVIA Surpac; Deswik for scheduling; QGIS/ArcGIS for maps.
Output
Block model, grade-tonnage curve, resource estimate, pit shell, season-by-season mine plan.
Cost
Licences plus a resource geologist or consultant.
Placer fit
Volumes, stripping ratios and cut sequencing.
Lode fit
Required for any formal resource.
Watch out
A model is only as good as the drill spacing behind it; sparse data gives smooth, confident-looking colours that may be wrong.
3D model, drag to rotateThe block model is cut in half to show the inside. Blocks follow the channel; red blocks sit on the deepest bedrock where gold concentrated. White sticks are the drill holes behind the estimate.
11

Bulk metallurgical testing

Shows how much gold gravity and leaching will recover before buying a plant.

Finding gold is not the same as recovering it. A GRG (gravity-recoverable gold) test runs a bulk sample through a lab centrifugal concentrator in stages, grinding finer each time, to measure how much gold a gravity circuit can catch and at what particle size. A bottle-roll leach test tumbles ground ore in a cyanide solution for one to three days, sampling the liquid over time to see how much gold dissolves and how fast.

Placer operations recover by gravity, so for them the bulk sample through a test sluice or concentrator, plus a size analysis of the gold, is what matters: fine and flaky gold is easily lost. Bottle-roll tests are for hard-rock ore heading to a leach plant.

Hardware
Lab Knelson or Falcon centrifugal concentrator, crushers and mills, bottle-roll rack, fire assay and atomic absorption; test sluice for placer bulk samples.
Software
Lab reporting; recovery models feed the block model and plant design.
Output
Recovery percentage by method and grind size, reagent use, plant flowsheet.
Cost
Bulk sampling plus commercial lab programme.
Placer fit
Gravity and size tests matter; leach tests don’t apply.
Lode fit
Decides plant type and project value.
Watch out
Small samples can miss coarse gold. Tests must use material that represents the whole deposit.

    What the stack can’t do

    No tool sees gold directly

    Satellites see alteration, magnetics see magnetite, IP sees sulphides, radar sees bedrock. Only drilling and bulk samples measure gold.

    The North fights back

    Forest, moss and snow hide rock from satellites; permafrost complicates soil sampling and electrode contact; thaw and clay kill radar depth.

    Scale decides value

    For a one-person claim most of this doesn’t pencil out. The economics work where a correctly targeted season is worth millions.

    Models can look too sure

    AI maps and block models produce clean colours from sparse data. Their confidence is only as good as the samples underneath.

## DEBATE 1: "Is Mobile Money Making Ghanaians Richer — Or Just More Trackable?"
**Theme:** Financial Inclusion vs. Digital Surveillance

### The Big Question
Ghana's mobile money ecosystem is one of the most advanced in Africa. But every transaction you make is logged, timestressed, and stored. Who owns that data — and what are they doing with it?

### What the Facts Say
- Ghana's mobile money transactions exceeded **GH₵ 1.5 trillion** in 2024, with over 20 million registered accounts
- The Bank of Ghana's regulatory sandbox allows fintechs to pilot products — but data privacy laws lag behind innovation
- The Data Protection Act 2012 exists, but enforcement remains weak
- In Kenya, Safaricom's M-Pesa data has been used by the government to identify tax defaulters — a model Ghana is studying

### Side A — Mobile Money Is Empowering the Unbanked
- Millions of Ghanaians who never had a bank account can now save, borrow, and insure through their phones
- Small traders in Kumasi, Tamale, and Takoradi access working capital through MoMo-linked microloans
- Digital records give entrepreneurs a financial history they can use to access formal credit

### Side B — You Are the Product, Not the Customer
- Telecom companies and fintechs build detailed behavioral profiles from your transaction data
- This data can be sold to third-party advertisers, credit scoring firms, or accessed by the state
- Poor Ghanaians who "opt in" to digital finance often don't understand what they're consenting to
- A data breach could expose the financial lives of millions with zero compensation

### Inform Your Listeners
> 💡 **Tip:** You can request your data from any registered data controller in Ghana under the Data Protection Act. Most people don't know this right exists.

### The Debate Question
> *"Would you give up your financial privacy for financial convenience? And who should be responsible when your MoMo data is misused — the telco, the government, or you?"*

## DEBATE 2: "Artificial Intelligence in Ghanaian Schools — Brilliant Tool or Academic Cheat Code?"
**Theme:** AI, Education & the Crisis of Critical Thinking

### The Big Question
Students across Ghana are using ChatGPT to write essays, solve exam questions, and complete assignments. Universities are panicking. But is AI cheating — or is it just the next calculator?

### What the Facts Say
- A 2024 UNESCO report found that **over 60% of African university students** have used generative AI for coursework
- The University of Ghana, KNUST, and Ashesi have each updated their academic integrity policies in response to AI use
- The World Economic Forum lists **critical thinking and AI literacy** as the top two skills employers will demand by 2030
- Countries like Finland and Singapore are integrating AI tools *into* their curricula rather than banning them

### Side A — AI Is a Leveller, Not a Cheater
- A student in Wa or Bolgatanga with no textbooks can now access world-class knowledge through a free AI tool
- AI doesn't replace thinking — it amplifies it, when used right
- Banning AI in education is like banning calculators — it protects a skill (arithmetic) the world no longer needs at that level
- The goal of education should be solving real problems, not memorising content AI can retrieve in seconds

### Side B — We Are Producing Graduates Who Cannot Think
- If students never struggle with problems, they never build the mental muscle to solve new ones
- Ghana's graduate unemployment crisis is partly a skills crisis — degrees without depth
- AI gives confident-sounding wrong answers (called "hallucinations") — a student who can't verify the output is more dangerous than one who didn't use AI
- Examiners cannot currently detect AI use reliably, making assessment meaningless

### Inform Your Listeners
> 💡 **Tip:** The difference between using AI as a crutch and using it as a tool is **verification**. Always cross-check AI-generated content with a credible source before submitting or sharing it.

### The Debate Question
> *"Should Ghanaian universities ban AI tools in exams — or should they redesign education entirely to work with AI? And who should teach students how to use these tools responsibly?"*

## DEBATE 3: "5G Is Coming to Ghana — Revolutionary Upgrade or Health Risk Nobody Is Talking About?"
**Theme:** 5G Connectivity, Digital Infrastructure & Public Trust

### The Big Question
5G promises to transform healthcare, agriculture, transport and education in Ghana. But misinformation about its health risks spread faster than the network itself. How do we separate science from fear?

### What the Facts Say
- MTN Ghana and Telecel are in active trials for 5G deployment as of 2025, with Accra and Tema as priority zones
- 5G operates on radio frequencies — non-ionising radiation, the same category as Wi-Fi and FM radio (which Pluzz FM uses)
- The WHO, after reviewing hundreds of studies, found **no confirmed health risk** from 5G at regulated exposure levels
- However, the rollout requires thousands of new base stations — raising legitimate questions about urban planning, aesthetics, and environmental impact
- Rural Ghana risks being left behind again, deepening the digital divide

### Side A — 5G Will Transform Ghana's Economy
- Smart agriculture: sensors on farms in the Brong-Ahafo region can relay real-time soil, weather and crop data to farmers' phones
- Smart healthcare: remote surgeries and real-time patient monitoring become possible in rural hospitals
- Speed: 5G is up to 100x faster than 4G — enabling AI applications, cloud computing, and autonomous systems at scale
- Ghana could attract major tech investment if it builds world-class digital infrastructure now

### Side B — Ghana Must Ask Hard Questions Before It Rolls Out
- Who owns the infrastructure? Foreign-owned towers mean profits and data leave Ghana
- Will pricing make 5G accessible to ordinary Ghanaians, or only the wealthy in cities?
- The digital divide between Accra and the North will widen if 5G investment is concentrated in commercial zones only
- Regulatory capacity: does the National Communications Authority have the tools to enforce standards and protect consumers?

### Inform Your Listeners
> 💡 **Tip:** The health misinformation around 5G (including the false COVID-19 link) was rated one of the most damaging tech myths of the decade by Reuters Institute. Always verify technology claims on **WHO.int** or **NCA Ghana's** official website.

### The Debate Question
> *"Should Ghana fast-track 5G rollout to stay competitive with Rwanda, South Africa and Nigeria — or slow down and ensure every Ghanaian benefits equally before upgrading?"*

## DEBATE 4: "Big Tech Is Colonising Africa — Are We Building Dependency or Development?"
**Theme:** Digital Sovereignty & Africa's Tech Future

### The Big Question
Google, Meta, Microsoft and Amazon are investing billions in Africa. But African data flows through foreign servers, African users generate profit that leaves the continent, and African governments have little leverage over these platforms. Is this digital opportunity — or digital colonialism?

### What the Facts Say
- Google's **Equiano subsea cable** connects West Africa including Ghana to Europe and South Africa, dramatically improving connectivity
- Meta has invested in internet access programs across Africa through its **Express Wi-Fi** initiative
- Microsoft's **African Development Centre** in Nairobi and Lagos employs hundreds of African engineers
- However, Africa generates roughly **$10 billion** annually in digital ad revenue — almost none of which is taxed on the continent
- Most African countries, including Ghana, lack the data localisation laws that would require companies to store African data on African soil

### Side A — Foreign Tech Investment Is Building Africa's Future
- Without Google and Meta's infrastructure investment, millions of Ghanaians would still lack internet access
- Big Tech brings jobs, skills transfer, and platforms that African startups can build on
- The mobile apps Ghanaians use daily — WhatsApp, YouTube, Google Maps — are services Africans did not have to build from scratch
- African developers can now access global markets through platforms that took decades to build

### Side B — We Are Renting Our Own Economy Back From Foreigners
- African user data enriches foreign shareholders — not African communities
- Local tech startups compete on an uneven playing field against companies with trillion-dollar market caps
- When Meta or Google changes an algorithm, Ghanaian businesses that depend on those platforms lose income overnight with no recourse
- The African Union's **Data Policy Framework** warns that without digital sovereignty, Africa's fourth industrial revolution will be built on foreign foundations

### Inform Your Listeners
> 💡 **Tip:** Ghana's **National Information Technology Agency (NITA)** and the **Ghana Investment Promotion Centre** actively support local tech companies. If you're building a tech business, check their incentive programmes before looking abroad for support.

### The Debate Question
> *"Should Ghana impose digital taxes on Big Tech platforms earning revenue from Ghanaian users? And is it even possible for Africa to build its own tech ecosystem — or is that ship already sailed?"*

## DEBATE 5: "Should Ghana Regulate Social Media — Or Is That Just Censorship With a Tech Badge?"
**Theme:** Free Speech, Disinformation & Government in the Digital Age

### The Big Question
Misinformation on WhatsApp influenced elections. Hate speech on Facebook fuelled ethnic tensions. TikTok is shaping what young Ghanaians believe about their own country. At what point does regulating social media become necessary — and at what point does it become a tool of political control?

### What the Facts Say
- Ghana ranked **9th globally** for social media penetration in Sub-Saharan Africa as of 2024
- During Ghana's 2024 elections, the Electoral Commission flagged multiple disinformation campaigns spreading on WhatsApp and Facebook
- Nigeria's 2023 Twitter/X ban lasted 222 days — costing the economy an estimated **$366 million**
- The EU's **Digital Services Act** now holds platforms legally responsible for harmful content — a model several African nations are studying
- Ghana's **Electronic Communications Act** gives regulators broad powers that could apply to social media, but clear social media-specific legislation does not yet exist

### Side A — Ghana Needs a Legal Framework for Social Media
- Unchecked disinformation destroys public trust in institutions, health systems, and democracy
- Platforms profit from outrage and division — they have no financial incentive to clean up harmful content voluntarily
- Children are exposed to violent, sexual, and psychologically harmful content with no parental consent mechanism
- Every major democracy — EU, UK, Australia — is moving toward platform regulation. Ghana should not be the last

### Side B — Regulation Is a Slippery Slope to Censorship
- Who defines "harmful" content? Governments that fear criticism will use regulation to silence journalists and opposition voices
- Internet shutdowns — even partial ones — harm businesses, civil society, and ordinary citizens more than bad actors
- Ghana's Constitution guarantees freedom of expression — social media is its modern form
- Regulation without strong, independent oversight becomes a political weapon

### Inform Your Listeners
> 💡 **Tip:** Ghana's **Commission on Human Rights and Administrative Justice (CHRAJ)** accepts complaints about digital rights violations. If you believe your online rights have been infringed by a state actor, you have legal recourse.

### The Debate Question
> *"Should Ghana pass a Social Media Regulation Act — and if so, who should write it: politicians, tech experts, civil society, or all three? And who watches the watchmen?"*



## Engineering tactics you can name


**Pull-to-refresh mimics the slot lever** (not an accident).


**Pull notification send-time optimization** — platforms like OneSignal/Braze use ML to learn *when each individual user is weakest* and deliver at that moment.


**Permission prompts are deliberately timed** — UX best practice is to ask for **notification access right after a "moment of delight,"** not at install. Opt-in rates measurably jump.


**Red badges, truncated previews ("Someone mentioned y..."), typing indicators** — all curiosity-gap and urgency engineering.


**Streaks (Snapchat, Duolingo)** = **loss aversion**, Straight from Kahneman/Tversky.


**What is phantom vibration syndrome?**
Feeling your phone buzz when it didn't. In Drouin's 2012 study, ~89% of undergrads reported it — the brain gets conditioned to the signal itself.

* **High stress:** Elevated anxiety makes your senses hyper-alert.
* **Digital burnout:** It often means you are spending too much time on your phone or feeling overwhelmed by constant notifications.


**(is turning off enough?) — the honest answer is "partially":** Nir Eyal's Hook Model explains why. Notifications start as  *external triggers* , but after enough loops, the trigger goes *internal* — boredom, anxiety, waiting in line. You reach for the phone with no notification at all. Removing external triggers breaks the loop's ignition, but the internal trigger habit needs replacement behavior. Evidence-backed fixes: batching notifications into 2–3 daily digests (reduced stress without lost connectedness), human-senders-only notifications (Cal Newport's rule), and limiting total social media time to ~30 min/day reduced loneliness/depression in UPenn's 2018 trial.


**The 2014 Facebook emotional contagion study (they manipulated feeds, measurably shifted users' moods)** is your best proof that this is tested, not theorized.


**Why do red notification badges work so well?**
They exploit the Zeigarnik effect — open, unresolved tasks occupy the mind. A badge is a loop your brain badly wants to close.


**Why do truncated previews like "Someone mentioned y..." work?**
The curiosity gap: unresolved information creates tension that only opening the app relieves. It's engineered incompleteness.


**How many times a day do people touch their phones?**
2,617 average touches per the dscout logged-data study; the top 10% of heavy users averaged 5,417.


**How many people check their phone within 15 minutes of waking?**
Around 80%, per IDC research.


**What did Facebook's first president admit?**
Sean Parker (2017): the goal was "how do we consume as much of your time and conscious attention as possible?" by "exploiting a vulnerability in human psychology."


**Who invented infinite scroll, and what does he say now?**
Aza Raskin. He says he accidentally removed the "stopping cue" and has compared modern interfaces to "behavioral cocaine."


**What's the dark side of the Like button?**
It quantifies social approval and delivers it on a variable schedule — likes arrive unpredictably, like payouts.


**Why is typing indicator ("..." bubble) so powerful?**
It weaponizes anticipation — a visible, unresolved reward that holds your attention hostage until it resolves.


**Does your phone drain you even when it's off?**
Ward et al. (2017): working memory performance improved when phones were in *another room* — off and face-down on the desk wasn't enough. Mere presence taxes you.


**What role does FOMO play?**
Uncertainty about what you're missing inflates its imagined importance. The unknown message feels more valuable than it almost ever is.

## Solutions

- **only allow notifications from *humans you'd welcome interrupting you* in person.**

	Settings > Notifications

	Settings > Messages > Filter Unknown Senders

	Settings > Phone > Silence Unknown Callers


- **batching notifications into digests reduced stress and phone-checking without reducing feelings of connectedness.**

	Settings > Notifications > Scheduled Summary — turn ON.


- **limiting social media time help**

	Settings > Screen Time > App Limits > Add Limit.


- **grayscale mode help**

	Settings > Accessibility > Display & Text Size > Color Filters > ON > select Grayscale.

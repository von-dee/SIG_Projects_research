Recommendations to Close Gaps
Gap: White-label PDF report export (Step 9)
How MINEASK can do it: Build a report templating engine that maps dossier data fields (title verification, ESG screening, logistics, community brief) into client-branded PDF templates. The dossier renderer already produces structured output; the missing piece is a template layer that applies client logos, colour schemes, cover pages, and executive summary formatting. This can leverage existing PDF generation libraries and allow consultants to save reusable templates per client. Priority: Phase 3, high impact for the primary persona.
Gap: Data-correction flywheel (Step 10)
How MINEASK can do it: Add an in-app correction interface where consultants can flag a data point as incorrect (e.g., wrong stool name, outdated boundary coordinates), provide the corrected value, and submit it for review. Accepted corrections update the platform's database, improving accuracy for all future users. This creates a virtuous cycle: field intelligence flows back into the system, making every subsequent dossier more reliable. It directly addresses the consultant's pain point that "field data corrections never feed back upstream." Priority: Phase 2, foundational for long-term data quality.

Recommendations to Close Gaps
Gap: Framework-structured ESG reporting (Step 7)
How MINEASK can do it: Build a reporting engine that maps MINEASK data fields to specific GRI, SASB, Equator Principles, and IFC Performance Standards disclosure requirements. Each framework has a defined set of indicators; the platform already collects the underlying data (protected areas, water stress, deforestation, community risk). The missing piece is a mapping layer that tags each data point with the relevant framework indicators and auto-populates disclosure tables. This directly addresses the pain point that "manual framework-to-data mapping is slow." Priority: Phase 3, high demand from institutional investors.
Gap: Continuous environmental monitoring (Step 9)
How MINEASK can do it: Activate Ground Watch to run scheduled spatial queries on a daily, weekly, or monthly cadence. The platform already has the data connectors (satellite imagery, GFW, Aqueduct) and the analysis logic (NDVI anomaly detection, water stress scoring). Ground Watch would simply re-run these on a schedule and flag changes via dashboard alerts or email digests. This transforms MINEASK from a one-time screening tool into a persistent monitoring platform for the ESG Manager, who currently lacks "a unified ESG screening tool" for ongoing compliance. Priority: Phase 3, highest-impact single feature across all personas.

Recommendations to Close Gaps
Gap: Qualitative economic breaker assessment (Step 5)
How MINEASK can do it: Deepen the infrastructure and ESG analysis by adding structured scoring for each economic breaker category: logistics (road condition index, port queue times, nearest rail head), water availability (Aqueduct baseline + seasonal variation), and power (grid connection status, off-grid solar viability). Currently the dossier "surfaces" these risks but does not score or compare them systematically. A structured risk score per category would let analysts quickly determine which breakers are material for a given asset. Priority: Phase 2, moderate effort.
Gap: Go/No-Go confidence scoring (Step 7)
How MINEASK can do it: Build a weighted decision model that aggregates all resolved risk signals (title status, legal showstoppers, ESG flags, infrastructure scores, fiscal compliance) into a composite investment confidence score. The model should be transparent: analysts should see which factors drove the score up or down. This supplements rather than replaces their financial model, directly addressing the need for "structured thinking, not gut instinct" in investment committee presentations. Priority: Phase 2, high analytical value.
Gap: Post-investment asset monitoring (Step 8)
How MINEASK can do it: Enable Ground Watch for portfolio monitoring: the analyst bookmarks invested assets and sets a monitoring cadence. The system then tracks regulatory changes, incident activity, environmental indicators, and license status changes on a schedule, alerting the analyst when any signal crosses a defined threshold. This directly addresses the pain point that "post-investment monitoring is manual" and protects invested capital. Priority: Phase 3, depends on Ground Watch.

Recommendations to Close Gaps
Gap: Encumbrance and charge verification (Step 7)
How MINEASK can do it: This is the one gap that cannot be fully solved by technology alone, because encumbrances are recorded at the Lands Registry and are not available in digital public datasets. However, MINEASK can address this in two ways: (1) add a structured checklist that prompts the legal manager with exactly what to request at the Lands Registry, including the specific form numbers and supporting documents needed, turning an open-ended task into a guided process; and (2) explore partnerships or data integrations with Lands Commission digitisation efforts to eventually incorporate this data directly. The honest flagging approach ("must verify at Lands Registry") is already the right design philosophy. Priority: Phase 2 for the guided checklist, longer-term for data integration.
Gap: Comprehensive legal audit trail (Step 8)
How MINEASK can do it: Expand the existing per-fact lineage system into a full legal evidence pack exporter. For every conclusion in a legal opinion, the system should be able to produce a supporting document pack that includes: the source data record, the timestamp of retrieval, the specific regulatory provision or gazette notice it maps to, and the confidence score. This pack should be exportable as a PDF appendix that can be appended to legal opinions or filed with regulators. This directly addresses the pain point that "manual audit trail is time-consuming" and makes every opinion defensible in court or before regulators. Priority: Phase 2, high value for the legal persona's core requirement of auditability.

Recommendations to Close Gaps
Gap: Real-time operations and dispatch (Step 8) -- Outside product scope by design
How MINEASK should handle this: This gap is intentionally outside MINEASK's scope. Real-time dispatch and control-room functions belong to specialized mining operations platforms (e.g., Modular Mining, Caterpillar Fleet Management). MINEASK's role is to provide the intelligence layer that feeds into those systems: site security status, encroachment alerts, terrain stability warnings, and haul route risk scores. The recommended approach is to build API integrations or data export pipelines that feed MINEASK's intelligence into existing dispatch and fleet management systems, rather than building a competing control room. This addresses the ops manager's need for a "unified site intelligence dashboard" without duplicating tools that already exist. Priority: Phase 3 integrations, not standalone features.
Gap: Continuous automated monitoring (Step 9)
How MINEASK can do it: Ground Watch is the solution here. For the operations manager specifically, Ground Watch should be configured to run site intelligence queries on a daily or shift-change cadence: satellite-based encroachment checks, slope stability change detection, shadow activity monitoring, and haul route incident scanning. Results should be delivered as a "daily site intelligence brief" that the operations manager reviews at the start of each shift. This transforms MINEASK from an on-demand query tool into the proactive early-warning system that the ops manager needs to move from "reactive firefighting" to "proactive management." Priority: Phase 3, core feature.

Recommendations to Close Gaps
Gap: Early-warning indicator system (Step 7)
How MINEASK can do it: The current approach uses proxy signals (GDELT events, NDVI changes) which is a good foundation. To build a true early-warning system, MINEASK should add: (1) temporal trend analysis that detects accelerating incident frequencies rather than one-off events; (2) composite risk scoring that weights multiple proxy signals (social media activity spikes, environmental stress indicators, political event calendars) into a single escalation score; and (3) configurable alert thresholds that let the risk manager define what constitutes "early warning" for each site. The key insight is that early-warning is about trends and velocities, not snapshots. Priority: Phase 2 for trend analysis, Phase 3 for composite scoring.
Gap: Live alerting and 24/7 monitoring (Step 8)
How MINEASK can do it: Ground Watch with push notifications is the primary solution. For the risk manager specifically, the system should support: (1) configurable alert rules (e.g., "alert me if any incident occurs within 10 km of Site A" or "alert me if the corridor risk score for Route B increases by more than 20%"); (2) multi-channel delivery (email, SMS, Slack/Teams webhook) so alerts reach the risk manager regardless of where they are; and (3) an escalation protocol that routes critical alerts to senior leadership if not acknowledged within a defined window. This addresses the pain point that "24/7 monitoring requires dedicated staff" by automating the monitoring layer and only requiring human attention when thresholds are breached. Priority: Phase 3, highest-value feature for this persona.

Cross-Cutting Themes: What the Gaps Reveal

Looking across all six personas, the unresolved gaps cluster into four distinct themes. These are not isolated issues but structural patterns that, if addressed together, would multiply the platform's value.

1. Ground Watch Is the Universal Multiplier

Four of six personas (ESG Manager, Investment Analyst, Ops Manager, Risk Manager) have continuous monitoring as a gap, and the Consultant would benefit from it for post-engagement tracking. Ground Watch is not one feature; it is the feature that transforms MINEASK from a point-in-time tool into a persistent intelligence platform. Every monitoring gap resolves when Ground Watch ships.

Affects: ESG, Analyst, Ops, Risk, Consultant (5 of 6 personas)

2. Export and Reporting Engine

The Consultant needs white-label PDFs, the ESG Manager needs framework-structured reports, the Legal Manager needs auditable evidence packs, and the Analyst needs investment committee-ready briefs. All four needs can be served by a single flexible reporting engine with template support, data-field mapping, and multi-format export (PDF, DOCX, Excel). This is a force multiplier for adoption.

Affects: Consultant, ESG, Legal, Analyst (4 of 6 personas)

3. The Data-Correction Flywheel

Every persona encounters data that is stale, incomplete, or incorrect. Currently there is no mechanism for field-verified corrections to flow back into the platform. Building a structured correction interface would compound in value over time: every correction improves the system for all subsequent users, creating a network effect that makes the platform progressively more reliable and defensible.

Affects: Consultant, Legal, Ops (3 of 6 personas directly; all benefit indirectly)

4. Integration, Not Replacement

Two gaps (Ops Manager's real-time dispatch, Legal Manager's encumbrance checks) are outside MINEASK's scope by design, and that is correct. The platform should not try to be a control room or a land registry. Instead, it should invest in API integrations and data export pipelines that feed its intelligence into the specialised tools that already serve those functions. This is how MINEASK becomes infrastructure rather than a standalone app.

Affects: Ops, Legal (2 of 6 personas; architectural principle for all)

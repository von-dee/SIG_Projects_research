Executive Summary

This document provides a step-by-step evaluation of how the MINEASK platform addresses the daily workflows of six key personas in the mining and minerals value chain. Each persona's real job tasks are mapped against what MINEASK currently does, with a clear verdict per step indicating whether the problem is fixed, partly addressed, or not yet solved.

The six personas covered are: Consultant, ESG and Compliance Manager, Investment Analyst, Legal and Compliance Manager, Mine Operations Manager, and Risk and Security Manager. Together, these roles represent the full spectrum of pre-investment due diligence, ongoing compliance, operational monitoring, and risk management activities that mining sector professionals perform.

Across all six personas, a consistent pattern emerges: query-and-assess workflows are well-solved today, while continuous monitoring capabilities are roadmapped for future phases. The Consultant persona, which the system was originally designed around, achieves the highest coverage at 8 of 10 steps fully fixed. The Legal and Compliance Manager and Mine Operations Manager follow closely, with 7 and 7 of their core steps addressed respectively.

This analysis is intended to serve as documentation-ready reference material for internal product teams, prospective clients evaluating MINEASK, investors assessing product-market fit, and any stakeholder who needs to understand where the platform delivers value today and where the development roadmap is headed.

Methodology and Scope

Each persona analysis follows the same structured methodology. First, the core job of the persona is defined in a single sentence that captures the essence of their role. Then, each discrete step of their actual daily workflow is listed in sequence, alongside what MINEASK does to address that step. Every step receives a verdict: Fixed (the problem is solved today), Partly (partial capability exists), or Not Yet / Not Fixed (the capability is on the roadmap or outside the product's scope by design).

The data sources feeding each verdict include the MINEASK product's current agent architecture (Cadaster, EITI, Sentinel, Voxel, Eco, Logistics, and Valua agents), the rules engine based on Ghana's Minerals and Mining Act 703, satellite imagery and geospatial data integrations, and the planned feature roadmap across Phase 2 and Phase 3 development milestones. The assessment reflects the system's capabilities as of the current release.

It is important to note that MINEASK is designed as an intelligence and assessment platform, not a control-room or real-time dispatch system. Capabilities that require live operational control, such as equipment dispatch or 24/7 alerting, are explicitly marked as outside the current product scope, though the Ground Watch feature planned for Phase 3 will move the platform closer to continuous monitoring.

Key Patterns and Strategic Implications

Several patterns emerge from this analysis that are relevant for product strategy, client positioning, and investor communications. Understanding these patterns helps clarify where MINEASK creates the most value today and where future development investment will yield the highest returns.

Query-and-Assess Is Solved

The most consistent finding across all six personas is that MINEASK fully solves the query-and-assess portion of their workflows. Whether the user is a consultant verifying a concession title, a lawyer checking for overlapping claims, or a risk manager mapping threat corridors, the platform can retrieve, join, and present the relevant data in seconds rather than the hours or days these tasks typically require. This is the core value proposition of the system and the capability around which the entire agent architecture was designed.

Continuous Monitoring Is the Universal Roadmap Item

Every persona that requires ongoing vigilance, whether environmental monitoring for the ESG manager, asset monitoring for the investment analyst, or threat monitoring for the security manager, has continuous monitoring flagged as a gap. The Ground Watch feature, which enables scheduled re-runs of spatial and data queries, is the planned solution for this across all personas. This represents the single highest-impact development investment, as it transforms MINEASK from a point-in-time tool into a persistent intelligence platform.

Export and Reporting Needs Vary by Persona

The Consultant needs white-label PDF reports for clients. The ESG Manager needs framework-structured outputs for GRI or SASB reporting. The Legal Manager needs auditable evidence packs. Each of these export requirements is currently partly addressed or roadmapped, suggesting that a flexible export and reporting engine should be a priority in Phase 3 development. The dossier renderer exists today, but the specific formatting, branding, and structural requirements of each persona's output need dedicated attention.

The System Is Honest About Its Boundaries

A notable design philosophy visible in the workflow analysis is that MINEASK explicitly flags data it cannot verify rather than guessing. Encumbrances that require Lands Registry checks, local stool names that need field confirmation, and operational capabilities that belong in a control room are all marked as checklist items or flagged as outside scope. This transparency is particularly important for the Legal and Compliance persona, where fabricated certainty would be actively harmful. The YAML-based rules engine reinforces this by making regulatory logic inspectable and reviewable by counsel.

Mining Intelligence User Landscape

How mining analysts, technical teams, ESG professionals, financial and commodity specialists, legal and government advisors, and development, academic and media audiences find, verify and analyse mineral-sector information today — and where MineSentinel currently stands against each audience's core needs.

Scope note. Each audience is documented as it operates today: what it needs, what hurts, where it looks for information, which websites and platforms it uses, which tools it works in, and how the workflow moves from information-gathering to an output. A MineSentinel fit table is layered onto every audience, mapping their core needs against what the platform currently provides, with a verdict of Covered, Partial or Not covered. Verdicts are grounded in the platform's own documentation as of August 2026; a Partial or Not-covered mark identifies a roadmap item or work that sits outside an intelligence-aggregation platform by design, not a feature assumed away.

The mining information ecosystem

Every audience in this document sits somewhere on the same underlying path — data is discovered, then verified, then analysed, before a person synthesises it into a report, decision, monitoring signal or action.

Core pain points
01 · Fragmentation

Distributed across many sources

The relevant information is distributed across many sources.

02 · Manual compilation

Finding outweighs analysing

A significant part of the analyst's time can be spent finding and organising information rather than analysing it.

03 · Inconsistent data

No shared reporting standard

Different companies and jurisdictions report information differently.

04 · Historical comparability

The past is harder to reconstruct

Current information may be easy to find while historical information is harder to reconstruct.

05 · Geographic linking

Entities don't connect themselves

Connecting a company, mine, concession, geological feature, infrastructure and country requires additional work.

06 · Verification

Figures need cross-checking

Important figures often need to be checked against multiple sources.

Looking at the floorplan, the SC positions run in two vertical strips flanking the central seating/booth block — SC1–SC9 on the west corridor (next to the Stage/Pavilion 1 side) and SC10–SC17 on the east corridor (next to Pavilion 2/G6 and the VIP Mining Club & Roundtable Room side). Both strips are essentially the main pedestrian corridors connecting the entrance level to Catering/Pavilions up top, but they're not equal in traffic quality.

**My pick: SC13 (with SC12 as a close second)**

Why this spot wins over the others:

- **Sits right on the coffee break choke point.** The COFFEE station splits the right strip into SC17–13 (upper) and SC12–10 (lower), with SC13 directly bordering it. Coffee breaks force people to slow down and linger — that's exactly the dwell time a small booth needs to convert a walk-by into a conversation.
- **Feeds off VIP/Roundtable traffic.** SC13–17 sit closest to the VIP Mining Club and Roundtable Room. If your buyers are decision-makers (which the mining-industry framing suggests), this is the highest-value foot traffic on the floor — people moving to/from closed-door sessions, not just casually browsing.
- **Multiple access points converge here**, unlike the west corridor which is fed mainly by one main entrance. The east side has the B5–B8 entrance/exit, an additional entrance near the VIP club, and an escalator at the top near SC17 — meaning attendees pass this strip repeatedly across the day, not just once on arrival.
- **Central, not a dead end.** It's roughly equidistant from Pavilion 2, the Tech Stage overflow, and G6 — so you catch spillover from sessions letting out in either direction, rather than being stuck at the far top or bottom of the strip where traffic thins out.

**If SC13 isn't available**, SC12 is nearly as strong (same coffee proximity, right at the G6/entrance junction) — and SC4 on the west strip is the equivalent "best of the left side" if you end up placed there instead (coffee-adjacent, near Pavilion 1 and the Tech Stage).

**What to avoid:** SC1/SC10 (bottom-most, closest to the noisy entrance queue — people aren't in browsing mode yet) and SC9/SC17 (top-most, tucked near Catering where people are focused on food, not vendors).

**To actually convert that traffic once you're there:**

1. **Design for the corridor, not the booth.** People will see you side-on while walking, not head-on — put your logo/value prop on a banner tall enough to read from 10+ meters down the strip, not just on the table.
2. **Time your push to the coffee rhythm.** Have your best pitch/demo ready to fire the moment the coffee queue forms next door — that's your guaranteed 3–5 minute captive audience window.
3. **Use a fast lead-capture method** (QR code straight to a form or calendar link) since people mid-walk-to-a-session won't stop for a long form.
4. **Coordinate with the Tech Stage/Pavilion 2 schedule** — know when sessions let out, and have staff actively engaging (not sitting) in that 5-minute surge window.
5. **If targeting the VIP crowd specifically**, consider a light touch — a standing staff member who can informally flag down Roundtable/VIP attendees as they pass, since that group often won't stop at a booth unprompted.
